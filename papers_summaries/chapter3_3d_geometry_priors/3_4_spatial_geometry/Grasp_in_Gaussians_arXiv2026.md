# Grasp in Gaussians: Fast Monocular Reconstruction of Dynamic Hand-Object Interactions

**Authors:** Ayce Idil Aytekin, Xu Chen, Zhengyang Shen, Thabo Beeler, Helge Rhodin, Rishabh Dabral, Christian Theobalt  
**Date:** 2026-04-14  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2604.12929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12929)

## Summary

Grasp in Gaussians (GraG) reconstructs dynamic 3D hand-object interactions from a single monocular video by combining strong pretrained priors with a fast, lightweight tracking scheme rather than optimizing heavy neural scene representations. A video-adapted SAM3D pipeline reconstructs a canonical object from selected keyframes and estimates per-frame pose with temporal guidance in its latent space, while Dyn-HaMR provides the initial hand trajectory; both are then refined with a compact Sum-of-Gaussians (SoG) tracking objective revived from classical tracking literature, plus simple silhouette, depth, 2D-joint, and contact losses. This reduces reconstruction of long sequences to roughly 16 minutes per 100 frames on one RTX 4090 (0.27 hours per 100 frames), 4.4x-38.9x faster than prior video-based HOI methods (HOLD 10.5 h, BIGS 3.6 h, MagicHOI 1.2 h), while achieving state-of-the-art object quality (F10 of 97.2% on HO3Dv3 and 94.7% on egocentric HOT3D) and a 100% success rate where SfM-based baselines drop to 50-56%.

## Background and Problem

Reconstructing hand-object interaction from monocular video (3D geometry and pose of both hand and object) is fundamental for robot learning from human demonstration and AR, but is ill-posed due to heavy mutual occlusion and depth ambiguity, and becomes harder under runtime constraints. Prior approaches make restrictive assumptions: template-based methods need pre-scanned 3D object models; template-free methods are trained on few instances or predefined categories and generalize poorly; in-hand object scanning assumes a rigid hand pose. Recent video-based methods (HOLD with implicit SDF/NeRF-style optimization, BIGS with 3D Gaussians plus triplane MLP and SDS loss, MagicHOI with novel-view synthesis) generalize without templates but are extremely slow (about 10 hours for HOLD and 4 hours for BIGS on a 100-frame video) and depend on SfM-style (COLMAP) initialization that fails on occlusion, textureless objects, and egocentric camera motion. The authors observe that modern image-to-3D generative models can provide object geometry from a single view, and that classical model-based tracking is fast and stable, and pose the problem as combining the two: use a generative 3D prior to obtain a canonical object and hand initialization, then perform lightweight tracking in a compact representation. The setting is manipulation of a single rigid object with the dominant hand.

## Method

Preprocessing extracts hand and object masks with SAM3 (object masks prompted by VLM labels), camera intrinsics/extrinsics and pointmaps with Depth Anything 3, and passes the camera parameters to Dyn-HaMR, whose MANO-based hand trajectory is natively in the same camera-registered coordinate system as the object; a Gemini 3 video VLM (gemini-3-flash-preview) labels per-sequence contact intervals, converted to per-frame contact flags. Stage 1 (keyframe selection and object reconstruction) selects a small set of K informative and diverse keyframes using the saddle-balanced selection strategy of Depth Anything 3 on DINOv2 [CLS] features (a balance cost over mean similarity, feature magnitude, and variance, combined with a diversity penalty, minimized greedily), and feeds them to MV-SAM3D, a multi-view extension of SAM3D, obtaining canonical shape tokens decoded into dense 3D Gaussians. Stage 2 (temporally stable object pose estimation) adapts SAM3D to video: canonical shape tokens are frozen by sharing them across frames and zeroing the shape velocity field, while the layout tokens (rotation, translation, scale) evolve per frame through conditional flow matching; a temporal guidance term subtracts a weighted latent difference between consecutive frames from the predicted pose velocity, discouraging abrupt changes unless strongly supported by the observation, and a quaternion consistency check repairs residual orientation flips. Stage 3 (hand-object tracking with SoG) refines both trajectories in sliding windows of 8 frames (stride 7, 100 AdamW iterations each): the dense Gaussian asset is sparsified by farthest-point sampling to 2000 isotropic Gaussians (object SoG), each frame's masked object image is encoded as a compact image SoG via quad-tree color clustering, and tracking maximizes a closed-form, color-weighted Gaussian overlap energy between projected object SoG and image SoG, with a hand-aware visibility gate excluding object Gaussians that fall inside the hand mask. This main alignment term is combined with lightweight priors: a silhouette loss on the signed-distance-transform of mask boundaries, a depth loss anchoring the median depths of hand and object to the pointmap and coupling object depth to hand depth in contact frames, a 2D hand-joint reprojection loss, a surface-aware contact loss on designated hand contact zones that also penalizes interpenetration, and a temporal smoothness loss. Only poses are refined; canonical object shape and hand shape stay fixed, and one global object scale and one global hand scale per sequence are clamped around their initialization.

## Contributions

- A fast monocular HOI reconstruction system that scales to long, unconstrained videos by leveraging pretrained object (MV-SAM3D) and hand (Dyn-HaMR) priors with efficient tracking instead of heavy per-frame representation optimization, cutting runtime from roughly 3-10 hours to about 16 minutes per 100 frames.
- A video adaptation of SAM3D that freezes canonical shape tokens and tracks only per-frame pose via conditional flow matching with temporal guidance in the pose-latent velocity field, improving stability under occlusion and suppressing jitter and orientation flips.
- Compact occlusion-aware SoG tracking for Gaussian-based object assets: sparsifying dense generative Gaussians to about 2000 isotropic primitives and aligning their projections to a quad-tree-clustered image SoG with closed-form overlap and hand-mask visibility gating, which removes the need for back-to-front alpha sorting and tolerates inaccurate generated textures that break render-and-compare objectives.
- Demonstration that the pretrained priors and the SoG refinement are complementary: ablations show Stage 3 priors recover most of the hand-relative pose error (CDh from 20.62 to 10.45) and the SoG term closes the remaining gap (to 7.65), plus a robotic application where GraG's reconstructed 6D object trajectories serve as goal poses for a SimToolReal dexterous tool-manipulation policy.

## Experimental Setup

Evaluation follows the HOLD protocol on 18 HO3Dv3 sequences (from HOLD's evaluation set) and 18 single-interaction segments selected from the HOT3D training set (egocentric fisheye videos, undistorted before processing). Metrics: root-relative hand MPJPE (mm); object template quality via ICP-aligned Chamfer distance (CD, cm) and F-score at 10 mm (F10); hand-relative object Chamfer distance (CDh, cm) after subtracting the hand root; success rate, where a sequence fails if no valid output is produced (e.g., SfM initialization breakdown) or CDh exceeds 1000 cm; and runtime normalized to hours per 100 frames on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, including preprocessing and optimization. Stage 3 uses AdamW (beta 0.9/0.95, no weight decay) with per-variable learning rates (object translation 2.5e-3, rotation 8e-4, scale 1e-3; hand pose 4e-4, translation 9e-4, orientation 5e-4, scale 2e-4) and loss weights 0.05 (SoG), 0.5 (2D joints), 1000 (depth), 100 (silhouette), 1000 (contact), 100 (smoothness). Baselines are HOLD, BIGS (re-run with the authors' implementation on HO3Dv3), and MagicHOI; these are full-system comparisons in which GraG also benefits from the stronger SAM3D and Dyn-HaMR priors unavailable to the baselines. Supplementary analyses add ARCTIC interaction-consistency metrics (contact deviation CDev, motion deviation MDev, hand/object acceleration errors, and hand-to-object relative root position error MRRPE), a Gaussian-count trade-off study, and contact-flag validation against ground-truth mesh proximity (in-contact when the minimum hand-object vertex distance is at most 3 mm).

## Results

- HO3Dv3: GraG achieves CD 0.51 cm (23.9% below BIGS at 0.67 and 34.6% below HOLD at 0.78), F10 97.2%, MPJPE 9.38 mm (second best; MagicHOI's 4.35 mm is best on its successful clips only), CDh 5.02 cm, 100% success rate, and 0.27 hours per 100 frames versus 1.20 (MagicHOI), 3.60 (BIGS), and 10.5 (HOLD); MagicHOI's success rate drops to 67.0% because its COLMAP-based initialization becomes unstable on full-length sequences.
- HOT3D (egocentric, dynamic cameras): GraG reaches CD 0.67 cm (77.1% below BIGS, 78.7% below HOLD), F10 94.7%, CDh 9.37 cm (88.9% below the strongest baseline), MPJPE 20.6 mm, and 100% success rate, while HOLD and BIGS fall to 50% success and MagicHOI to 55.6% with CD 22.6 cm.
- Interaction consistency: on HOT3D, contact deviation drops from 451-570 mm for HOLD/BIGS to 23.8 mm and hand-to-object relative root position error from 606-762 mm to 155 mm; on HO3Dv3, GraG attains the best motion deviation (5.37 mm) and hand acceleration error (6.17 m/s^2) with competitive contact deviation (17.0 versus HOLD's 15.8).
- Ablations (2 HO3Dv3 + 2 HOT3D sequences; full model CDh 7.65 cm, MPJPE 11.3 mm): replacing Stage 3 entirely with only foundation models degrades CDh to 20.62; keeping the priors but dropping the SoG term gives 10.45, showing the priors recover most error and SoG closes the remaining gap. Removing the contact loss is the most damaging prior ablation (CDh 19.58), followed by removing the depth loss (12.70) and silhouette loss (10.71); removing the visibility gate (9.35) biases alignment toward hand-occluded regions. Replacing SoG with dense Gaussian splatting tracking (10.46, and 4.4x slower at 1.20 h), mesh rendering (12.60, 2.40 h), or optical flow (11.45, tracks lost under occlusion) all underperform; single-view keyframe selection degrades CDh to 22.73 and MPJPE to 18.2, while balanced K-keyframe selection beats random or all-frame selection; freezing shape tokens and temporal guidance reduce CDh from 10.81/10.60 to 7.65 in the full system.
- Gaussian-count trade-off: sparse SoG (~2000 Gaussians) takes 0.27 h with 0.11 MB storage and 2.4 GB peak CUDA memory; the reduced ~60,000-Gaussian asset takes 1.20 h (about 4.4x) and the full ~300,000-Gaussian asset 34.1 h (over two orders of magnitude slower), while CD, F10, MPJPE, and CDh change only marginally.
- Contact signal: the Gemini-based VLM flag attains balanced accuracy of 0.80 on HO3Dv3 and 0.66 on HOT3D versus 0.70 and 0.59 for a tuned mask-proximity fallback, hallucinating contact on only 1.2% and 1.8% of frames; substituting the mask-proximity flag raises CDh from 5.02 to 5.53 cm (HO3Dv3) and from 9.37 to 10.60 cm (HOT3D).
- Robotic application: feeding GraG's reconstructed 6D trajectory of a short screwdriver demonstration to SimToolReal as goal poses enables zero-shot dexterous tool manipulation; the authors note that incorrect object tracking contributes to as many as 44% of SimToolReal failure cases, motivating accurate tracking.

## Limitations

The authors state several limitations. Severe Depth Anything 3 pointmap failures can still bias object scale and translation despite using median depth aggregates. The color-weighted SoG alignment is robust to texture imperfections but vulnerable to illumination changes, shadows, specularities, and weak object texturing. The isotropic approximation of projected 3D Gaussians loses fidelity under large viewpoint changes or partial cropping near the image boundary, where exact perspective projection yields elliptical footprints; anisotropic or hybrid covariances are left as future work. The pipeline depends on high-quality hand and object masks, which are hard to obtain under extreme occlusion. The system is restricted to single-hand manipulation of a single rigid object, with bimanual interactions and deformable objects deferred to future work, as is integrating stronger physical plausibility constraints beyond the lightweight contact and penetration priors.

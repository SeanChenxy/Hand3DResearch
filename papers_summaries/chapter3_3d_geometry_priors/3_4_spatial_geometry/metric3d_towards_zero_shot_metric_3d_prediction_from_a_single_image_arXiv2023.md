# Metric3D: Towards Zero-shot Metric 3D Prediction from A Single Image

**Authors:** Wei Yin, Chi Zhang, Hao Chen, Zhipeng Cai, Gang Yu, Kaixuan Wang, Xiaozi Chen, Chunhua Shen  
**Date:** 2023-07-20 (arXiv v1; published at ICCV 2023)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2307.10984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.10984)  
**Evidence status:** No Zotero record; the summary was compiled from the arXiv PDF (all 10 pages, including tables and appendix).

## Summary

Metric3D is a monocular model that recovers metric 3D structure — metric depth maps and camera-parameter-aware 3D shapes — from a single image, generalizing zero-shot to unseen cameras and scenes. The paper identifies the core obstacle to zero-shot metric depth as the conflict between two research lines: metric depth models trained on a single dataset with one camera model generalize poorly, while relative/affine-invariant models trained on large mixed datasets achieve robustness but lose real-world scale. Metric3D resolves this dilemma with a canonical camera space transformation module (CSTM) that explicitly accounts for the camera focal length: training images and/or ground-truth depths are transformed into a canonical camera space so that millions of images captured by thousands of camera models can be mixed for training, and a de-canonical transformation at inference recovers the metric depth of the original camera. Trained on about 8 million images from 11 datasets, the model achieves state-of-the-art performance on 7 zero-shot metric benchmarks, won the championship in the 2nd Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge, and directly benefits downstream tasks — plugging its depths into monocular SLAM drastically reduces scale drift and enables metric dense mapping.

## Background and Problem

Reconstructing accurate 3D scenes from a single image is ill-posed, so leading methods either rely on multi-view geometry or on learned priors. Existing learned approaches fall into three groups: metric depth methods (trained and tested on one dataset with fixed camera intrinsics, e.g., early MiDaS-stage models), relative depth methods (predicting ordinal relationships only, with limited application value), and affine-invariant depth methods (trained on large-scale mixed data, which decouple metric information during training and generalize well but can recover only up to an unknown scale and shift, as in LeReS). The paper argues that the key obstacle is metric ambiguity induced by camera models: photos of the same scene taken from the same location with different focal lengths produce identical image appearances but different metric depths, so a network that sees only image appearance is fundamentally confused when supervision comes from mixed cameras. CameraConv addresses this by encoding camera models inside the network, but its data diversity and network capacity are limited. The goal is a single zero-shot transferable model that recovers real-world metric 3D from any single image.

## Method

The paper first analyzes pinhole projection: the projected object size relates to real size through the pixel-represented focal length, giving scale factor α = 1/s. Two observations follow: (O1) sensor size and pixel size do not affect metric depth estimation, and (O2) the focal length is the vital factor for metric estimation — two cameras with different focal lengths at different distances produce identical images but different metric depths. Since estimating focal length from appearance is ill-posed, Metric3D instead assumes the focal length of each training/test image is available and makes the network's problem easier by transforming everything into a canonical camera space with fixed focal length f_c.

Two equivalent transformation strategies are proposed. CSTM_label transforms ground-truth depth labels into the canonical space by scaling with ratio ω_d = f_c/f while leaving the image unchanged; at inference the predicted canonical depth D_c is de-canonicalized as D = (f/f_c)·D_c. CSTM_image instead resizes the input image by ω_T = f_c/f to simulate the canonical camera (labels are resized without scaling), and inference de-canonicalizes by resizing the prediction. Both are explicitly computed from known intrinsics and require no camera-model encoding inside the network, making the method applicable to existing architectures.

Training uses a UNet architecture with a ConvNext-large backbone initialized from ImageNet-22k weights. The supervision combines: a scale-shift-invariant log loss (L_ssi, robust to noisy pseudo-ground-truth depths from SfM), a pairwise normal loss (L_vnl, following LeReS), and a proposed random proposal normalization loss (RPNL) that normalizes predicted and ground-truth depths over 32 randomly cropped local patches instead of the whole image, preserving local contrast that whole-image normalization squeezes out. The total loss is L = L_PWN + L_VNL + L_ssi_log + L_RPNL.

## Contributions

1. A canonical camera space transformation (and de-canonical) method that explicitly resolves the metric depth ambiguity caused by varying camera models, enabling strong zero-shot monocular metric depth learning from large-scale mixed datasets.
2. A random proposal normalization loss (RPNL) that effectively boosts depth accuracy by preserving local contrast during normalization.
3. A model that achieves state-of-the-art performance on 7 zero-shot metric depth benchmarks and wins the 2nd Monocular Depth Estimation Challenge, performing high-quality metric 3D structure recovery in the wild.
4. Demonstration that the recovered metric information directly improves downstream tasks, including monocular SLAM scale-drift reduction, metric dense mapping, and single-image metrology.

## Experimental Setup

Training collects about 8 million images from 11 public datasets spanning indoor and outdoor scenes: DDAD, Lyft, DrivingStereo, DIML, Argoverse2, Cityscapes, DSEC, Mapillary PSD, Pandaset, UASOL, and Taskonomy, with more than 10k different cameras; mini-batches are balanced across datasets. Images are processed by the canonical camera transformation module, horizontally flipped with 50% chance, and cropped to 512×960. Training uses AdamW (batch size 192, initial learning rate 0.0001, polynomial decay, power 0.9) on 48 A100 GPUs for 500K iterations. Evaluation covers: in-domain-without-finetuning metric depth on NYUv2 and KITTI; zero-shot metric depth on 7 unseen benchmarks (7-scenes, iBIMS-1, DIODE, ETH3D, NuScenes, plus others) using officially provided focal lengths and no scale alignment; zero-shot transfer of affine-invariant depth on 5 benchmarks (NYUv2, DIODE, ETH3D, ScanNet, KITTI) with manual scale-shift alignment for baselines; 3D scene reconstruction on 9 unseen NYUv2 scenes (Chamfer l1 and F-score after ICP alignment); metric dense SLAM mapping by plugging predicted depths into Droid-SLAM on the KITTI odometry benchmark (ATE RMSE); and in-the-wild metrology on Flickr photos with EXIF intrinsics.

## Results

- Zero-shot metric depth on unseen scenes: on DIODE the model reaches AbsRel 0.252 (indoor) and 0.414–0.533 (outdoor) versus 0.443 and 0.685–0.751 for Adams and 0.404–0.454 and 0.534–0.549 for NeWCRFs; on iBIMS-1 it attains 0.160/0.521 (AbsRel/RMS) versus 0.312/0.621 for Adams; on 7-scenes 0.183/0.363; on ETH3D 0.416–0.542 AbsRel; on NuScenes 0.147–0.154 AbsRel — outperforming prior metric methods by a large margin on most datasets.
- In-domain-without-finetuning benchmarks: on NYUv2, δ1 = 0.942–0.944 with AbsRel 0.085, comparable to methods trained on the benchmark for hundreds of epochs; on KITTI, δ1 = 0.961–0.962 with AbsRel 0.056–0.058.
- Zero-shot affine-invariant transfer: the model ranks first (rank 1.7–1.3) among DiverseDepth, MiDaS, LeReS, Omnidata, HDN, and DPT-style models, e.g., NYUv2 AbsRel 0.058/δ1 0.963, KITTI 0.090/0.961, ETH3D 0.367/0.964.
- 3D scene reconstruction on 9 unseen NYUv2 scenes: Chamfer l1 of 0.042–0.150 and F-score 0.348–0.845, outperforming RCVD, SC-DepthV2, DPSNet, DPT, and LeReS, whose scale-unknown outputs introduce distortions.
- SLAM integration: feeding Metric3D depths into Droid-SLAM reduces KITTI trajectory error dramatically (e.g., sequence 00 ATE RMSE 1.446 m versus 33.70 m for original Droid-SLAM; sequence 02 3.22 m versus 48.84 m), enabling metric dense mapping.
- Ablations: both CSTM variants achieve comparable metric ability while a no-CSTM baseline fails to converge with mixed metric datasets; CSTM outperforms camera-model encoding via CamConv; a canonical focal length of 1000 pixels performs slightly best; adding RPNL clearly improves over the SSI baseline (e.g., KITTI zero-shot AbsRel 0.097 versus 0.194 with whole-image normalization), which only slightly improves performance.

## Limitations

The method assumes the focal length of each test image is available as input — the canonical transformation requires camera intrinsics, and although the authors argue obtaining them is far easier than understanding scene semantics, this is an external dependency that purely appearance-driven models do not have. Single-image metric recovery remains ill-posed for semantically ambiguous scenes, so accuracy still depends on learned scene-layout priors. On in-domain benchmarks such as NYUv2 and KITTI, the mixed-trained model is comparable to, but does not surpass, specialist networks finetuned on those datasets. The training data, while 10k+ cameras large, still cannot cover every lens-sensor combination, and noisy pseudo-ground-truth depth (SfM-derived stereo supervision) for some outdoor sources requires the scale-shift-invariant loss to suppress label noise.

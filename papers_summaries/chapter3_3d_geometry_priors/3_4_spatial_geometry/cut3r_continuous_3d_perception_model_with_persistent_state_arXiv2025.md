# CUT3R: Continuous 3D Perception Model with Persistent State

**Identifier:** [arXiv:2501.12387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12387)

## Summary

CUT3R (Continuous Updating Transformer for 3D Reconstruction) is an online 3D perception model that processes a stream of RGB images with a persistent internal state: a set of learnable tokens that is continuously updated as each new image arrives. After every observation, the model reads out metric-scale pointmaps (dense per-pixel 3D points in meters), camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and per-pixel confidences — all expressed in the coordinate frame of the first image — so that outputs accumulate online into a coherent dense scene reconstruction without any global-alignment post-processing. Beyond reconstructing what is visible, the model can infer unseen scene structure: a virtual camera query, encoded as a raymap, interacts with the state to produce the pointmap of an unobserved view, in a mechanism the authors liken to completion in Masked Autoencoders. The same network handles video streams and unordered photo collections, static and dynamic scenes, and varying input lengths. Built on the DUSt3R/MASt3R pointmap lineage, it adds the persistent-state mechanism that makes reconstruction streaming and bidirectionally interactive, and it serves as a metric-scale geometry prior for downstream pipelines that need online, calibration-free 3D cues.

## Background and Problem

The paper opens from the observation that humans continuously process visual streams, reconciling prior knowledge with new evidence rather than starting from scratch for each scene. In contrast, the authors classify existing reconstruction pipelines as "tabula rasa": traditional SfM, SLAM, and recent Neural Radiance Fields and 3D Gaussian Splatting approaches start from scratch per scene and therefore struggle with sparse observations or ill-posed conditions. Learning-based priors help — DUSt3R predicts pointmaps for an image pair in a shared frame, implicitly accounting for camera intrinsics and extrinsics — but DUSt3R is tailored to pairs, and although it can be extended to multiple views via an additional global-alignment (GA) stage, that process operates offline, is time-consuming, and cannot dynamically update the reconstruction as new observations arrive. Concurrent online variants have their own restrictions: Spann3R introduces a spatial memory but is based on a static-scene assumption and degrades on dynamic content, while MonST3R finetunes DUSt3R for dynamics yet requires a GA stage that incorporates optical flow at optimization time. Monocular SLAM systems recover ego-motion and sparse point clouds but typically require known camera intrinsics, and learning-based approaches such as 3D-R2N2 are object-centric or need posed images. The desired capability set is: reconstruct from few observations, refine continuously with more observations, and infer properties of unobserved regions — while handling dynamic objects, sparse views, and degenerate camera motion.

## Method

The model takes a stream of images without any camera information. Each image It is encoded by a ViT encoder into tokens Ft. A persistent state, initialized as a set of learnable tokens shared by all scenes, interacts with the image tokens bidirectionally: a state-update pathway integrates the current image into the state, and a state-readout pathway retrieves past context from the state into the image tokens. Both interactions are implemented with two interconnected transformer decoders that jointly operate on image and state tokens, cross-attending to each other in every decoder block. A learnable "pose token" is prepended to the image tokens; its output captures frame-level information such as ego motion. After the interaction, DPT heads produce a self pointmap X̂_t^self with confidence (in the input image's own coordinate frame), a world pointmap X̂_t^world with confidence (in the world frame, defined as the coordinate frame of the initial image), and an MLP pose head produces the 6-DoF relative transformation P̂_t (quaternion and translation) from the current frame to the world; all pointmaps and poses are in metric scale, i.e., meters. Redundant yet supervised prediction of self, world, and pose enables each head to receive direct supervision and permits training on datasets with partial annotations (pose-only or single-view depth).

For querying unseen regions, a virtual camera is encoded as a raymap R — a 6-channel image encoding the origin and direction of rays at each pixel — by a lightweight raymap encoder; these tokens interact with the state through the same decoders (state-update disabled) and the same heads parse them into a pointmap and color for the virtual view. Completion happens at the image level, leveraging the global context of the scene captured in the state, analogous to MAE.

Training uses a confidence-aware 3D regression loss in the style of MASt3R — a scale-normalized Euclidean pointmap distance weighted by predicted confidence with a −α log c regularizer — with the scale factor s fixed to the ground-truth scale (s := s) so the model learns metric-scale pointmaps whenever ground-truth pointmaps are metric. A pose loss minimizes the L2 norm between predicted and ground-truth quaternion and (scale-normalized) translation, and an MSE RGB loss applies when the input is a raymap. Training covers 32 datasets spanning synthetic and real, static and dynamic, indoor and outdoor, and scene-level and object-centric data — including CO3D-v2, ARKitScenes, ScanNet++, ScanNet, TartanAir, Waymo, MegaDepth, MapFree, DLDV, Dynamic iCases, RealEstate10K, and Synscapes — with a flexible formulation allowing partially annotated data. A three-stage curriculum is used: stage one trains on 4-view sequences from the main static datasets at 224×224; stage two adds dynamic-scene datasets and partially annotated datasets; stage three trains at higher resolution (maximum side 512, varied aspect ratios) on longer sequences of 4 to 64 views with the encoder frozen, targeting long-context reasoning. Implementation uses a ViT-Large image encoder initialized from DUSt3R's pretrained encoder, a ViT-Base decoder, 16×16 patches, a state of 768 tokens with dimensionality 768, a 2-block raymap encoder, and AdamW with initial learning rate 1e-4, linear warmup, and cosine decay on eight A100 GPUs (80GB).

## Contributions

1. An online 3D perception framework with a persistent, continuously updated internal state that unifies reconstruction from few observations, progressive refinement as new images arrive, and inference of unobserved scene regions in a single feed-forward model.
2. A state-update/state-readout interaction mechanism, realized with two interconnected transformer decoders plus a learnable pose token, through which each image both modifies the scene representation and reads global context from it.
3. A virtual-view probing capability: queries encoded as raymaps interact with the frozen state to produce pointmaps and colors for unseen viewpoints, directly yielding novel structure beyond the observed views at metric scale.
4. Direct metric-scale pointmap prediction (with ground-truth scale preserved in the loss) that removes the need for a global-alignment stage, enabling fully online processing of videos and unordered photo collections, including dynamic scenes.
5. State-of-the-art or competitive performance across monocular and video depth estimation, camera pose estimation, and 3D reconstruction, with large online-inference speedups over optimization-based counterparts.

## Experimental Setup

Evaluation covers monocular depth estimation, video depth estimation, camera pose estimation, and scene-level 3D reconstruction, all zero-shot with respect to the test domains. Primary baselines are DUSt3R, MASt3R, and the concurrent Spann3R and MonST3R; MASt3R and MonST3R require an extra global-alignment (GA) stage, Spann3R is online but static-scene-only, and only CUT3R (and MASt3R with GA) predict metric pointmaps while others predict relative ones. Monocular depth uses KITTI, Sintel, Bonn, and NYU-v2 with AbsRel and δ < 1.25 per frame. Video depth evaluates per-frame quality and inter-frame consistency on Sintel, BONN, and KITTI under per-sequence scale alignment (and without alignment for metric methods), reporting FPS on KITTI at 512×144 resolution on an A100. Camera pose estimation follows MonST3R on Sintel, TUM-dynamics, and ScanNet, reporting Absolute Translation Error (ATE), Relative Translation Error (RPE trans), and Relative Rotation Error (RPE rot) after Sim(3) alignment; no camera calibration is required. 3D reconstruction uses 7-Scenes and NRGBD with accuracy, completion, and normal consistency (NC), evaluating on image collections with minimal overlap using 3–5 frames per scene for 7-Scenes and 2–4 frames for NRGBD. The state-update analysis introduces a "revisiting" variant: run the model online once, freeze the final state, then re-process the same images to generate predictions. Unseen-region inference is demonstrated qualitatively on the validation sets of MapFree and ARKitScenes, which provide metric camera pose annotations and were not seen in training.

## Results

- Monocular depth (zero-shot, metric): best δ < 1.25 on Bonn (96.2 with AbsRel 0.063, versus DUSt3R 82.5/0.141, MASt3R 82.0/0.142, MonST3R 93.1/0.079) and on NYU-v2 (90.9 with AbsRel 0.086), and second on KITTI (91.2/0.092, versus MASt3R 94.4/0.079); on Sintel the model reaches 53.4 δ with AbsRel 0.428.
- Video depth (per-sequence scale alignment): AbsRel/δ of 0.421/47.9 on Sintel, 0.078/88.5 on BONN, and 0.118/85.1 on KITTI, consistently outperforming DUSt3R-GA (0.656/45.2, 0.155/83.3, 0.144/81.3) and Spann3R (0.622/53.6, 0.118/85.7, 0.198/73.7); MonST3R-GA is stronger on BONN (0.067/96.3) but requires the offline GA stage, whereas CUT3R stays online at 16.38 FPS on KITTI — nearly 50× faster than MonST3R-GA (0.35 FPS) at 512×144 on an A100. In metric scale without alignment, CUT3R reaches 1.029/23.8 on Sintel, 0.103/88.5 on BONN, and 0.122/85.5 on KITTI, clearly ahead of MASt3R-GA (1.022/14.3, 0.272/70.6, 0.467/15.2).
- Camera pose: best overall performance among online methods; on TUM-dynamics CUT3R attains ATE 0.046, RPE trans 0.015, RPE rot 0.473, and on ScanNet 0.099/0.022/0.600, competitive with the optimization-based MonST3R-GA (0.079/0.020/0.475 on ScanNet) and far ahead of online DUSt3R (0.236/0.108/8.210 on ScanNet) and Spann3R (0.096/0.023/0.661).
- 3D reconstruction (sparse views, online): on 7-Scenes, mean accuracy 0.126 (median 0.047), completion 0.154 (median 0.031), NC 0.727 (median 0.834); on NRGBD, accuracy 0.099, completion 0.076, NC 0.837 — significantly outperforming the online Spann3R (0.298 accuracy on 7-Scenes) and matching or beating the top offline method DUSt3R-GA (0.146 accuracy) while running 25× faster (17.00 versus 0.68 FPS on NRGBD).
- State update ("revisiting"): re-processing the sequence with the frozen final state improves results, especially in unseen regions — 7-Scenes accuracy improves from 0.126/0.047 to 0.113/0.107 (mean/median) and NRGBD accuracy from 0.099/0.031 to 0.094/0.076 — verifying that the state effectively accumulates global context.
- Unseen-region inference: on MapFree and ARKitScenes validation scenes, raymap queries of unobserved views produce pointmaps that align with the ground truth and generate plausible new structures (e.g., bushes, ovens, stools) absent from the input view, although without high-frequency detail due to the deterministic nature of the approach.

## Limitations

As with many online methods, predictions may drift over very long sequences because the model lacks explicit or implicit global alignment; the authors name incorporating global alignment as future work. Structure generation is deterministic (regression-based) rather than generative, so probing views that extrapolate far from the provided views produces blurry results — a common failure of regression approaches that a generative formulation could address. Training recurrent networks is also time-consuming. Additionally, unseen-region predictions can lack high-frequency detail, and the state's shared global representation must reconcile static and dynamic content within a single recurrent pass.

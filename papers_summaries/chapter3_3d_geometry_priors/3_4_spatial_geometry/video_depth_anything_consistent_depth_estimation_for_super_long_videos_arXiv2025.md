# Video Depth Anything: Consistent Depth Estimation for Super-Long Videos

**Authors:** Sili Chen, Hengkai Guo, Shengman Zhu, Feihu Zhang, Zilong Huang, Jiashi Feng, Bingyi Kang (ByteDance)  
**Date:** 2025-01 (arXiv v1); 2025-06-15 (arXiv v3)

## Summary

Video Depth Anything is a feed-forward video transformer that estimates temporally consistent, affine-invariant depth for arbitrarily long videos — several minutes and beyond — without sacrificing the spatial accuracy, detail richness, or computational efficiency of the Depth Anything V2 (DAv2) foundation model. The method replaces DAv2's DPT head with a lightweight spatiotemporal head (STH) that inserts temporal self-attention layers at a few low-resolution positions, so temporal information interactions are learned without corrupting the well-trained image representation. Temporal consistency is enforced through a novel temporal gradient matching (TGM) loss, which constrains the temporal gradient of predicted depth to match that of the ground truth directly at corresponding coordinates — requiring no optical flow or geometric priors — combined with the standard scale-shift-invariant loss. For inference on super-long videos, a key-frame-based segment-wise strategy with overlapping frames and depth stitching maintains global scale consistency without cumulative drift. Trained jointly on 730K annotated video frames and 0.62M pseudo-labeled images, the model sets a new state of the art in zero-shot video depth estimation across five benchmarks in spatial accuracy, temporal consistency, and latency simultaneously, with the smallest variant running in real time at 30 FPS.

## Background and Problem

Depth Anything V2 achieves strong zero-shot monocular depth with rich detail, but it is trained exclusively on static images, so its video outputs flicker and blur — fatal for robotics, augmented reality, and video editing, which all require temporally consistent depth. Existing remedies each fall short: test-time optimization methods (e.g., NVDS) align monocular predictions with optical-flow or camera-pose constraints but are too slow at inference; video-diffusion approaches (DepthCrafter, DepthAnyVideo, ChronoDepth) produce fine-grained consistent depth but are computationally expensive, cannot reuse existing depth foundation models, and — limited by training window size — still flicker between windows and degrade on long videos; plug-and-play temporal modules depend on external optical flow or pose estimates and inherit their errors. The open question is whether a model can perfectly inherit the capabilities of an existing depth foundation model while gaining temporal stability for arbitrarily long videos, without introducing geometric or generative priors.

## Method

Architecture: the frozen DAv2 encoder (built on DINOv2) processes video frames by collapsing the temporal dimension into the batch dimension, extracting strong per-frame features while preserving its learned representation. The spatiotemporal head is built upon the DPT head with the only modification being inserted temporal layers — a multi-head self-attention block plus feed-forward network executed solely along the temporal dimension, with absolute positional embeddings capturing frame order — placed at a few lower-resolution feature positions to bound the extra compute. Four feature maps sampled from the encoder pass through Reassemble and fusion layers into the temporal layers, and the fused high-resolution features are decoded into depth that shares one scale and shift across the entire video.

Temporal gradient matching loss: optical-flow-based warping (OPW) losses assume depth at corresponding points is invariant across frames, which breaks under camera or object motion (e.g., a moving car changes the relative depth of static background). TGM instead observes that the change in depth between adjacent frames — the temporal gradient — should be consistent with the ground truth: it penalizes the discrepancy between the predicted and ground-truth temporal gradients computed directly at the same pixel coordinates (the "stable error"), with no optical flow. In practice the loss is applied only where the ground-truth temporal gradient is below 0.05, avoiding edges, dynamic objects, and other instability sources. The total loss is a weighted combination of TGM and the scale-shift-invariant loss.

Super-long inference: videos are processed in overlapping windows. Key-frame referencing augments each new window with two key frames sampled from the previous window (interval 12) plus eight overlapping frames (window N=32); new predictions are scale-shift-aligned to the previous window through the overlapping frames, keeping content from earlier windows with minimal computation and preventing drift. Depth clip stitching then fuses the predictions on overlapping frames with linearly decaying weights to avoid flickering at window boundaries.

## Contributions

1. A method that transforms Depth Anything V2 into a video depth estimator for arbitrarily long videos while preserving its generalization, detail, and efficiency — no geometric or video-generation priors involved.
2. A simple yet effective temporal gradient matching loss that enforces temporal consistency without optical flow, geometric assumptions, or extra network inputs.
3. A key-frame-based, segment-wise inference strategy with depth stitching that maintains global scale consistency on videos of several minutes without accumulated drift.
4. State-of-the-art zero-shot video depth estimation across five benchmarks simultaneously in spatial accuracy, temporal consistency, and computational efficiency, with released models at multiple scales, the smallest running in real time at 30 FPS.

## Experimental Setup

Zero-shot video evaluation uses five datasets spanning indoor, outdoor, and wild scenes — KITTI, ScanNet, Bonn (up to 500 frames per video, versus 110 for prior work), NYUv2, and Sintel (30- and 170-frame subsets; Sintel is reported only for methods that fit its frame budget, as DepthAnyVideo caps at 192 frames). Predicted depth is aligned to ground truth by uniform scale and shift; metrics are AbsRel and δ1 for geometric accuracy and the Temporal Alignment Error (TAE), which measures reprojection error between consecutive depth frames, for temporal stability. Baselines are NVDS, ChronoDepth, DepthCrafter, DepthAnyVideo, DAv2, and NVDS with a DAv2 backbone, compared under the official evaluation protocol. Static-image quality is assessed on five image benchmarks (KITTI, Sintel, NYUv2, ETH3D, DIODE) against DAv2 and DepthCrafter. Latency is measured per 518×518 frame on one A100. Ablations cover the loss design (VideoAlign, OPW+SSI, SE+SSI, TGM+SSI), inference schemes (baseline, overlap alignment, overlap interpolation, overlap interpolation + key-frame reference) and window sizes (16/32/48), and image-dataset distillation.

## Results

- Zero-shot video depth: VDA-L ranks first overall (mean δ1 rank 1.7), outperforming all baselines on temporal consistency across all datasets and on spatial accuracy on four of five. On KITTI it reaches AbsRel 0.083 (synthetic-only variant) versus 0.137 for DAv2-L and 0.164 for DepthCrafter; ScanNet 0.087; Bonn 0.070; NYUv2 0.061/δ1 0.961. On Sintel-170 TAE is 0.570 versus 2.176 (NVDS) and 0.639 (DepthCrafter) — and VDA-L is trained on much less video data than DepthCrafter (over 10M frames) or DepthAnyVideo (4M).
- Long videos (500 frames): metrics evaluated at 110/192/300/400/500 frames show minimal degradation with length, clearly outperforming DepthCrafter at all lengths, surpassing DAv2 on ScanNet and NYUv2 and matching it on Bonn — while supporting arbitrary-length inference, which DAv2 and window-limited baselines cannot.
- Static images: VDA-L remains competitive with DAv2-L (e.g., KITTI AbsRel 0.075/δ1 0.946, NYUv2 0.046/0.974), showing the image capability of the foundation model is retained.
- Latency (A100, per frame): VDA-S 9.1 ms and VDA-L 67 ms (FP32) versus DepthCrafter 910 ms, ChronoDepth 506 ms, DepthAnyVideo 159 ms, NVDS 204 ms, and DAv2-L 60 ms — the large model is only ~10% slower than its image-only teacher.
- Loss ablation: TGM+SSI attains the best temporal stability (TAE 0.267 versus 1.207 for VideoAlign+SSI and 0.918 for OPW+SSI) with spatial metrics comparable to the strongest alternative (AbsRel 0.166, δ1 0.832), eliminating the dependence on optical flow.
- Inference ablation: overlap interpolation + key-frame reference with window 32 gives AbsRel 0.144 / δ1 0.851 / TAE 0.718 versus baseline 0.157/0.826/0.874; overlap alignment scores similarly on 500-frame benchmarks but exhibits cumulative scale drift on a 4-minute real video, which key-frame referencing avoids.
- Image distillation: adding 0.62M pseudo-labeled images improves both image metrics (AbsRel 0.180→0.167, δ1 0.876→0.883) and video metrics (AbsRel 0.148→0.142, TAE 0.761→0.742).

## Limitations

The output is affine-invariant depth with a single shared scale and shift per video — not metric depth — so downstream metric uses still require alignment with poses or other references. Temporal consistency is supervised by ground-truth depth from video datasets, whose scale and diversity (730K frames) remain far below image-depth corpora; the heuristic gradient threshold (0.05) for masking unstable regions is hand-tuned. On Sintel — movie footage with cinematic focal lengths — the compact model trails DepthCrafter, which was trained on movie data, indicating a domain gap that synthetic-and-real video depth annotations do not fully cover. The key-frame referencing scheme assumes approximate scale continuity across adjacent windows, so extreme viewpoint jumps or cuts can still perturb the global scale, and the choice of window size (32) trades memory against accuracy with diminishing returns beyond it.

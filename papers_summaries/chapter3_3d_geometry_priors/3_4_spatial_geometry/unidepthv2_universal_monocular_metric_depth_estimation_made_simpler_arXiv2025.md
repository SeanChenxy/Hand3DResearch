# UniDepthV2: Universal Monocular Metric Depth Estimation Made Simpler

## Summary

UniDepthV2 is a universal monocular metric depth estimation (MMDE) model that reconstructs metric 3D scenes from a single image across domains — indoor, outdoor, and challenging environments — without requiring camera intrinsics or any other external information at inference time. Extending the CVPR 2024 UniDepth, it predicts 3D points directly in the output space through a self-promptable camera module that produces a dense camera representation (azimuth-elevation rays) which conditions the depth module via cross-attention. A pseudo-spherical output parameterization (azimuth, elevation, log-depth) disentangles the camera and depth dimensions, and a geometric invariance loss enforces that camera-conditioned depth features stay consistent across geometric augmentations simulating different cameras of the same scene. Compared to its predecessor, UniDepthV2 adds an edge-guided scale-shift-invariant loss that sharpens depth discontinuities, a variable-shape training strategy for robustness to input aspect ratio and resolution, an uncertainty-level output for confidence-aware downstream use, and a substantially simplified and more efficient architecture (about one-third of UniDepth's compute, 2x faster). Across ten zero-shot evaluation benchmarks UniDepthV2 consistently demonstrates superior performance and generalization, ranking first among published methods in the official KITTI Depth Prediction Benchmark.

## Background and Problem

Accurate monocular metric depth underpins downstream 3D perception in robotics, AR, and autonomous driving, but MMDE models remain confined to their training domains: datasets typically cover few scenes and cameras, and even the imperfect ground truth used for supervision is sparse and incomplete near edges, yielding blurry predictions with inaccurate fine-grained geometry. A few methods achieve generalizable MMDE, but they assume controlled test-time setups — most require ground-truth camera intrinsics, which restricts applicability to the full in-the-wild spectrum (video processing, crowd-sourced images) and injects intrinsic noise into the model. The demanding target is universal MMDE: deliver metric 3D predictions for any scene from a single RGB image with no additional external information, while remaining flexible enough to exploit camera information when available. Beyond generalization, the metric-depth field also lacks local accuracy: global scene-scale balance is achieved at the cost of sharp, well-localized depth discontinuities, and few models expose calibrated confidence for downstream tasks.

## Method

Camera conditioning: the camera module initializes four tokens from ViT class tokens and processes them through two layers of self-attention to produce multiplicative residuals on pinhole parameters (focal lengths and principal point), making predictions invariant to input image size. These parameters back-project every pixel into normalized camera rays, forming a dense camera representation C (a tensor of azimuth and elevation values) that is embedded via Sine encoding into conditioning embeddings E. The depth module conditions its decoder features through cross-attention with E at every resolution — the camera acts as a prompt, bootstrapping global scene scale so the depth module need not relearn it, and conversely the model can be run with rays from a known camera model via an unprojection operator.

Pseudo-spherical output space: instead of Cartesian (x, y, z), the 3D output is expressed as azimuth θ, elevation φ, and log-depth z_log — an orthogonal parameterization by design, since θ and φ belong to the camera direction and z to depth, in contrast to the entanglement present in Cartesian backprojection.

Geometric invariance loss: for each sample, two geometric augmentations (scaling and relative translation followed by cropping) simulate the same scene captured by different cameras; the consistency loss ||T2∘T1⁻¹(Z1) − sg(Z2)||₁ is applied on camera-aware depth features (D|E) and averaged over both directions, so camera-prompted features remain invariant when conditioned on bootstrapped camera predictions.

Edge-guided normalized loss: to recover sharp local discontinuities, patches are sampled from regions where the RGB spatial gradient ranks in the top 5% (4% and 8% of image size), excluding superpixels whose uniformity emphasis suppresses thin structures; within each patch, predicted and ground-truth depth are independently normalized by median absolute deviation, and the L1 discrepancy between the two normalizations is penalized. This targets two failure modes — ignoring genuine depth discontinuities and hallucinating discontinuities from high-frequency appearance (checkerboards, repetitive textures) — and is computed with a custom CUDA kernel 20x faster than naive patch extraction.

Training: 23 public datasets totaling 16M images; ViT backbones initialized with DINOv2 weights in Small, Base, and Large variants; AdamW with 10x-lower backbone learning rate, cosine annealing with one restart, 300K iterations at batch 128 (~6 days on 16 NVIDIA A4090). Images are sampled with variable pixel counts (0.2-0.6MP) and per-batch aspect ratios (2:1) with photometric and geometric augmentations, producing robustness to input shape. The total loss combines the reformulated MSE in 3D output space (per-dimension weights, log-depth weighted 0.15), the consistency loss, the edge-guided loss, and an L1 uncertainty loss supervising the predicted uncertainty against the log-space depth error.

## Contributions

1. A revised, simplified camera-conditioned architecture for universal MMDE — Sine-encoded camera embeddings, attention-free MLP decoder with ResNet blocks, and FPN-style multi-resolution fusion — that is substantially faster (latency 35.1 ms versus 91.0 ms for UniDepth at 0.5MP) and one-third the computation, while more accurate.
2. A novel edge-guided scale-shift-invariant loss computed on RGB-gradient-selected patches, improving localization and sharpness of metric-depth edges (+4.7% δ1, +1.8% F_A over the same architecture without it).
3. A practical training strategy over variable image shapes and resolutions within each mini-batch, yielding near-invariance of depth accuracy to input aspect ratio and resolution.
4. An additional uncertainty-level output supervised without extra signals, enabling downstream geometric applications that require confidence-aware depth inputs, plus state-of-the-art zero-shot generalization across ten benchmarks spanning indoor, outdoor, and challenging domains.

## Experimental Setup

Training uses 23 datasets (e.g., A2D2, Argoverse2, ARKit-Scenes, BEDLAM, BlendedMVS, DL3DV, DrivingStereo, DynamicReplica, HM3D, Mapillary-PSD, MegaDepth, PointOdyssey, ScanNet, TartanAir, Taskonomy, WildRGBD) totaling 16M images. Generalization is evaluated zero-shot on ten unseen datasets grouped as indoor (SUN-RGBD, iBims-1, TUM-RGBD), outdoor (ETH3D, Sintel, DDAD, NuScenes), and challenging (HAMMER, Booster, FLSea — underwater and transparent objects). Metrics: δ1 and AbsRel for depth, F_A (AUC of F1 up to 1/20 of max depth) for 3D estimation accuracy, and ρ_A (AUC of mean angular error of camera rays up to 15°) for camera performance; no test-time augmentation and no ground-truth camera are used. Baselines include Metric3D, Metric3Dv2, ZoeDepth, UniDepth, MASt3R, and DepthPro, several of which require GT intrinsics. Additional evaluations cover inference efficiency (A6000, 0.5MP), the official KITTI Depth Prediction Benchmark, in-domain fine-tuning on NYU and KITTI, uncertainty calibration (AUSE, nAUSE, Spearman ρ), and ablations of architecture, loss placement, output representation, and conditioning.

## Results

- Zero-shot depth and 3D: UniDepthV2 performs better than or on par with all baselines, including those consuming GT camera parameters, with F_A improvements of 0.5%-18.1% over the second-best method. Highlights (Large model): SUN-RGBD δ1 96.4 / AbsRel 6.8, iBims-1 94.5 / 7.8, TUM-RGBD 90.5 / 22.1, ETH3D F_A 59.3, Sintel F_A 73.6, DDAD δ1 80.5, NuScenes δ1 87.0 / AbsRel 15.0, HAMMER δ1 89.7, Booster δ1 62.1, FLSea δ1 90.5; it outscores UniDepth F_A on iBims-1 (70.9), DDAD (74.3), and NuScenes (69.7) in nearly all cases.
- Camera accuracy (ρ_A): improves only marginally over UniDepth, indicating the diversity gain is capacity-driven; depth and 3D metrics nonetheless improve substantially.
- Efficiency (A6000, 0.5MP): Small 23.0 ms / 34.18M params / 0.29 TFLOPs; Base 35.1 ms / 114.4M / 0.82T; Large 60.4 ms / 353.8M / 2.17T — versus DepthPro 276 ms / 932M / 19.3T and Metric3Dv2 133.6 ms / 411.9M / 3.47T; UniDepthV2 is the fastest and most efficient despite the extra camera-prediction module.
- Official KITTI benchmark: UniDepthV2 ranks first among published methods.
- In-domain fine-tuning: on NYU, Large attains δ1 98.8 / RMSE 0.180, surpassing DepthAnything (98.4 / 0.206); on KITTI Eigen-split training, δ1 98.9 / RMS 1.71.
- Ablations: the pseudo-spherical output decisively beats Cartesian regression (δ1 60.0 versus 49.1); applying the consistency loss on camera-conditioned features at every scale outperforms output-space-only application; the edge-guided loss contributes most of the δ1/F_A gain and visibly sharpens edges on KITTI and Sintel; conditioning via the prompted camera module improves over both UniDepth and a condition-free variant.
- Uncertainty: in-domain nAUSE 0.199-0.221 and ρ 0.68-0.74 (well calibrated); zero-shot nAUSE 0.54-0.66 and ρ 0.22-0.29 (ranking partly preserved, calibration degrades).
- Robustness: accuracy (δ1) is nearly invariant to image shape across 0.5-2x normalized aspect ratios, unlike Metric3Dv2 which degrades sharply.

## Limitations

The model still fails on domains that violate its pinhole-camera assumption: mirrors, non-pinhole and fisheye imagery, paintings, and optical illusions — it can resolve certain human-related illusions and recognize framed paintings but struggles with non-realistic paintings and extreme projection geometries. Camera-parameter accuracy (ρ_A) improves only marginally over UniDepth, so the dense camera estimate should be treated as a conditioning signal rather than a calibration product. Zero-shot uncertainty is weakly calibrated (nAUSE 0.54-0.66, ρ 0.22-0.29), limiting the reliability of the confidence output under domain shift, and larger models learn sharper edge-focused uncertainty priors while misestimating magnitude in novel domains. The edge-guided loss depends on RGB gradients, so chromatic edges without depth transitions and depth edges without appearance contrast remain adversarial cases, and the 16M-image training mixture, while broad, does not cover every sensor geometry — accuracy still degrades gracefully rather than remaining perfect out of distribution.

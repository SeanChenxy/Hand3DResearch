## Summary

OpenShape is a method for learning multi-modal joint representations across text, 2D images, and 3D point clouds. It adopts the standard multi-modal contrastive learning framework, but its distinguishing focus is scaling up 3D training data to enable open-world 3D shape understanding. The paper identifies four factors to improve during training: (a) data scale, by ensembling four public 3D datasets into 876k training shapes; (b) text quality, addressed with three automatic strategies that filter and enrich the inaccurate, noisy text descriptions dominating web-contributed 3D datasets; (c) 3D backbone scaling, with a study showing that unlike small datasets, large ensembled data makes backbone scaling non-trivial and rewarding; and (d) data resampling through an offline hard negative mining module that combats severe class imbalance. Evaluated zero-shot, OpenShape reaches 46.8% top-1 accuracy on the 1,156-category Objaverse-LVIS benchmark, where prior methods score under 10%, and 85.3% on ModelNet40, outperforming previous zero-shot baselines by at least 20%. Because its shape embeddings are aligned with the CLIP embedding space, OpenShape plugs into off-the-shelf CLIP-based models for point cloud captioning and point cloud-conditioned image generation. In HOI reconstruction pipelines, this open-vocabulary alignment is exactly what allows a recognized object name or an image crop to be turned into a retrieved 3D shape hypothesis from Objaverse.

## Background and Problem

3D shape understanding is in demand for augmented/virtual reality, autonomous driving, and robotics, but existing data-driven approaches remain limited in training-dataset scale and generalize poorly to unseen categories, hindering real-world deployment. One popular route to open-vocabulary 3D understanding projects 3D point clouds into the 2D image domain and uses CLIP for inference, but such methods suffer from occlusion and information loss during projection, unnecessary latency from point cloud rendering, and multiple 2D inference passes. The other route trains a 3D-native encoder that distills knowledge from pretrained 2D models, exemplified by ULIP, which fixes CLIP's text and image encoders and trains a 3D point-cloud encoder to align the 3D embedding space with CLIP's image and language spaces via contrastive learning across all three modalities. However, ULIP trains on only 52k shapes spanning 55 object categories (ShapeNetCore), so it still struggles with out-of-distribution shapes and fails to demonstrate open-world understanding. The bottleneck is the amount of knowledge that can be distilled, which is determined by the size and diversity of the 3D dataset. Scaling up, in turn, raises new problems: Objaverse's web-uploaded shapes carry noisy user-written names, the ensemble of datasets is highly class-imbalanced, and the paper empirically shows that scaling up 3D backbones — which most prior work targeting ShapeNet found unnecessary — becomes important and non-trivial at this data scale.

## Method

OpenShape trains a 3D-native point-cloud encoder f^P to align with the frozen CLIP text encoder f^T and image encoder f^I (OpenCLIP ViT-bigG-14 in most experiments), preserving CLIP's feature priors and avoiding collapse by keeping the 2D encoders frozen; their features are pre-extracted and cached for speed. For a batch of triplets {P_i, T_i, I_i}, a symmetric contrastive loss of four terms couples point clouds with texts, point clouds with images, and images with texts, using normalized projected features and a learnable temperature. Training triplets are built by sampling 10,000 points per mesh with interpolated vertex colors and rendering 12 color images per shape with cameras uniformly covering the object; dataset thumbnails are added as image candidates.

Data scale: four public datasets are ensembled — Objaverse (~800k shapes), ShapeNetCore (52k), 3D-FUTURE (17.6k), and ABO (8.8k) — giving 876k shapes with far more diverse categories than any single source.

Text filtering and enrichment: (1) Filtering — GPT-4 judges batches of 256 raw user texts and marks inaccurate or uninformative descriptions (timestamps, model numbers, filenames, random characters), removing about 30% of raw texts while keeping all shapes, with prompts presented as text; (2) Captioning — BLIP and the Azure cognition service caption thumbnails or frontal renders, producing two captions per shape that complement each other; (3) Image retrieval — k-NN images of shape renderings are retrieved from LAION-5B with the CLIP ViT-L retrieval index and their captions adopted as additional texts, which cover a wider range of text styles and more fine-grained semantics. At each pretraining iteration a text source category (raw text, captions, or retrieved) is randomly sampled per shape; template-based prompt engineering (extending a word to templated sentences and averaging embeddings) is applied to training texts and test-time category names alike.

Backbone scaling: six backbones — PointNet (1.3M), DGCNN (2.3M), PointMLP (9.3M), PointNeXt (2.8M), PointBERT (5.1M), SparseConv (6.84M) — are compared when trained on ShapeNet versus the ensembled dataset. On ShapeNet all perform similarly (e.g., 67.0-73.5 top-1 on ModelNet40), but on the ensemble the gap widens (74.2-84.5), showing that some backbones saturate on small data. Because traditional non-hierarchical backbones such as DGCNN and PointNet rely on dense points or kNN modeling and become time- and memory-intensive when scaled, the paper selects the transformer-based PointBERT and the convolution-based SparseConv, and scales them up (e.g., PointBERT to 39.6M, SparseConv to 42.9M and 99M parameters) for final training.

Hard negative mining: given the severe imbalance of the ensemble (common categories in the tens of thousands, rare ones with a few dozen shapes), random batches rarely place confusing categories (e.g., apple and cherries) together. An offline two-round scheme first trains with random batches until near convergence, computes the kNN of each shape in the learned 3D embedding space, and then constructs each iteration's batch as s seed shapes plus m of their kNN neighbors, so confusable shapes share a batch. The resulting false negatives (e.g., two apples) are removed by leveraging the frozen text and image embeddings: when the contrastive loss between a shape i and a same-seed shape j is computed, j is excluded as a negative if i^T * b^t + δ > i^T * b^s, where b^t and b^i are text and image embeddings of j and δ is a small threshold.

## Contributions

1. OpenShape, a multi-modal joint representation learning framework for text, image, and 3D point clouds with a specific focus on scaling up 3D training data toward open-world 3D understanding.
2. Three automatic strategies — GPT-4-based text filtering, captioning with two complementary models, and retrieval-based text enrichment — for handling the noisy, low-quality texts of web-contributed 3D datasets.
3. A systematic study of 3D backbone scalability under large-scale ensembled data, showing that backbone scaling becomes non-trivial and beneficial at this scale and leading to scaled PointBERT and SparseConv encoders.
4. An offline hard negative mining strategy that counters class imbalance in the ensembled dataset and yields faster, better contrastive training.
5. State-of-the-art zero-shot 3D classification (46.8% top-1 on Objaverse-LVIS, 85.3% on ModelNet40) plus demonstrations that the learned embeddings encode fine-grained subcategories, attributes, and styles and support cross-modal applications such as point cloud captioning and point cloud-conditioned image generation without extra training.

## Experimental Setup

Training uses the ensembled dataset of 876k shapes from Objaverse, ShapeNetCore, 3D-FUTURE, and ABO, with 10,000-point clouds and 12 rendered images per shape. The pretrained CLIP model is OpenCLIP ViT-bigG-14, with text and image encoders frozen and cached. Backbone scaling experiments sweep six architectures (PointNet, DGCNN, PointMLP, PointNeXt, PointBERT, SparseConv) at their original sizes and scaled-up sizes on the ensemble excluding Objaverse-LVIS. Zero-shot classification is evaluated on ModelNet40 (40 categories) and ScanObjectNN (15 categories) and on the challenging Objaverse-LVIS benchmark (46,832 shapes over 1,156 categories, a human-verified subset of Objaverse). Baselines include projection-based PointCLIP and PointCLIPv2 and 3D-native ReCon, CG3D, CLIP2Point, and ULIP, using released checkpoints, with ULIP additionally retrained on the ensemble under three data regimes ("Ensembled", "Ensembled (no LVIS)", and "ShapeNet") to disentangle the sources of improvement. Few-shot linear probing is averaged over 10 random seeds at 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 labeled samples per class. Ablations examine data ratios, text filtering/enrichment, RGB input, and each contrastive loss term. Cross-modal applications feed the 3D embeddings into ClipCap for point cloud captioning and Stable unCLIP for point cloud-conditioned image generation.

## Results

- Zero-shot classification (Ensembled training): OpenShape-PointBERT achieves 85.8% top-1 (96.5% top-3, 98.0% top-5) on ModelNet40, 52.2/79.7/88.7 on ScanObjectNN, and 46.8/69.1/77.0 on Objaverse-LVIS; OpenShape-SparseConv reaches 84.4/96.5/98.0 on ModelNet40, 56.7/79.7/88.7 on ScanObjectNN, and 46.8/69.1/77.0 on Objaverse-LVIS (PointBERT variant 46.8 top-1). The paper reports ModelNet40 accuracy of 85.3% for OpenShape, at least 20 points above previous zero-shot baselines (ULIP-Official 60.4, CLIP2Point 47.9, CG3D 48.5), and Objaverse-LVIS 46.8% versus less than 10% for all prior methods.
- Objaverse-LVIS long-tail: OpenShape improves classification accuracy on long-tail categories from a dull value under 10% to 46.8%, and reaches 77.0% top-5, about 20 points above the retrained ULIP (58.9 top-1, 80.0 top-5 for ULIP-PointBERT Retrained) — gains attributed to text enrichment, backbone scaling, and other training strategies rather than data alone.
- Fully-supervised parity on ModelNet40: OpenShape (85.8%) outperforms fully-supervised 3D-Jigsaw (85.3%) and VoxNet (84.9%), the first time a zero-shot method has matched fully-supervised 3D learning on this benchmark. On ScanObjectNN, OpenShape-SparseConv achieves 56.7% zero-shot accuracy without sim-to-real training, surpassing the 52.7% of SKPCov, a method specifically designed for sim-to-real transfer.
- Data-scale ablation: training with 1% of the ensemble (about 8.8k shapes) achieves similar or better Objaverse-LVIS zero-shot performance than 77.1k shapes without it, indicating that data diversity is sometimes more crucial than raw scale; model scaling plays an essential role on the large-scale data (Figure 4 shows all backbones improving substantially with scale).
- Text strategies: removing caption and retrieval text drops Objaverse-LVIS top-1 from 42.0 to 37.0; removing text filtering drops it to 41.4; PointBERT gains more than 10 points from text enrichment. Removing 3D-text contrastive learning is the most damaging ablation (23.3 on Objaverse-LVIS, 67.4 on ModelNet40), while removing 3D-image contrastive learning costs less (41.0/81.0); OpenShape works well with xyz coordinates only (39.6/83.6). Hard negative mining adds a further 43.0 to 43.4 on Objaverse-LVIS and 83.1 to 83.4 on ModelNet40.
- Retrieval and generation: OpenShape retrieves shapes from images, texts, or point clouds with detailed-text queries covering subcategories (e.g., "right heels", "white conical mug"), attributes (color, shape, style), and their combinations, with retrieved shapes often exhibiting integrated visual and semantic elements from multiple input shapes; feeding its embeddings into ClipCap and Stable unCLIP produces qualitative point cloud captions and point cloud-conditioned images without additional training or finetuning.

## Limitations

The authors explicitly list three directions for future work in their discussion. First, 876k 3D shapes, while large by 3D standards, is still quite limited compared with the billions of samples behind 2D models, so more powerful 3D representations await further data investment. Second, the shape representations mainly capture global semantic and visual features and lack part-level information; adding part-level supervision would be beneficial. Third, the model is trained mainly on synthetic data, and the sim-to-real domain gap remains a challenge to address with explicit designs. Beyond the stated ones, the reported Objaverse-LVIS evaluation excludes all shapes of that benchmark from training, yet the ensemble still contains other Objaverse shapes, and the few-shot experiments show that on in-domain benchmarks (ModelNet40, ScanObjectNN) the margin over a properly retrained baseline (ULIP) largely disappears, with error dominated by in-category sample bias and the sim-to-real domain gap rather than representation quality. The text-filtering pipeline also depends on the availability and judgment of a large language model (GPT-4), and cross-modal generation results are presented qualitatively without quantitative benchmarks.

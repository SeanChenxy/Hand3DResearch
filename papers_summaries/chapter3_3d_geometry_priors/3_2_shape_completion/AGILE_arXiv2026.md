## Summary

AGILE reconstructs dynamic hand-object interactions from monocular video by replacing reconstruction-centric pipelines (neural rendering plus Structure-from-Motion initialization) with an agentic generation pipeline: a VLM agent selects keyframes, supervises multi-view synthesis with rejection sampling, and drives texture refinement to produce a complete, watertight, textured object mesh despite heavy hand occlusion; object pose is then anchored at a single interaction onset frame with FoundationPose and propagated bi-directionally without SfM. On DexYCB it reports a Chamfer Distance of 0.52 cm^2 and a 100% success rate, versus 2.05 cm^2 and 25% success for MagicHOI, and it delivers simulation-ready assets demonstrated through real-to-sim retargeting to a dexterous robotic hand.

## Background and Problem

The paper targets reconstruction of 4D hand-object interaction trajectories (hand motion plus full object geometry, texture, and 6D pose over time) from monocular video captured by a fixed camera, with the goal of producing digital twins that are visually coherent, geometrically explicit, and physically plausible for downstream tasks such as robotic imitation learning, physics-based simulation, and VR. The authors identify two barriers in existing pipelines. First, reconstruction-based geometry via neural rendering (NeRF or 3D Gaussian Splatting, as in HOLD and BIGS) depends on multi-view consistency, which is violated by severe hand occlusion, yielding fragmented, noisy, non-watertight geometries unsuitable for physics engines. Second, pose initialization anchored on Structure-from-Motion (COLMAP) is brittle in dynamic scenes with textureless objects, rapid motion, or occlusion; when SfM fails to register frames, the entire pipeline collapses. They also note that generative alternatives have gaps: MagicHOI uses diffusion priors with NeRF but produces over-smoothed meshes (via Score Distillation Sampling) and retains SfM dependency, while single-view methods such as SAM 3D suffer from occlusion-induced information loss. Harnessing 2D generative priors to synthesize missing views is itself unreliable because diffusion models hallucinate views inconsistent with the actual object, so the paper argues for an intelligent mechanism to select informative frames and filter generated views for consistency.

## Method

AGILE has three stages. (1) Agentic textured object generation: a VLM agent (Gemini 3 Pro) selects 1-4 informative keyframes that maximize viewpoint coverage and minimize occlusion; these prompt an image generation model (Gemini 2.5 Flash) to synthesize orthogonal views of the object; a VLM critic scores geometry, texture, and material consistency against the original frames and discards candidates below a threshold via rejection sampling. Validated views are lifted to 3D by a feed-forward generative model (Hunyuan3D 2.0/2.5); the raw mesh undergoes automated retopology (Blender quad remeshing to roughly 10,000 faces) and UV unwrapping, and an agentic texture refinement step uses an image-to-image editing model, again supervised by the VLM critic through rejection sampling to remove hallucinated artifacts. (2) SfM-free initialization of pose and scale: MoGe-2 provides camera intrinsics and per-frame metric depth; SAM2 provides hand and object masks; WiLoR predicts per-frame MANO hand parameters, with hand metric scale recovered by constrained ICP against depth-unprojected hand points and per-frame hand translation by PnP against 2D keypoints. The object's global metric scale is estimated by ICP alignment of the generated mesh to unprojected object point clouds across frames. The interaction onset frame (IOF), the first frame where the object mask shows significant displacement, is detected from mask variation, and the object's initial pose is estimated at the IOF with FoundationPose using the scale-normalized generated mesh. (3) Contact-aware hand-object optimization: optimization is anchored at the IOF and propagates bi-directionally frame by frame. At the IOF, hand and object poses plus the object's anisotropic scale are jointly optimized; thereafter the scale is frozen and each frame alternates two steps. Step 1 refines hand translation by a joint reprojection loss (MSE between projected 3D joints and 2D detections) with rotation and scale fixed. Step 2 optimizes the object's rigid pose with a composite objective Lobj = lambda_mask * Lmask + lambda_dino * Ldino + lambda_interact * Linteract, where Lmask is an L2 silhouette alignment between the rendered alpha map and the segmentation mask, Ldino maximizes DINOv3 semantic feature similarity at projected locations of canonical surface sample points under an occlusion mask, and Linteract maps hand vertices into the object's local frame and penalizes their displacement relative to the previous frame, SDF-gated by soft weights w_i = 1 - tanh(sigma * d_i) (sigma = 40) so that only near-contact grasping regions are constrained, preventing sliding or interpenetration. Reported loss weights are Lmask 5.0, Ldino 10.0, a ray-traced contact loss 5.0 at the IOF, and Linteract 400 on HO3D / 200 on DexYCB with a 0.05 m distance threshold; per-frame optimization runs up to 200 iterations for the hand and 400 for the object with Adam (learning rates 2e-3 for rotation, 1e-3 for translation).

## Contributions

- The first agentic HOI pipeline that integrates VLM-guided quality assessment with generative models: VLM keyframe selection, multi-view synthesis with VLM rejection sampling, and VLM-supervised texture refinement, producing high-fidelity, watertight, simulation-ready meshes independent of video occlusion.
- A robust anchor-and-track optimization strategy that eliminates dependency on brittle SfM: pose initialization is anchored at a single interaction onset frame using FoundationPose, and the trajectory is propagated via semantic (DINOv3), silhouette, and SDF-gated interaction-stability constraints.
- Extensive validation across single-hand benchmarks (DexYCB, HO3D-v3), bimanual interaction (ARCTIC rigid-object subset), and in-the-wild sequences, showing state-of-the-art geometric accuracy and a 100% success rate where baselines collapse (up to 75% failure), plus a real-to-sim retargeting demonstration on a dexterous robotic hand in Isaac Gym that qualitatively validates contact states without manual mesh cleanup.

## Experimental Setup

Evaluation uses DexYCB (20 selected trajectories spanning diverse object categories), HO3D-v3 (18 sequences following the HOLD protocol), the ARCTIC dataset rigid-object subset (following the HOLD protocol, processed bimanually with independent contact constraints), and an in-the-wild collection combining sequences from prior work with self-captured footage of complex-geometry objects. Baselines are HOLD (implicit neural rendering) and MagicHOI (diffusion-prior NeRF) on the single-hand benchmarks, and additionally BIGS (3D Gaussian Splatting) on ARCTIC. Metrics: root-relative hand MPJPE in millimeters; object Chamfer Distance in squared centimeters with F-scores at 5 mm and 10 mm; hand-relative Chamfer Distance (CDh) for interaction quality; and Success Rate (SR), where a sequence fails on initialization breakdown (e.g., SfM collapse) or catastrophic tracking drift. The paper emphasizes that baseline metrics are averaged only over their successful subsets, creating a survivor bias. Implementation runs on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 with differentiable rendering via PyTorch3D; sequences are subsampled with stride 5, HO3D processing is capped at 1024 frames per sequence, and ablations are conducted on HO3D. For the real-to-sim application, reconstructed sequences are imported into Isaac Gym and human hand motion is retargeted to a Shadow-style dexterous robotic hand via kinematic optimization (AnyTeleop).

## Results

On DexYCB, AGILE achieves MPJPE 19.06 mm, CD 0.52 cm^2, F@5mm 83.21%, F@10mm 95.43%, CDh 94.60 cm^2, and SR 100%, versus MagicHOI (21.20 mm, 2.05 cm^2, 45.67%, 67.14%, 661.90 cm^2, SR 25%) and HOLD (30.86 mm, 19.30 cm^2, 33.20%, 54.94%, 170.9 cm^2, SR 45%), with the baseline figures biased toward their easy successful subsets; MagicHOI's very high CDh indicates the object frequently drifts away from the hand. On HO3D-v3, AGILE reports MPJPE 3.92 mm, CD 0.27 cm^2, F@5mm 86.63%, F@10mm 97.77%, CDh 15.81 cm^2, SR 100%, versus MagicHOI (7.38 mm, 0.90 cm^2, 76.74%, 91.59%, 21.81 cm^2, SR 83.3%) and HOLD (22.09 mm, 1.11 cm^2, 81.75%, 92.42%, 18.66 cm^2, SR 100%). On the ARCTIC rigid-object subset, AGILE attains MPJPE_l 25.0 mm, MPJPE_r 23.8 mm, CDo 1.12 cm^2, F@5 57.6%, CDl 21.9 cm^2, CDr 30.6 cm^2, outperforming both HOLD and BIGS on most metrics. Ablations on HO3D show that removing the agentic multi-view generation collapses object geometry (CD rises from 0.27 to 2.85 cm^2, F@5 from 86.63% to 30.12%), that replacing the VLM keyframe selector with a max-mask heuristic (CD 2.04) or dropping multi-view synthesis (CD 2.61) both degrade substantially, that skipping texture refinement raises CDh from 15.81 to 20.18 cm^2 because FoundationPose initialization and the DINO loss rely on sharp textures, and that removing Linteract spikes CDh to 54.40 cm^2 due to object sliding and interpenetration; removing Ljoint, Lmask, or Ldino each degrades their respective metrics (e.g., MPJPE 5.06 mm without Ljoint, CD 0.40 cm^2 without Lmask). Runtime on an RTX 4090 for a representative 200-frame processed sequence is approximately 2.1-2.2 hours total (preprocessing 21-29 minutes including agentic generation of 16-24 minutes, optimization about 1.75 hours, roughly 30-50 seconds per frame), compared to about 25.1 hours for HOLD and 2.4 hours for MagicHOI. In the real-to-sim experiment, the generated watertight meshes load into the simulator without manual cleanup and retargeted grasps closely follow the recovered hand-object configurations via purely kinematic mapping, though the demonstration is kinematic visualization rather than full physics-based rollout.

## Limitations

The framework inherits limitations of the underlying foundation models for depth, segmentation, and multi-view generation, so extreme cases such as transparent or highly reflective objects may induce scale drift or local tracking errors. The static camera assumption is strictly limited to automatic interaction-onset-frame detection (mask-displacement heuristics), not the core tracking, which operates in the camera frame and remains valid under camera motion as long as a valid IOF is identified; for dynamic cameras the authors suggest replacing IOF selection with VLM-based action recognition, contact predictors, or manual specification, and recovering per-frame camera pose with SLAM tools such as MegaSaM. The method targets rigid objects; extending agentic generation to articulated and deformable categories, using VLMs as a reconstructibility pre-filter, and using multiple anchors for bi-directional propagation against long-sequence drift are left to future work. The real-to-sim validation is limited to kinematic retargeting without contact-dynamics rollout or policy learning.

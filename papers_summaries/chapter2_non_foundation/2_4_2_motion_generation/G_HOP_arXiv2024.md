# G-HOP: Generative Hand-Object Prior for Interaction Reconstruction and Grasp Synthesis

## Summary

G-HOP learns a category-conditioned denoising diffusion prior over the joint 3D distribution of hands and objects, representing both in a homogeneous spatial format ("interaction grids" that concatenate a latent object signed distance field with a skeletal distance field of the hand). Trained by aggregating seven real-world interaction datasets spanning 155 object categories, the prior can directly generate plausible hand-object configurations and, via score distillation, serve as generic guidance for two other tasks: reconstructing 3D hand-object shape from monocular interaction clips (beating DiffHOI on HOI4D with F@5mm of 0.76 versus 0.62 while running about 85% faster) and synthesizing human grasps for arbitrary object meshes (preferred over GraspTTA and even ground truth in user studies).

## Background and Problem

Humans can effortlessly envision how their hand would configure around everyday objects — a cylindrical bottle, a flat knife — with the interaction depending on geometry and intent. The paper aims to build a computational system with this capability: a generative model capturing the joint distribution p(O, H|C) of 3D object shape O and hand configuration H conditioned on the object category C, e.g., "a hand holding a plate." A key representational obstacle is that objects are conventionally described by spatial signed distance fields while hands are modeled as parametric meshes controlled by articulation vectors, and diffusing these heterogeneous representations together is problematic because the model cannot easily reason about spatial interactions between a 1D pose vector and a 3D grid. Beyond generation, the authors target using the learned prior to aid inference for two well-studied tasks — reconstructing 3D hand-object shapes from everyday interaction clips and synthesizing plausible human grasps given an arbitrary object mesh — motivated by robots learning from humans. Real-world 3D interaction data is sparse, motivating aggregation of multiple datasets, and the work is positioned as the first generative model that jointly generates both hand and object.

## Method

The core representation is the interaction grid: a concatenation of (i) a latent signed distance field for the object, obtained by compressing 64^3 SDF grids with a VQ-VAE to 16^3 resolution (z = E(O), O = D(z)), and (ii) a 15-channel skeletal distance field H(theta) for the hand, encoding for each grid cell the squared distance to each of the 15 hand joints. Both are expressed in a normalized hand-centric frame with the palm facing up, which captures interaction structures common across objects (e.g., grasping handles) regardless of object identity. The skeletal field is differentiably convertible to and from MANO pose parameters: MANO produces joint positions to build the field, and pose parameters are recovered by gradient descent minimizing the field difference plus an L2 regularizer. A denoising diffusion model Psi, implemented as a 3D-UNet with three 3D convolution blocks, is trained on noisy interaction grids to restore the clean grid, with the text prompt encoded by a CLIP text encoder and injected via cross-attention at each block. For downstream tasks, inference is framed as test-time optimization combining task-specific objectives with score distillation sampling (SDS), which corrupts the current interaction grid, denoises it with the diffusion model, and uses the discrepancy as an approximate log-likelihood gradient. For reconstruction, the paper follows DiffHOI's scene decomposition (a time-persistent object SDF as an implicit network, time-varying hand pose, and their relative transformation, differentiably rendered into 2D masks) but replaces DiffHOI's 2D prior, which models only p(object | hand) and cannot guide hand pose, with the joint 3D prior, optimizing mask reprojection loss plus eikonal and temporal-smoothness regularization for 15,000 iterations per clip (about an hour, 85% faster than DiffHOI). For grasp synthesis, a grasp is parameterized by hand articulation and relative hand-object pose; the object mesh is converted to an SDF grid, transformed into the hand-centric frame, and the grasp parameters are optimized for 500 iterations by maximizing the interaction likelihood, followed by refinement against the original mesh to encourage surface contact and penalize collision. A stochastic sampling procedure yields diverse grasps, and a ranking score approximating a likelihood upper bound (averaging SDS loss across timesteps) measures each grasp's agreement with the diffusion model.

## Contributions

1. The first generative model that jointly generates 3D hand and object across diverse categories, conditioned on category text, enabled by the homogeneous interaction-grid representation (latent object SDF plus skeletal distance field) that makes spatial hand-object co-diffusion tractable.
2. A single learned prior reusable as generic test-time guidance for multiple downstream tasks — video-based HOI reconstruction and human grasp synthesis — through score distillation, rather than task-specific architectures.
3. A training aggregation of seven real-world interaction datasets (including ContactPose, DexYCB, GANHand, HOI4D, GRAB, and OakInk) normalized to 155 object classes, producing a long-tailed prior that still generates plausible samples for few-shot classes.
4. A stochastic grasp synthesis pipeline with a likelihood-based ranking mechanism that meaningfully separates plausible from implausible sampled grasps.

## Experimental Setup

The diffusion prior is trained on 3D hand and object meshes from seven real-world datasets, with category names manually normalized (362 raw words reduced to 155 classes); SDF grids are computed at 64^3 resolution spanning 30 cm in a hand-centric frame. Training data is extremely long-tailed: the most common classes (e.g., mug) have more than 10k grasps while few-shot classes (e.g., skillet lid) have fewer than 100. Reconstruction is evaluated on HOI4D using the same split as DiffHOI (2 clips for all portable rigid object categories, with test objects held out from training), reporting object F-score at 5 mm and 10 mm and Chamfer distance after scaled-ICP alignment, hand-object alignment via Chamfer distance in the hand-centric frame (CDh), and hand error via MPJPE and AUC. Baselines are iHOI (single-view reconstruction, finetuned on HOI4D, evaluated per frame), HHOR (video optimization without data-driven prior), DiffHOI (2D conditional diffusion prior with per-sequence optimization), and G-HOP (Cond), an ablated 3D model conditioned on hand pose like DiffHOI; for fair comparison, the diffusion model for the HOI4D evaluation trains only on the HOI4D train split. Grasp synthesis is evaluated on HO3D (real YCB objects, in-domain) and on a synthetic 3D Warehouse subset (3DW, from Hasson et al., never seen in training), measuring mesh intersection (max/mean depth and volume), object displacement when placed in PyBullet simulation, and hand contact ratio and area, against GraspTTA (trained in-domain on 3DW with annotated grasps) and dataset ground truth; a user study collected 440 and 380 pairwise preference answers from 22 and 19 users on HO3D and 3DW respectively.

## Results

On HOI4D reconstruction, G-HOP outperforms all baselines: object F@5mm 0.76 and F@10mm 0.97 (versus 0.62/0.91 for DiffHOI, 0.42/0.70 for iHOI, 0.31/0.55 for HHOR), Chamfer distance 0.4 (versus 0.8), hand-object alignment CDh 18.4 (versus 48.7 for DiffHOI and 165.4 for HHOR), MPJPE 1.05, and AUC 0.79. Qualitatively it captures details other methods miss, such as the concavity of a mug and the space between a kettle handle and body, and hand pose reconstruction also improves because the prior guides the hand as well. Ablations show that upgrading the prior from 2D to 3D improves object reconstruction substantially (G-HOP Cond: F@5mm 0.66, CDh 19.3), while joint modeling of hand and object further improves hand pose (MPJPE 1.05 versus 1.14), which in turn benefits object reconstruction; a variant jointly modeling HOI in image space performs even worse than DiffHOI. For grasp synthesis on HO3D, G-HOP achieves the lowest mean intersection depth (0.31 versus 0.37 for GT and 0.61 for GraspTTA), the lowest simulation displacement (0.95 versus 2.32 and 2.89), a perfect contact ratio (1.00), and the largest contact area (0.23, tied with GraspTTA), at the cost of higher intersection volume (11.46 versus 6.16 for GT and 5.25 for GraspTTA) — reflecting a contact/stability versus penetration trade-off that no single metric resolves. On out-of-domain 3DW objects, G-HOP still attains the lowest max and mean depth (0.74/0.51) and largest contact area (0.25), though displacement (1.85) trails GraspTTA (1.54). Contact maps show G-HOP distributes contact over fingers and palm, consistent with human grasping, whereas GraspTTA concentrates at fingertips and even the back-of-hand nail region. In the user study, G-HOP is preferred over all methods on both datasets, including ground truth. Grasp diversity is comparable between methods in the object-centric frame, though GraspTTA's diversity sometimes includes implausible modes that disregard object function, and the proposed ranking score yields consistent orderings: top-10% grasps reduce displacement to 0.71 and intersection volume to 10.57 versus 0.95 and 11.46 overall.

## Limitations

The method requires object category information as input, which may prevent further scaling. There is no explicit mechanism to guarantee hand-object contact, and generated grasps exhibit higher intersection volume than ground truth and GraspTTA on both benchmarks. The model's scale remains far below generative models in other domains because of limited available 3D interaction training data. Additionally, on unseen out-of-domain objects (3DW), simulation displacement is worse than the in-domain-trained GraspTTA baseline, and the grasp synthesis comparison with Grasping Fields is only reported in a modified evaluation setting because their setup assumes a known object pose relative to the hand, which randomization in this paper's protocol substantially degrades.

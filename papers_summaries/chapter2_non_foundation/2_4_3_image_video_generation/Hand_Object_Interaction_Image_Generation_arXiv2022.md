**Authors:** Hezhen Hu, Weilun Wang, Wengang Zhou, Houqiang Li  
**Date:** 2022-11-28  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2211.15663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15663)

## Summary

The paper introduces Hand-Object Interaction image Generation (HOIG), a new conditional generation task that produces an image of a hand interacting with an object under a target hand-object posture while preserving the source image's appearance — the inverse of hand-object pose estimation. The proposed HOGAN framework conditions on model-aware MANO/YCB mesh representations, unwraps their shared topology into a unified surface space where self- and mutual occlusion are explicitly located, and synthesizes the final image through separate hand, object, and background streams combined by learned fusion masks. On HO3Dv3 and DexYCB it clearly beats baselines adapted from single-hand generation (e.g., FID 41.3 versus 45.6, and user preference of 74.7% versus 24.8% on HO3Dv3), and generated images can augment training data for hand-object pose estimation.

## Background and Problem

Hand-object interaction research has been dominated by pose estimation (capturing hand and object pose from an image), while its generative inverse had not been explored. The paper defines HOIG: given a source image of a hand-object interaction, an object model with known texture, a source posture, and a target posture (hand and object 3D mesh poses), generate the interacting hand-object image under the target posture while preserving the source appearance. Potential applications include AR/VR games, online shopping (immersively previewing customized products), and data augmentation. Two main challenges distinguish HOIG from prior pose-guided image synthesis of bodies, faces, or isolated hands (e.g., GestureGAN): first, the interacting hand and object are co-occurring instances with complex self-occlusion (hand joints hiding each other) and mutual occlusion (hand and object hiding each other), which increases transformation complexity and requires locating occluded regions; second, the two instances have different appearance characteristics — an articulated hand versus a rigid, finely textured object — so a single translation pipeline is inadequate. The object is assumed well-modeled with known texture.

## Method

HOGAN has two stages. In occlusion-aware topology modeling, the hand (MANO) and object (YCB) triangulated meshes form a joint model with Nv vertices and Nf faces whose pre-defined topology is unraveled into a unified surface space binding representation to mesh faces independently of pose. Source image appearance is mapped into this space by a flow Tu<-s built from per-face indices and barycentric weights, and an occlusion map flags pixels where the source and unified-space face indices disagree, so only visible texture is warped into the unified space Iu; occluded object regions are replaced with the pre-stored object texture to form a complete texture image. A second flow Tt<-u samples this texture under the target pose to produce a coarse target image, together with a fine-grained topology map computed from face barycenters. In hand-object synthesis, a generator with three streams processes the instances separately: a Background Stream inpaints the background cropped from the source image using the hand-object foreground mask; an Object Stream styles the rendered object into the real-image domain with the object topology map injected via spatially-adaptive normalization (SPADE); and a Hand Stream warps the visible hand from the source image with the pose transformation flow and refines it through a U-Net with SPADE-modulated hand topology. Multi-scale features from a generator that reconstructs the source image are integrated into refinement through an attention sampler. Finally, two convolutional layers predict a hand mask and a hand-object foreground mask, and a fusion module composes the three stream outputs (unoccluded hand, object, background) into the final image. Training combines an adversarial loss against a posture-conditioned discriminator, an L1 reconstruction loss on the source image, and a VGG-19 perceptual loss (weights 10 and 10).

## Contributions

1. First exploration of the hand-object interaction image generation task: conditionally generating an interacting hand-object image under given hand, object, and interaction status, with analysis of its challenges and application value.
2. The HOGAN framework, which leverages model-aware hand-object mesh representations and their inherent topology in a unified surface space, explicitly models self- and mutual occlusion, and synthesizes the target image in a split-and-combine manner that respects the different characteristics of hand and object.
3. Comparison baselines constructed by adapting representative single-hand generation methods (GestureGAN and MG2T) to include object conditions, plus a comprehensive evaluation protocol covering fidelity (FID, LPIPS), structure preservation (AUC, PA-MPJPE, ADD-0.1D via an off-the-shelf pose estimator), and subjective user study.

## Experimental Setup

Experiments run in PyTorch on 4 NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs; the networks are trained end-to-end and simultaneously for 30 epochs with Adam, batch size 8, learning rate 2e-4 decaying linearly to 2e-6. Evaluation uses two large-scale datasets with annotated hand-object meshes: HO3Dv3 (real-world captures of 10 subjects manipulating 10 YCB objects; 58,148 training and 13,938 test images) and DexYCB (controlled environment, 10 subjects and 20 objects; frames containing hand-object interaction only, giving 33,562 training and 8,554 test images). Fidelity is measured with FID and LPIPS. Structure preservation is assessed by running an off-the-shelf hand-object pose estimator (Liu et al.) on generated images and comparing to the target annotation: AUC (area under the 3D PCK curve) and PA-MPJPE for hand pose, ADD-0.1D (percentage of frames with mean object vertex error within 10% of object diameter) for object pose, reported per object category on HO3Dv3 (sugar box, bottle, banana, mug, power drill). A user study with 20 volunteers selects the most high-fidelity result among methods, recorded as User Preference Ratio (UPR). Baselines are GestureGAN + OBJ (cycle-consistent single-hand translation, with the rendered object concatenated to the target posture) and MG2T + OBJ (model-aware single-hand generation extended with object posture in the foreground branch); direct rendering-based methods are deemed inapplicable because the source image provides only partial hand texture.

## Results

On fidelity, HOGAN surpasses both baselines on both datasets: on HO3Dv3 it reaches FID 41.3 and LPIPS 0.171 versus 45.6/0.214 for MG2T + OBJ and 82.0/0.316 for GestureGAN + OBJ, and on DexYCB FID 30.1 and LPIPS 0.109 versus 37.8/0.121 and 34.5/0.214. In the user study, HOGAN achieves an UPR of 74.7% on HO3Dv3 and 74.0% on DexYCB, versus 24.8/22.0 for MG2T + OBJ and 0.5/4.0 for GestureGAN + OBJ. For structure preservation on HO3Dv3, HOGAN attains hand AUC 76.5 and PA-MPJPE 11.8 (close to ground truth at 76.9/11.6) and an average ADD-0.1D of 50.5 across the five object categories (bottle 85.0, mug 55.0, sugar box 52.1, power drill 34.1, banana 26.3), versus 36.5 average for MG2T + OBJ and 4.5 for GestureGAN + OBJ. Qualitatively, HOGAN produces images with reasonable spatial hand-object relationships in highly interacting scenes, avoiding the blurriness, texture aliasing, and false interactions of the baselines. Ablations on HO3Dv3 confirm each design choice: removing hand or object topology degrades FID to 47.3/47.8 and LPIPS to 0.183/0.187; replacing the attention-based source feature transfer with a bilinear sampler raises FID to 42.5 and removing transfer entirely to 46.5; and removing the split-and-combine strategy (using a parameter-comparable joint generator) worsens FID to 44.2 and LPIPS to 0.207. Two applications are demonstrated: object texture editing, where characters written on the object texture ("NeurIPS", "2022") are preserved in the generated image, and real-world generation, where a pre-trained HOGAN applied to a real hand photo preserves hand identity under the target posture. As a data augmentation use case, training the hand-object pose estimation backbone of Liu et al. on HO3D plus synthesized images improves hand AUC from 77.2 to 78.0 and object ADD-0.1D from 67.6 to 76.8.

## Limitations

The dense mesh representation used as the pose condition inevitably contains misalignment with the RGB image plane even when manually annotated; this disturbs coarse image synthesis, and the framework currently relies on the hand-object synthesis stage for refinement, with a more accurate hand-object pose estimation method suggested as an alternative mitigation. The authors also outline open directions: exploring more suitable condition representations with robustness analysis, extending applications to further fertilize the task, and scaling from hand-object to broader human-object interaction generation. Evaluation of occlusion handling relies on the chosen pose-estimation-based metrics, which inherit that estimator's errors, though the paper does not report this as an explicit limitation.

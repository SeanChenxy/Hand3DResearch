## Summary

SNS-Grasp is an AAAI-26 framework for intent-based grasp generation (e.g., "use" versus "hold" a mug) that replaces the isotropic noise of standard diffusion models with anisotropic, joint-specific noise scaling driven by manipulation semantics. A lightweight Intent Recognizer (0.414M parameters, 98.7% classification accuracy) provides per-joint gradient sensitivities and task-confidence scores; during denoising, semantically critical joints receive noise scaled by 1/(1+gamma) (approaching zero for intent-critical joints such as fingertips when using a camera), while non-critical joints keep full noise for kinematic diversity. A Fine-grained Grasp Refinement (FGR) module then couples each MANO joint to all 778 mesh vertices via SDF-guided cross-attention, overcoming MANO's fixed skinning weights, and iteratively optimizes physical feasibility over three iterations. On OakInk seen categories, SNS-Grasp achieves 0.446 cm penetration depth, 3.306 cm3 intersection volume, 1.411 cm simulation displacement, 97.39% intent controllability, and an 82.06% user preference rate, beating GrabNet, AffordPose-injected GrabNet, and RAGG on all five metrics, with similar gains on unseen OakInk categories and cross-dataset GRAB evaluation.

## Background and Problem

Intent-based grasp generation synthesizes human-like hand poses that fulfill a specific manipulation intent, with applications in human-computer interaction, virtual reality, and robotics. Diffusion-based generators have become the dominant approach thanks to stable training and diverse samples, but the authors identify two failures of their isotropic noise schedules: (1) the biomechanical hierarchy of the MANO hand requires differentiated control across joints, with distal joints (fingertips) demanding finer control than proximal joints (wrist) to preserve manipulation semantics; and (2) different interaction purposes, such as exploratory holding versus constrained manipulation (e.g., holding a camera body versus pressing its button), require task-adaptive noise profiles. A motivating experiment with the RAGG diffusion backbone compares four noise configurations (isotropic scale 1, weak isotropic 0.1, joint-indexed increasing 0 to 1, and decreasing 1 to 0) across six affordance-aware categories (Use/Hold Mug/Bottle/Camera): the joint-specific 0-to-1 scaling beats isotropic scale 1 by 14.63% on Use Bottle, and the best configuration differs per task (0-to-1 for Use Camera and Hold Bottle, 1-to-0 for Hold Mug). A second motivation is physical feasibility: prior refinement methods optimize MANO parameters with vertex-object signed distance field (SDF) features, but MANO's fixed skinning weights limit deformation adaptability, causing semantic drift and residual penetration. The paper thus targets semantic alignment and physical feasibility jointly.

## Method

The framework has two components. SNS-Diff first runs a standard denoising pass from x_T ~ N(0, I) to obtain an exploratory grasp proposal, then restarts denoising from x_T with Semantic-guided Noise Scaling (SNS). The pretrained Intent Recognizer, a lightweight mixer network consuming the concatenation of the MANO hand mesh (778 vertices, from theta in R16x6 joint rotations and 3-d translation) and a 4,096-point object point cloud (4874x3 in total), predicts the manipulation category with a softmax head; its per-joint gradient magnitude of the intent cross-entropy loss and the logit confidence of the ground-truth intent class define a joint-specific, task-aware criticality coefficient gamma_j = |dL_int/dtheta_j|^2 * sigma(z) >= 0. Each joint's initial noise is rescaled as x^SNS_{j,T} = x_{j,T} * 1/(1 + gamma_j) in (0, 1], so critical joints receive near-zero perturbation (variance provably bounded by C/(1+gamma_j)^2 under the DDPM cosine schedule) while non-critical joints retain natural diversity. The denoiser conditions on the diffusion timestep (sinusoidal, R256), the one-hot intent via a trainable 16-d lookup table (R256), and 4096-d Basis Point Set object features compressed to R256; the concatenated 3x256 conditioning matrix drives stacked Semantic Calibration Transformer (SCT) blocks from RAGG, followed by a two-layer residual MLP that predicts clean MANO parameters. Training minimizes mesh reconstruction (vertex and edge-length losses, weights 34.825 and 29.85) plus hand-to-object and object-to-hand contact/penetration losses (34.825 and 29.85).

The FGR module refines the coarse grasp over three iterations: per-vertex signed distances to the object surface (778x1) are computed; an SCT-based cross-attention treats joint parameters as queries and SDF features as keys/values, producing dynamic joint-vertex coupling weights (17 x d) so that all 778 vertices adaptively respond to the 16 joints, replacing MANO's rigid linear blend skinning; and three residual KAN (ResKAN) blocks with skip concatenations fuse pose, SDF, and coupling features to output updated MANO parameters, progressively reducing penetration while preserving manipulation semantics.

## Contributions

1. SNS-Diff, the first anisotropic noise scheduling for grasp generation that adapts noise per joint and per task using intent-recognizer gradients and confidence, with a theoretical variance bound showing reduced output variance for semantically critical joints.
2. A motivating empirical study of four noise scaling modes across six affordance-aware categories demonstrating that isotropic noise is suboptimal and that the ideal noise profile is both joint-specific and task-dependent.
3. The FGR module, which establishes dynamic joint-vertex coupling through SDF-guided cross-attention and iterative ResKAN refinement, overcoming the fixed skinning-weight limitation of MANO-based refinement pipelines.
4. Extensive validation on OakInk (seen and held-out unseen categories) and GRAB (cross-dataset, subject-held-out) showing state-of-the-art semantic controllability and physical feasibility against GrabNet, AffordPose-injected GrabNet, and RAGG.

## Experimental Setup

Experiments follow the RAGG protocol. On OakInk (1,800 objects, 32 categories), 9 representative categories are used with two functional intents each (use and hold): bottles, cameras, cylinder bottles, eyeglasses, game controllers, lotion pumps, mugs, pens, and trigger sprayers; 8 additional categories (headphones, bowls, cups, hammers, frying pans, scissors, wine glasses, binoculars, teapots) are held out as unseen tests. GRAB (10 subjects, 51 objects) is used for out-of-domain evaluation on objects manipulated by subject S1 in pass actions, comprising categories overlapping OakInk (camera, mug) and novel ones (wineglass, toothpaste). Metrics cover physical feasibility (penetration depth, solid intersection volume, simulation displacement) and semantic controllability (intent recognition accuracy with the pretrained classifier, reported as Auc., and a web-based user preference study where 10 participants choose preferred samples over 50 random trials). Baselines are GrabNet, AffordPose (intent-injected GrabNet variants), and RAGG (AAAI 2025 retrieval-augmented grasp generation by the same group), all evaluated under the same protocol.

## Results

On OakInk seen categories, SNS-Grasp attains penetration depth 0.446 +/- 0.467 cm, intersection volume 3.306 +/- 7.682 cm3, displacement 1.411 +/- 1.382 cm, controllability 97.39%, and user preference 82.06%, versus RAGG (0.467 cm, 3.467 cm3, 1.565 cm, 92.24%, 62.73%), AffordPose (0.499 cm, 4.309 cm3, 1.945 cm, 91.04%, 34.96%), and GrabNet (0.527 cm, 5.016 cm3, 1.784 cm, 89.50%, 23.96%). On OakInk unseen categories, it reaches 0.459 cm penetration depth, 1.979 cm displacement, 89.27% controllability, and 67.50% user preference; GrabNet achieves a better intersection volume (6.228 versus 6.933 cm3), which the authors attribute to its simpler, semantically unconstrained grasps, evidenced by GrabNet's much lower controllability (67.80% versus 89.27%). On GRAB cross-domain objects, SNS-Grasp wins all metrics with 0.343 cm penetration depth, 1.734 cm3 intersection volume, 1.652 cm displacement, 73.44% controllability, and 84.46% user preference, the displacement being 23.8% lower than RAGG's. Ablations on OakInk show that plain diffusion generation without SNS yields 0.608 cm penetration and 93.38% controllability; gradient-only scaling (SNS1) gives 0.592 cm; a sigmoid variant that increases noise for critical joints (SNS2) reaches 96.48% controllability; and the full 1/(1+gamma) scaling (SNS3) is best with 0.568 cm, 4.820 cm3, and 97.74%. For refinement, GrabNet's refinement network and RAGG's ProKAN achieve penetration depths of 0.454 cm and 0.444 cm but displacements of 2.273 cm and 1.699 cm with controllability dropping to 95.47% and 95.60%, whereas FGR keeps 0.446 cm penetration while cutting displacement to 1.411 cm and preserving 97.39% controllability. Qualitative analyses visualize intent-recognizer gradients (index and ring fingertips dominate "use camera", the thumb fingertip dominates "hold camera"), attention maps that shift from finger-contact vertices ("use mug") to broad palmar regions ("hold mug"), and t-SNE plots with compact intra-class clusters and clear inter-class separation.

## Limitations

The paper contains no explicit limitations section. Grounded observations: on OakInk unseen categories the intersection volume (6.933 cm3) remains worse than GrabNet's 6.228 cm3, an acknowledged trade-off between functional precision and minimal intersection; in the refinement ablation FGR's displacement (1.411 cm) is higher than the unrefined SNS-Diff output (1.154 cm), indicating refinement improves penetration and semantics at some stability cost. The controllability metric is measured with the authors' own pretrained intent recognizer (98.7% accuracy, an imperfect oracle), and the user study aggregates preferences from only 10 participants over 50 trials. The pipeline also requires a pretrained intent recognizer plus a two-pass denoising procedure (exploratory pass, then SNS-restarted denoising) followed by three refinement iterations, and its semantic scope is bounded by the two intents (use, hold) and the 9+8 OakInk categories and S1 GRAB objects evaluated.

## Summary

The paper introduces CHOIR (Coarse Hand-Object Interaction Representation), a versatile, continuous, and fully differentiable field for hand-object interaction that encodes the object geometry as unsigned distances from a fixed Basis Point Set (BPS) grid, the hand pose and shape as distances from the same basis points to 32 pre-assigned MANO mesh anchors, and the dense contact map compactly as 32 fitted 3D multivariate Gaussian distributions. On top of it, JointDiffusion, a conditional denoising diffusion model with a 3D U-Net that jointly decodes the distance field and contact Gaussians from a shared latent space, serves both as a grasp refiner (conditioned on a noisy hand-object pair) and a grasp synthesizer (conditioned only on the object geometry), followed by a two-stage gradient-descent test-time optimization (TTO) that fits a MANO mesh to the field. On the Perturbed ContactPose refinement benchmark, JointDiffusion raises the contact F1 score by 5 percentage points over TOCH (27.20 versus 22.23 percent) and 10 over ContactOpt (17.27 percent), achieves the lowest intersection volume (6.04 cubic centimeters) and best root-aligned pose error (23.54 mm), while for synthesis it reduces simulation displacement by 46 percent and intersection volume by 13 percent relative to GraspTTA, showing that one representation plus one model family can replace task-specific interaction fields.

## Background and Problem

Accurate hand-object interaction (HOI) reconstruction and generation matters for computer vision, AR/MR, and human-robot collaboration, but state-of-the-art models still fail to generalize to novel grasps on unknown objects, and small errors — subtle penetrations or slightly misplaced fingers — strongly degrade realism. The dominant coarse-to-fine paradigm predicts a coarse hand pose from images and then refines it with test-time optimization against dense contact or interaction fields. Existing fields, however, are limited: ContactOpt predicts dense contact maps that require feature engineering and maximize hand-object contact; CPF's coarse anchors with a spring system involve intricate energy functions that are slow at test time and cannot handle non-full or dynamic grasps; the Grasping Field's SDF takes whole hand and object point clouds, making it high-dimensional; UV-based reconstruction is image-centric and uninformative for fingers approaching but not yet touching the object; and TOCH, the current state of the art for refinement, is not fully differentiable, needs a search algorithm and random restarts, and imposes a lower bound on optimization time, having been designed for dynamic grasps. The problem addressed is therefore to design a single coarse interaction field that is fully differentiable, continuous, scalable, cheap to evaluate on GPUs, informative before contact occurs, and usable across both refinement and synthesis tasks.

## Method

CHOIR represents a hand-object pair in the object's canonical frame as the concatenation of three parts. First, the object geometry is encoded with the BPS representation: M basis points on a regular grid record squared distances to the normalized object point cloud. Second, hand pose and shape are encoded as squared distances from the same basis points to 32 MANO anchors pre-assigned on the hand mesh surface (following CPF); the anchor-to-basis-point assignment is a repeating pattern of the 32 ordered indices (a shuffled variant performed equivalently in the appendix). Third, contacts are probabilistic: raw per-vertex contact weights are computed by counting object surface points lying within a 4 mm radius and inside a cone of tolerance (half-angle defined by kappa = 4*pi/3) along each vertex normal — inspired by ContactOpt's contact capsules but excluding object vertices inside the hand mesh, so that penetration patches are not rewarded as contact; one 3D multivariate Gaussian per MANO anchor is then maximum-likelihood fitted to the weighted vertex multiset (each vertex repeated by its weight), giving a 32x9 parameter vector (Gaussian means plus Cholesky-factorized covariances), from which any vertex's contact probability is queried from the nearest anchor's PDF. Fitting MANO meshes to ground-truth CHOIRs yields less than 1 mm absolute mean per-joint pose error, so representation fidelity is not the bottleneck. JointDiffusion is a DDPM conditioned on an observation y: either a noisy CHOIR without contacts (refinement) or the object's BPS encoding alone (synthesis). Its backbone is a 3D U-Net with multi-head self-attention and multi-head cross-attention conditioning that predicts the noise for the distance field, while a second fully connected residual decoder, fed by the latent variable and the U-Net bottleneck features, predicts the noise for the contact Gaussian parameters, encouraging shared pose-contact features; the context is embedded by an encoder identical to the U-Net encoder plus shallow spatial pooling. One model is trained per context modality (a multimodal variant is studied in the appendix). TTO proceeds in two gradient-descent stages with Adam. Stage one fits a MANO mesh to the predicted unsigned distance field by minimizing a reconstruction loss on anchor distances (weighted 1000) plus shape and pose regularizers (weights 1e-4 and 1e-8; the pose regularizer is dropped for synthesis), initialized from the noisy input in the refinement setting, converging in roughly 150 iterations. Stage two refines contacts by minimizing the probability-weighted squared distances from each MANO vertex to its K = 5 nearest object points, together with a ContactOpt-style penetration regularizer (weight 1000), a pose regularizer relative to the stage-one solution (eta1 = 1e-2, eta2 = 1e-1, weight 0.5), and the shape regularizer (weight 10 for the reconstruction term) — continuous losses that give a smoother landscape than contact-agreement objectives and require neither search nor random restarts.

## Contributions

- CHOIR, a versatile and fully differentiable hand-object interaction field that encodes shape, pose, and contacts with discrete unsigned distances and is efficient on GPUs, removing the engineering burden, non-differentiability, and slow search of prior fields such as TOCH.
- A novel probabilistic contact representation: dense contact maps compressed into 32 multivariate Gaussian distributions fitted to cone-of-tolerance contact weights, plus a simple scheme for computing contact weights for all hand vertices that avoids encoding penetration as contact.
- JointDiffusion, a multimodal conditional diffusion model tailored to CHOIR that handles both grasp synthesis and noisy grasp refinement with the same architecture by changing the conditioning, with joint decoding of pose distances and contact parameters from a shared latent space.
- Empirical evidence that the single CHOIR + JointDiffusion + TTO stack beats task-specific state-of-the-art methods on both refinement (Perturbed ContactPose) and synthesis (ContactPose) in contact accuracy and physical plausibility.

## Experimental Setup

Grasp refinement replicates the benchmark of Grady et al. (ContactOpt): a perturbed version of ContactPose (static grasps of 25 objects by 50 participants) with additive i.i.d. noise — translation N(0, 5 cm), pose N(0, 0.05) in MANO PCA space, and rotation N(0, 15) in radians. Because ContactOpt's protocol omits a validation split, the authors re-split the data into 70 percent training, 10 percent validation, and 20 percent testing, using 16 perturbed versions of each training sample and 4 for validation and testing; ContactOpt and TOCH (the state of the art for dynamic-grasp refinement, retrained here since it had not been evaluated on static grasps with this noise level) are retrained on the new splits, and all methods are evaluated with one non-cherry-picked output per sample, with additional object-split experiments for generalizability in the appendix. Grasp synthesis trains JointDiffusion on ContactPose conditioned on the object's BPS encoding and compares against a retrained GraspTTA, a recent synthesis method that also uses test-time adaptation, on the same splits. Metrics are MPJPE and root-aligned R-MPJPE (mm) for pose; intersection volume (IV, cubic centimeters); contact Precision, Recall, and F1 (F1 being the most meaningful for contact fidelity, since Recall can be inflated by maximizing contact); and simulation displacement (SD) with IV for synthesis stability. In the appendix, a multimodal variant is also evaluated on OakInk against GrabNet. The appendix further documents the metric definitions and the keypoint diffusion baseline used in the ablation.

## Results

- Grasp refinement on Perturbed ContactPose: JointDiffusion reaches contact F1 27.20 percent, Precision 25.21 percent, Recall 32.80 percent, MPJPE 27.69 mm, R-MPJPE 23.54 mm, and IV 6.04 cubic centimeters. This is a 5 percent F1 gain over TOCH (22.23) and 10 percent over ContactOpt (17.27), the best R-MPJPE (4.6 mm better than ContactOpt's 28.17), the highest precision (3.8 percent over TOCH), and the lowest intersection volume (about 4 cubic centimeters less than TOCH's 10.14); MPJPE is second, within 1 mm of TOCH's 26.96, and ContactOpt retains a 2 percent Recall advantage because it maximizes hand-object contact at the cost of 12 percent worse precision and a 10 percent lower F1. The perturbed inputs themselves score 83.02 mm MPJPE and 1.55 percent F1.
- Grasp synthesis on ContactPose: JointDiffusion with contact-fitting TTO enabled achieves IV 4.51 cubic centimeters and SD 2.05 cm versus GraspTTA's 5.17 and 3.81 — 13 percent lower intersection volume and 46 percent lower simulation displacement; without the contact-fitting stage it records IV 8.13 and SD 2.07, still ahead of GraspTTA on SD.
- Ablation on Perturbed ContactPose: a keypoint diffusion baseline yields MPJPE 22.11 mm, IV 9.18, F1 19.45 percent; adding BPS pose/shape encoding (+BPS) trades 2.58 mm MPJPE for better contacts (F1 20.77, Recall 23.38); adding the probabilistic contacts (+BPS +Contacts, the full CHOIR) improves all contact and penetration metrics (F1 27.20, IV 6.04) at the cost of a higher MPJPE of 27.69 mm, which the authors attribute to the second TTO stage re-aligning the hand to satisfy predicted contacts — CHOIR thus gives the best pose-accuracy/plausibility compromise.
- Qualitatively, JointDiffusion produces less penetration than TOCH and substantially better grasps than ContactOpt, whose contact-maximizing objective fails on a challenging case; synthesized grasps show consistent finger-object contact with minimal penetration.

## Limitations

The paper explicitly lists two limitations. Reliance on the fixed BPS representation may limit the ability to capture detailed, fine-grained interactions, and the method's focus on static interactions may restrict its applicability to real-world dynamic scenarios; the proposed future work is to investigate learnable geometry representations and to incorporate temporal information into CHOIR. Additional constraints evident from the evaluation: the pipeline requires test-time optimization after sampling (about 150 iterations in the first stage plus a second contact stage), so it is not single-pass real-time; each context modality uses a separately trained diffusion model (a unified multimodal model is only studied in the appendix); and the refinement benchmark evaluates static grasps with synthetic noise, so performance on genuine image-based reconstruction noise is not directly measured in the main experiments.

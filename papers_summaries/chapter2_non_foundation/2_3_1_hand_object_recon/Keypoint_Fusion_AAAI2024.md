## Summary

Keypoint-Fusion estimates 3D hand pose from paired RGB and depth images by restricting cross-modal fusion to sparse yet informative spatial regions — the keypoints (joints) — instead of fusing dense pixel-wise features, which the authors argue causes redundant interactions over invalid pixels (empty voxels, background) and lets visual-feature ambiguity (color/texture similarity, noise from frequent hand movement) corrupt data-driven fusion. A KeyPoint Feature Aggregation Module (KFAM) aggregates RGB and depth local features around initially predicted joints and iteratively refines the hand pose: during RGB aggregation, a per-joint 3D geometric structure representation built from depth clarifies the ambiguous weights of color-similar RGB pixels, while during depth aggregation, RGB features projected into 3D point cloud space supply the edge information lost to depth holes and noise. The aggregated keypoint features then interact through a single Transformer encoder for intra-modal (depth) long-range correlation followed by a Transformer decoder-encoder for inter-modal fusion. On DexYCB, Keypoint-Fusion reports MPJPE 6.94/8.64/7.56/7.02 mm on the four official splits S0-S3 (average 7.54 mm, PA-MPJPE 4.79 mm), an average MPJPE reduction of 13.6% (from 8.03 mm to 6.94 mm) over IPNet on the default S0 split, and on HO-3D it reports 1.79 cm MPJPE before and 1.87 cm after scale-translation alignment on the root joint, the best among compared methods in both settings. Code is available at github.com/TrueVen/Keypoint-Fusion.

## Background and Problem

3D hand pose estimation is critical for interactive media and human-computer interaction, and augmented-reality applications involve hand-object interactions with frequent movements and occlusions, demanding higher accuracy in 3D than in 2D. Single-modal methods have complementary weaknesses: RGB-based methods are prone to color similarity and lack local geometric information, while depth-based methods suffer from noise and depth holes at the edges of hands and objects, especially during motion (Figure 1). RGB data provide rich texture and accurate edge information while depth data contain detailed geometric structure features, so fusion should alleviate individual modality defects; however, multimodal fusion — extensively studied for RGB-D semantic segmentation and LiDAR-camera 3D object detection (e.g., TransFusion, AutoAlign, CMX) — has not been widely explored for 3D hand pose estimation. The paper identifies two problems in applying existing fusion methods. First, redundancy of dense feature fusion: pixel-wise interactions contain redundant interactions of many invalid pixels, incurring high computational costs and ineffective calculations, and prior sparse schemes built on deformable-attention offsets around sampling points capture pixel-level offsets but struggle with joint-level feature dependencies, although long-range joint correlation is proven crucial in 3D hand pose estimation (Lin, Wang, and Liu 2021a,b). Second, ambiguity of visual features: frequent movement, high degrees of freedom, and frequent object interactions cause severe feature ambiguity (color and texture similarity, noise), and pixel-wise weighted aggregation or cross-attention fusion is inherently data-driven, so low-quality features interfere with modeling cross-modal correlations. The keypoint representation used in prior 3D hand pose estimation (Hampali et al. 2022) represents potential joint locations and unveils more informative feature regions, motivating fusion at these sparse regions plus explicitly extracting relatively reliable per-modality information as robust disambiguation evidence.

## Method

Given RGB and depth inputs, two parallel 2D CNN backbones (ResNet-18, He et al. 2016) extract RGB visual features F_2d^RGB ∈ R^{H×W×C} and depth visual features F_2d^D ∈ R^{H×W×C}; a 2D heatmap H_2d ∈ R^{H×W×J} and the initial 3D hand pose J_init ∈ R^{J×3} are predicted from the depth feature map through weighted average regression (Huang et al. 2020c). The two KFAMs individually aggregate RGB and depth local features around each joint and iteratively update the hand pose, so later stages aggregate and fuse based on more accurate joint positions.

In RGB keypoint feature aggregation, a learnable weighted operation jointly uses 2D heatmaps and geometric structure. Concatenating the 2D heatmap and RGB features along the channel dimension and applying N 1×1 convolution kernels generates the heatmap-based weight map W_hm ∈ R^{H×W×J} = φ([F_2d^RGB || H_2d]) (N = number of hand joints, φ = convolution layer); during the first few training epochs, the ground-truth 2D heatmap supervises its generation. Separately, the depth image is downsampled to the RGB feature-map size and converted to real-world coordinates through camera intrinsics, giving coordinates (x_p, y_p, z_p) of each RGB feature pixel; from the 3D joint coordinates (x_J, y_J, z_J) of J_init, the geometry adjacency map W_geo ∈ R^{H×W×J} is computed as the inverse of γ times the squared 3D Euclidean distance between each feature pixel and joint (Eq. 2), where γ controls how fast the joint-pixel correlation decays with distance and is set to 10. The RGB keypoint feature K_RGB ∈ R^{J×C} is aggregated as FC(α(W_hm + (1 − α)W_geo) F_2d^RGB) (Eq. 3) with a learnable α balancing the two maps.

In depth keypoint feature aggregation, the 2D image representation loses 3D geometric structure, so the paper adopts IPNet's 2D-3D projection module (Ren et al. 2023) to generate point cloud features F_3d^d ∈ R^{(H×W)×C}. Because depth data are prone to noise and holes at hand and object edges during motion and cannot compensate via intra-modal fusion alone, the RGB features are projected into 3D point cloud space through the real-world coordinates and fused with the point cloud features: K_D ∈ R^{J×C} = FA(ReLU(BN(W_0 F_proj^RGB + W_1 F_3d^D))) (Eq. 4), where FA is IPNet's local feature aggregation module and W_0, W_1 are learnable matrices.

For cross-modal interaction, prior Transformer-based 3D hand pose methods either perform intensive dense interaction (e.g., HandOccNet) or reduce dimension step by step with multi-layer Transformer encoders (e.g., METRO); to avoid high computational and global-scale attention, interaction is performed sparsely at the joint level. Given the serial arrangement of the RGB-D KFAMs, the depth keypoint feature K_D ∈ R^{J×C} first undergoes intra-modal interaction: a single Transformer encoder performs self-attention over keypoints and an FC layer estimates the refined hand pose J_refined. In inter-modal interaction, K_RGB ∈ R^{J×C} is the query Q and the intra-modal-correlated depth feature K_D^P ∈ R^{J×C} the Key K and Value V: a Transformer decoder cross-attends to fuse the RGB keypoint feature with the geometry-rich depth keypoint feature, a Transformer encoder then self-attends to model non-local dependencies of the fused keypoints, and an FC layer on the fused feature estimates the final hand pose J_fuse.

## Contributions

- A sparse RGB-D fusion approach for 3D hand pose estimation that performs cross-modal feature interaction and fusion in a more efficient way.
- A cross-modal information guidance strategy that excavates the unique advantages of complementary modalities to clarify intra-modal ambiguous information before cross-modal feature interaction.
- Experiments showing leading performance on two challenging datasets, DexYCB and HO-3D, significantly outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods.

## Experimental Setup

DexYCB is a hand-object dataset captured by multiple RGB-D cameras, containing 582K RGB-D frames over 1,000 sequences of 10 subjects grasping 20 objects from 8 views, with four official train/val/test splits S0-S3 split by sequences, subjects, views, and objects respectively; comparisons cover all four splits and ablations use the default S0 split. HO-3D is an RGB-D hand-object interaction dataset containing 66,034 training images and 11,524 test images from 68 sequences, captured in multi-camera and single-camera setups with 10 subjects manipulating 10 YCB objects; evaluation of the HO-3D test set is conducted at an online submission server. Experiments use an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU with PyTorch and the AdamW optimizer (initial learning rate 8e-4); training takes 15 epochs on DexYCB and 25 on HO-3D. Data augmentation crops input RGB-D images to 128×128 with random rotation ∈ [−180, 180], random scaling ∈ [0.9, 1.1], and random translating ∈ [−10, 10]. The evaluation metric is Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE); for the HO-3D comparison, two ConvNeXt (Liu et al. 2022b) are adopted as the 2D backbones, while the 2D CNN baseline in the fusion-stage ablation uses ResNet-18. Code is available at github.com/TrueVen/Keypoint-Fusion.

## Results

- DexYCB (Table 1, MPJPE mm on S0/S1/S2/S3/average, plus PA-MPJPE): Keypoint-Fusion 6.94/8.64/7.56/7.02/7.54 with PA-MPJPE 4.79, the lowest MPJPE on all four official splits and the best PA-MPJPE on S0. Comparisons: IPNet (depth + point cloud) 8.03/9.01/8.60/7.80/8.36; SA-Fusion 9.51 (S0); DiffHand 12.10 (S0, PA-MPJPE 4.98); RGB-only HandOccNet 14.04 (S0, PA-MPJPE 5.80), METRO 15.24 (S0, PA-MPJPE 6.99), Liu et al. 15.28 (S0, PA-MPJPE 6.58), Tse et al. 16.05/21.22/27.01/17.93/20.55, Spurr et al. 17.34/22.26/25.49/18.44/20.88 (PA-MPJPE 6.83); depth-only AWR 11.23 (S0), A2J 23.93/25.57/27.65/24.92/25.52. Stated average MPJPE reduction versus IPNet: 13.6% (from 8.03 mm to 6.94 mm) on the default S0 split.
- HO-3D (Table 2, MPJPE in cm before / after scale-translation alignment on the root joint): Keypoint-Fusion 1.79/1.87, the best both before and after alignment, versus IPNet 1.81/2.01, HandVoxNet++ 2.46/-, HandOccNet 2.49/2.40, DiffHand 2.57/-, I2L-MeshNet 2.68/2.60, Keypoint Transformer 2.57 (aligned), Zheng et al. 2.51 (aligned), ArtiBoost 2.53 (aligned), METRO 2.89/2.89, Liu et al. 3.00/3.17, Hampali et al. 3.04 (aligned), and Hasson et al. 5.52/3.18.
- Number of iterative fusion stages (Table 3, DexYCB): depth 2D CNN baseline (ResNet-18 backbone, Huang et al. 2020c) 11.23 mm; 1-stage Keypoint-Fusion 7.23 mm (a 4.0 mm reduction from one fusion stage); 2 stages 6.94 mm; 3 stages 6.89 mm — more stages further improve performance.
- Efficiency (Table 4, DexYCB): 1-stage Keypoint-Fusion 12.47 ms with 7.23 mm MPJPE, versus SA-Fusion 7.54 ms / 9.51 mm, a dense fusion baseline (KFAMs ablated, dense cross-modal interaction) 16.19 ms / 9.76 mm, and IPNet 19.91 ms / 8.03 mm; the authors conclude the method achieves significant performance gains with acceptable additional inference time over RGB-D image-based fusion, higher efficiency and effectiveness than IPNet via cross-modal fusion in 3D space, and more efficient interaction than dense fusion.
- KFAM design ablation (Table 5, DexYCB): baseline 11.23 mm; RGB KFAM alone (ID 1, heatmap-based weight map plus geometry adjacency map) 7.54 mm; depth KFAM alone (ID 4, point cloud feature, projected RGB feature, pose refinement) 7.09 mm; full model (ID 10) 6.94 mm. Discarding RGB feature fusion (ID 2, 7.17 mm) or hand pose refinement (ID 3, 7.11 mm) in the depth feature aggregation degrades performance; removing the geometry adjacency map (ID 5, 6.97 mm) or heatmap-based weight map (ID 6, 6.96 mm) from RGB aggregation also hurts; without the local RGB feature complement in the depth KFAM, the gap among RGB KFAM designs widens (IDs 7-9: 8.76/7.19/7.27 mm).
- Visualization (Figure 4): the heatmap-based weight map focuses on relevant spatial regions around joints based on color features, while the geometry adjacency map provides local geometric structure information distinguishing joints from invalid pixels (backgrounds, objects in contact, other hand regions); it suppresses features that are visually adjacent but far from the joint in 3D, and where relative joint distance is hard to determine from a single RGB image (wrist and index tip), it pinpoints relevant feature regions.
- Qualitative results: on DexYCB (Figure 5) the method handles noise and depth holes (rows 1, 3, 4), motion blur (row 2), and severe occlusion (rows 5-7) better than IPNet, predicting visually blurred joints and unseen joints more accurately; on HO-3D (Figure 6), where ground-truth test positions are not provided, the output pose estimates joints occluded during object interaction more accurately than the initial pose.

## Limitations

The paper contains no dedicated limitations section, so only constraints stated in the paper itself can be cited. The heatmap-based weight map relies on ground-truth 2D heatmap supervision during the first few training epochs; the geometry adjacency map is computed from depth data downsampled and converted to real-world coordinates through camera intrinsics, tying the RGB aggregation to the availability and quality of calibrated depth; the distance-decay factor γ is an empirical constant set to 10; and evaluation is restricted to joint-position metrics (MPJPE and PA-MPJPE on DexYCB, MPJPE before and after scale-translation alignment on HO-3D), with no mesh or shape accuracy reported. The efficiency comparison uses the 1-stage variant (12.47 ms), and the paper does not report inference time for the multi-stage configurations whose accuracy is highest.

## Summary

WiLoR is, per the authors, the first full-stack framework for in-the-wild multi-hand 3D reconstruction, combining a real-time fully convolutional hand localization network with a high-fidelity transformer-based MANO reconstruction model. To train the detector they introduce WHIM, a new dataset of over 2 million in-the-wild hand images automatically annotated from more than 1,400 YouTube videos via an ensemble of detectors and model fitting with biomechanical and PCA pose priors. The anchor-free, DarkNet/PANet-based detector reaches 175 FPS (small) or 138 FPS (medium) on an RTX 4090 — 45x faster and 32x smaller than the previous state of the art ContactHands — while improving mAP by about 26 percent on average across COCO-WholeBody, OxfordHands, and WHIM. The reconstruction model is a pre-trained ViT encoder with learnable pose, shape, and camera tokens that first regresses coarse MANO parameters, then refines them with residuals predicted from image-aligned per-vertex features sampled from multi-scale deconvolution-upsampled feature maps; it sets state of the art on FreiHAND (PA-MPJPE 5.5 mm, PA-MPVPE 5.1 mm, F@5 0.825) and HO3Dv2 (PA-MPJPE 7.5 mm, AUCJ 0.851), and, without any temporal module, achieves by far the smoothest frame-wise video reconstruction (frame-to-frame vertex error 4.43 versus 10.60 for HaMeR; jitter 5.92 versus 20.43), which the authors attribute to stable detections.

## Background and Problem

Hand pose estimation is driven by applications in VR, sign language, robotics, and behavior recognition, but existing 3D estimators assume a fixed number of hands and operate on tight crops, so they cannot generalize to in-the-wild images. In contrast to body and face reconstruction, whose state-of-the-art pipelines rest on high-performance detectors, real-time hand detection has lagged: classic detectors such as MediaPipe and OpenPose fail on multiple hands and challenging poses, while recent accurate methods like ContactHands and ViTDet run at only 3 and 1 FPS, which would dominate the runtime of any downstream pose estimation pipeline. A root cause is the lack of large-scale in-the-wild training data with multiple hands, occlusions, motion blur, and diverse lighting and skin tones. On the reconstruction side, single-image regression of MANO parameters (even at the scale of HaMeR's 0.5-billion-parameter ViT) yields poor image alignment and incorrect poses, and prior alignment solutions rely on sub-optimal intermediate heatmap representations; frame-wise methods also suffer from jitter across video frames. WiLoR therefore targets robust real-time multi-hand detection, accurate image-aligned 3D reconstruction, and stable 4D hand tracking from monocular video without temporal components.

## Method

The WHIM dataset is collected from over 1,400 YouTube videos covering sign language, cooking, everyday activities, sports, and games from ego- and exo-centric viewpoints. Humans are first detected with ViTPose and AlphaPose (boxes with confidence above 0.65), and crops are passed to an ensemble of MediaPipe, OpenPose, and ContactHands; the hand bounding box is the confidence-weighted average of the three detectors' boxes. The estimated 2D landmarks are used to fit the MANO model by minimizing an L1 reprojection loss under weak-perspective projection, augmented with biomechanical constraints (bone-length and joint-angle losses following Spurr et al.) and a PCA pose prior trained on the ARCTIC dataset that penalizes the reconstruction error of the mesh projected through the eigenvector basis. The detector is a one-stage, anchor-free, fully convolutional backbone-neck-head network built on the DarkNet backbone: the last three feature maps feed a Path Aggregation Network (a bottom-up-augmented FPN), and three detection heads predict bounding boxes, left/right hand side labels, and hand keypoints at different resolutions, the keypoint supervision (as in RetinaFace) improving robustness. Training minimizes binary cross-entropy for box labels, distributional focal loss and CIoU for boxes, and an L2 keypoint loss; augmentations include random rotations in [-60, 60] degrees, translations in [-0.1, 0.1], random masking and cropping, and mosaic and mixup. The reconstruction network takes a tight hand crop, splits it into patch tokens, and adds positional embeddings plus three learnable tokens for pose, shape, and camera; a ViT encoder pre-trained with ViTPose weights processes all tokens, and MLP heads regress coarse MANO pose (48), shape (10), and weak-perspective camera parameters from the respective tokens. The multi-scale pose refinement module then improves alignment: the updated image tokens are reshaped into a low-resolution feature map and upsampled through deconvolutional layers into multi-resolution maps, the coarse hand mesh (not just joints) is projected onto these maps with the estimated camera, per-vertex features are sampled by bilinear interpolation, aggregated into a global feature vector, and mapped by MLPs to pose and shape residuals; low-resolution features contribute global structural shape corrections while high-resolution features contribute fine pose detail. Training is supervised with L1 losses on 3D vertices and reprojected 2D joints, L2 losses on MANO parameters where available, and an adversarial discriminator loss (following HMR) to enforce plausible poses.

## Contributions

- WHIM, a large-scale in-the-wild dataset of over 2M images with multiple hands and diverse poses, illuminations, occlusions, and skin tones, automatically annotated through a detector ensemble with MANO fitting under biomechanical and PCA-prior constraints, released together with 2D and 3D annotations and a challenging hand-detection benchmark.
- A real-time anchor-free fully convolutional hand detector that outperforms previous hand detection methods by a large margin in both accuracy and efficiency (45x faster and 32x smaller than ContactHands) and is lightweight enough for mobile use.
- A transformer-based 3D hand reconstruction method with a novel fully differentiable multi-scale refinement module that samples mesh-aligned image features and predicts MANO parameter residuals, addressing the misalignment of direct single-pass regression while keeping poses explainable and plausible.
- State-of-the-art reconstruction accuracy on FreiHAND and HO3Dv2, and a demonstration that precise, stable localization substantially improves frame-wise 3D tracking (reduced jitter) without temporal modeling.

## Experimental Setup

Detection is evaluated on COCO-WholeBody, OxfordHands, and the proposed WHIM dataset on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, reporting model size (MB), FPS, AP at IoU 0.5, and mAP at IoU 0.5:0.05:0.95, against MediaPipe, OpenPose, ContactHands, and ViTDet. Ablations on OxfordHands and WHIM vary the training-set size (0.25M, 0.5M, 1M images), the training dataset (WHIM versus OxfordHands only), the backbone (ResNet50 and HRNet versus DarkNet), and the augmentation strategy and landmark loss. The reconstruction regressor is trained on a combination of FreiHAND, HO3D, MTC, RHD, InterHand2.6M, H2O3D, DexYCB, COCO WholeBody, Halpe, MPII NZSL, BEDLAM, ARCTIC, Re:InterHand, and Hot3D — 4.2M images in total, 55 percent more than the previous state of the art. Reconstruction is evaluated on FreiHAND and HO3Dv2 under the standard protocols with PA-MPJPE and PA-MPVPE (mm), F@5 and F@15 (fractions of poses under 5 mm and 15 mm error), and AUC for joints and vertices (AUCJ, AUCV) on HO3D, against I2L-MeshNet, Pose2Mesh, I2UV-HandNet, METRO, Tang et al., Mesh Graphormer, MobRecon, AMVUR, HaMeR, HandOccNet, Hampali et al., Hasson et al., ArtiBoost, Keypoint Transformer, and SimpleHand. Reconstruction ablations on FreiHAND swap the backbone for FastViT, drop the ViTPose pre-training, use single-scale refinement, remove refinement entirely, and restrict training data to FreiHAND only or to HaMeR's dataset mix. For dynamic evaluation, a 4D HO3D sequence is reconstructed frame-wise and temporal coherence is measured by mean per-frame vertex and joint Euclidean distance between consecutive frames (MPFVE, MPFJE), jerk of the 3D joint motion, and root translation error (RTE) of the wrist.

## Results

- Hand detection: Proposed-M (25 MB, 138 FPS) reaches AP50/mAP of 62.48/25.97 on COCO-WholeBody, 82.64/48.98 on OxfordHands, and 96.06/53.79 on WHIM; Proposed-S (7 MB, 175 FPS) reaches 46.96/18.56, 75.21/38.16, and 91.80/46.50. ContactHands (819 MB, 3 FPS) and ViTDet (1400 MB, 1 FPS) are an order of magnitude slower and larger; MediaPipe and OpenPose are far less accurate (e.g., OpenPose mAP 9.06 on COCO-WholeBody versus 25.97).
- Detection ablations: training with ResNet50 or HRNet backbones gives similar accuracy (HRNet 84.82/49.83 AP50/mAP on OxfordHands versus 82.64/48.98 for the DarkNet model) but at 34 and 30 FPS versus 138 FPS; shrinking WHIM to 1M, 0.5M, or 0.25M images degrades OxfordHands mAP to 43.04, 35.15, and 26.69; training on OxfordHands instead of WHIM drops WHIM AP50 from 96.06 to 70.14; removing the augmentation strategy costs 14 percent mAP, and removing the landmark loss also lowers precision.
- FreiHAND reconstruction: PA-MPJPE 5.5 mm, PA-MPVPE 5.1 mm, F@5 0.825, F@15 0.993, ahead of HaMeR (6.0/5.7/0.785/0.990), MobRecon (5.7/5.8/0.784/0.986), and Mesh Graphormer (5.9/6.0/0.764/0.986).
- HO3Dv2 reconstruction: AUCJ 0.851, PA-MPJPE 7.5 mm, AUCV 0.846, PA-MPVPE 7.7 mm, F@5 0.646, F@15 0.983, ahead of HaMeR (0.846/7.7/0.841/7.9/0.635/0.980) and AMVUR (0.835/8.3/0.836/8.2/0.608/0.965).
- Reconstruction ablations (FreiHAND): replacing the ViT backbone with FastViT degrades performance to 6.5/6.3/0.741/0.967; training the backbone from scratch without ViTPose pre-training gives 5.9/5.7/0.795/0.989; removing the refinement module gives 6.1/5.8 and single-scale refinement 6.0/5.9, versus 5.5/5.1 for the full multi-scale module; training only on FreiHAND gives 6.1/5.8/0.793/0.990 and on HaMeR's dataset mix 5.9/5.7/0.805/0.992, confirming the value of the enlarged training set.
- Dynamic reconstruction on HO3D: WiLoR attains MPFVE 4.43 (x100), MPFJE 0.762 (x100), Jitter 5.92, and RTE 0.07, versus HaMeR's 10.60, 1.768, 20.43, and 2.92, and far ahead of MeshGraphormer (21.86/4.99/41.16/7.92), MobRecon, and SimpleHand — achieved without any temporal module, which the authors attribute to the stability of the detector's bounding boxes.

## Limitations

The paper has no dedicated limitations section. Several constraints are evident from the methodology. WHIM's 2D and 3D annotations are produced automatically by an ensemble of detectors and model fitting, so the training signal is pseudo-ground-truth whose accuracy is bounded by the annotation pipeline. The reconstruction model processes each detected hand independently and per frame; despite the markedly improved temporal coherence reported on HO3D, no explicit temporal modeling is used, and residual jitter is not zero. Runtime figures are reported on a high-end RTX 4090 GPU. The reconstruction model targets single hands, with multi-hand behavior emerging from the detector plus per-hand reconstruction rather than from a joint two-hand model, and hand-object interaction is not explicitly modeled in the reconstruction stage.

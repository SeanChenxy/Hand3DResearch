# DDF-HO: Hand-Held Object Reconstruction via Conditional Directed Distance Field

**Authors:** Chenyangguang Zhang, Yan Di, Ruida Zhang, Guangyao Zhai, Fabian Manhardt, Federico Tombari, Xiangyang Ji  
**Date:** 2023-08-16  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2308.08231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.08231)

## Summary

DDF-HO reconstructs the 3D shape of an object held by a human hand from a single RGB image, replacing the Signed Distance Field (SDF) used by prior template-free and category-agnostic methods with a Directed Distance Field (DDF) that maps a 3D ray — an origin plus a direction — to a binary visibility signal and a distance to the first intersection with the object. Because rays enclose hand and object meshes jointly and features are aggregated along the projection line, the method captures hand-object interaction cues in 3D where SDF pipelines rely on surface patches or global hand-pose embeddings alone. The pipeline combines off-the-shelf pose estimation, a 2D ray-based feature aggregation scheme built on cross-attention, a 3D intersection-aware hand pose embedding, and a symmetry loss from bidirectional ray pairs, all consumed by an 8-layer MLP. On the synthetic ObMan benchmark DDF-HO reaches F-5 0.55, F-10 0.67, and Chamfer Distance 0.14 versus IHOI's 0.42/0.63/1.02 — an 85% CD reduction — and on real-world data it reports 0.28/0.42/0.52 on HO3D(v2) and 0.16/0.22/1.59 on MOW, consistently ahead of all baselines, at 44 FPS on one A100 GPU.

## Background and Problem

Hand-held object reconstruction creates a 3D model of the grasped object without object templates or depth input. The dominant shape representation, SDF, brings two core challenges. First, SDF is an undirected function: a sampled point's feature must be aggregated from a local patch centered at its image projection, but when the sampled point is far from the object surface, that patch may contain only background and no information about the point's nearest surface neighbor, so SDF-based methods either encode hand pose as a global cue (IHOI) or propagate hand-object information in 2D feature space (AlignsDF), failing to model interactions in 3D space. Second, SDF cannot naturally encapsulate the inherent symmetry characteristics of objects: most man-made objects exhibit (partial) symmetry, and the SDF of heavily self-occluded regions loses the information needed to recover high-quality shape. DDF, previously proposed as an extension of SDF, conditions on direction: a ray with origin P and direction θ is visible if it intersects the object, and the DDF maps it to the non-negative distance from the origin to that first intersection, plus a binary visibility field ξ(P, θ).

## Method

Given an RGB image, an off-the-shelf framework predicts MANO hand articulation θ_H (45D parameters) and camera pose θ_c (rotation R and translation t), and a ResNet34 image encoder extracts a hierarchical visual feature pyramid. 3D rays with origins P in a bounding volume B and directions θ on the sphere are transformed into a normalized wrist frame defined by the predicted hand pose, as in IHOI; during training 20K rays are sampled per object and ground-truth DDF values are generated by ray marching in Trimesh. For each ray, three feature sources are collected. (1) 2D ray-based feature aggregation: the ray origin is projected to the image via intrinsics, a second point on the ray yields a projected 2D ray, K_i = 8 points are sampled along that 2D ray, and local patch features are bilinearly interpolated from the hierarchical feature maps for the K_i points and the origin projection; a multi-head cross-attention with 2 heads then aggregates them, F_2D = F^0_2D + MultiH(F^0_2D, F^1_2D, F^1_2D) with the origin feature as query. (2) 3D intersection-aware hand embedding F^c_3D: the shortest path from the ray to the MANO hand skeleton yields a start point P^S on the ray and an endpoint P^D on the skeleton; the K_3D = 8 nearest neighboring hand joints of P^D are found, and P^S is expressed in the local coordinates of detected hand joints using geodesic distance and concatenated. (3) A global hand pose embedding F^g_3D following IHOI. An 8-layer MLP with positional encoding of the input (P, θ) predicts distance D and visibility ξ; the visibility output is taken after the 3rd layer to leave network capacity for the harder distance estimation, and a skip connection from the input ray to the 4th layer preserves low-level local geometry. The loss L = L_D + λ1·L_ξ + λ2·L_s combines a conditional distance field loss, a BCE visibility loss, and a symmetry loss |D̃1 − D1|: symmetric objects (checked by a 1e-3 Chamfer threshold after XY-plane flipping) must yield identical DDF predictions for two bijection sets of rays with identical origins on the reflective plane but opposite directions, handling self-occluded regions.

## Contributions

- DDF-HO, a hand-held object reconstruction pipeline that uses DDF as the shape representation, demonstrating superiority in modeling hand-object interactions over SDF-based competitors.
- A 2D ray-based feature aggregation scheme and a 3D intersection-aware hand pose embedding that together extract local-to-global features capturing hand-object relationships by combining 2D and 3D cues per sampled ray.
- Extensive experiments on synthetic (ObMan) and real-world (HO3D(v2), MOW) datasets showing consistent outperformance of competitors by a large margin, especially under Chamfer Distance.

## Experimental Setup

Three datasets are used. ObMan is synthetic: 2772 objects of 8 categories from ShapeNet, with 21K grasps generated by GraspIt and objects rendered over random backgrounds by Blender. HO3D(v2) contains 77,558 images from 68 sequences with 10 persons manipulating 10 YCB objects, pose annotations from multi-camera optimization pipelines, and the same training/testing splits as IHOI. MOW comprises 444 images and 121 object templates from in-the-wild hand-object interaction datasets, with approximate ground truths from a single-frame optimization method and splits identical to the released IHOI code. Predicted DDFs are converted to point clouds and compared against sampled ground-truth point clouds, reporting Chamfer Distance in mm (lower is better) and F-scores at 5 mm (F-5) and 10 mm (F-10) thresholds (higher is better). Baselines that predict hand-held object shape directly from a single RGB image without templates, category priors, or depth priors: Atlas-Net, HO, the implicit field GF, and SDF-based IHOI; both finetuned and zero-shot (direct inference from ObMan weights) settings are evaluated. Training uses one NVIDIA A100 40GB GPU with Adam, learning rate 1e-4 for 100 epochs end-to-end; ObMan weights initialize training on HO3D(v2) and MOW, which then finetune at learning rate 1e-5 for 100 and 10 epochs respectively. Loss weights are λ1 = 5.0 and λ2 = 0.5; the training loss omits the symmetry term L_s, which is set only in the ablation study.

## Results

- ObMan (Table 3): DDF-HO 0.55 F-5 / 0.67 F-10 / 0.14 CD versus IHOI 0.42/0.63/1.02, GF 0.30/0.51/1.39, and HO 0.23/0.56/0.64 — gains of 13% and 4% on F-5 and F-10 over IHOI, with CD reduced by 85% versus IHOI and 77% versus HO, indicating fewer outliers.
- HO3D(v2) with finetuning (Table 1, left): 0.28/0.42/0.52 versus IHOI 0.21/0.38/1.99, GF 0.09/0.21/5.22, HO 0.08/0.19/4.60; zero-shot generalization from ObMan (right): 0.24/0.36/0.73 versus IHOI 0.17/0.31/4.17, GF 0.07/0.16/6.25, HO 0.05/0.14/6.03 — versus IHOI this is F-5 up 7%, F-10 up 4%, CD down 73% finetuned and F-5 up 7%, F-10 up 5%, CD down 82% zero-shot.
- MOW with finetuning (Table 2, left): 0.16/0.22/1.59 versus IHOI 0.10/0.19/7.83 (F-5 up 6%, F-10 up 3%, CD down 80%); zero-shot from ObMan (right): 0.14/0.19/1.89 versus IHOI 0.09/0.17/8.43 — a synthetic-only model still achieves decent real-world performance.
- Efficiency (Section 4.4, single A100): 44 FPS versus IHOI's 172 FPS — slower because of the 2D aggregation attention and more elaborate 3D feature generation, but still real-time; model sizes are similar (24.5M versus 24.0M parameters).
- Ablations (Table 4): from the SDF baseline (IHOI, 0.42/0.63/1.02 on ObMan; 0.21/0.38/1.99 on HO3D(v2)), switching to DDF without new aggregation improves CD by nearly 80% (0.45/0.60/0.22 and 0.24/0.38/0.62) with a 3% F-5 gain on ObMan; adding ray-based feature aggregation (RFA), the intersection-aware hand feature (IHF), and the symmetry loss (SYM) in turn yields 0.50/0.64/0.17, 0.55/0.67/0.14, and 0.57/0.68/0.13 on ObMan (5%, 5%, and 2% F-5 gains) and 0.26/0.39/0.58, 0.28/0.42/0.55, and 0.29/0.43/0.52 on HO3D(v2).
- Input hand pose robustness (Table 5): on ObMan, Gaussian noise with σ = 0.1 to 1.5 added to predicted hand poses degrades 0.55/0.67/0.14 to 0.38/0.52/0.31, while ground-truth poses give 0.59/0.70/0.10; the same trend holds on HO3D(v2) (0.28/0.42/0.55 predicted versus 0.30/0.45/0.50 ground truth), read as robustness to noisy input hand poses.
- Qualitatively (Figures 6-7), IHOI produces surfaces with numerous outliers from novel views, whereas DDF-HO yields smooth and precise reconstructions from any viewpoint on both ObMan and MOW.

## Limitations

The paper states limitations explicitly. DDF-HO naturally inherits the shortcomings of DDF: first, the higher-dimensional input of DDF makes it harder to train than SDF, resulting in more complex data, algorithm, and network structure requirements, which may hinder the performance of DDF-based methods when scaling up to large-scale scenes like traffic scenes; second, to enable more photorealistic object reconstruction, other characteristics like transparency, material, and appearance need to be properly represented, which requires further research to fill the gap. It also runs slower than its SDF-based competitor (44 versus 172 FPS).

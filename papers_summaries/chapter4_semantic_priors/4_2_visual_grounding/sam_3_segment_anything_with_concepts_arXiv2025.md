## Summary

SAM 3 is a unified model from Meta that detects, segments, and tracks objects in images and videos based on concept prompts, defined as short noun phrases (e.g., "yellow school bus"), image exemplars, or a combination of both. The paper formalizes this as Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS): given a prompt, the model returns segmentation masks and unique identities for all matching object instances, generalizing the point/box/mask Promptable Visual Segmentation (PVS) of SAM and SAM 2 to open-vocabulary concepts. The system couples a DETR-style image detector with a memory-based video tracker that share a single Perception Encoder backbone, and introduces a presence head that decouples recognition from localization. A human- and model-in-the-loop data engine produced a training corpus with 4M unique concept labels including hard negatives, and the companion SA-Co benchmark covers 207K unique phrases. SAM 3 doubles the accuracy of existing systems on both image and video PCS and improves on SAM 2 for visual prompts, while remaining fully interactive through refinement prompts. Released with code and checkpoints in late 2025, it is a strong upstream segmentation prior for pipelines that need to localize all instances of a named category — for example hand and object regions in interaction imagery.

## Background and Problem

The original SAM introduced promptable segmentation for images, focusing on Promptable Visual Segmentation with points, boxes, or masks to segment a single object per prompt; SAM 2 extended this to videos. While these methods were a breakthrough, they did not address the general task of finding and segmenting all instances of a concept appearing anywhere in the input (e.g., all "cats" in a video). Prior open-vocabulary detection and segmentation methods (OWL-ViT, Minderer et al.; GroundingDINO; DINO-X; T-Rex2) leverage vision-language encoders, but the paper argues they fall short on the abstract concept of objects as text prompts, with concept vocabularies orders of magnitude smaller — SAM 3 introduces a benchmark with over 100x more unique concepts than prior work.

The PCS task is defined as follows: given an image or a short video (up to 30 seconds), detect, segment, and track all instances of a visual concept specified by a short text phrase, image exemplars, or both. Concepts are restricted to simple noun phrases (e.g., "red apple", "striped cat") plus optional modifiers; the model is not designed for long referring expressions or queries requiring reasoning, though it can be combined with a Multimodal Large Language Model for that purpose. The task is intrinsically ambiguous — polysemy ("mouse" device vs. animal), subjectivity ("cozy", "large"), brand identity, boundary ambiguity (does a mirror include its reflection?), and occlusion or blur — which the authors address through test annotations collected from three experts, an adapted evaluation protocol, and an ambiguity module in the model. All prompts must agree on a single category interpretation for behavior to be well defined.

## Method

SAM 3 is a generalization of SAM 2, supporting PCS alongside PVS with visual prompts (points, boxes, masks). The architecture consists of a dual encoder-decoder transformer — a detector for image-level capability and a tracker for video — used in combination, with both consuming vision-language inputs from an aligned Perception Encoder (PE) backbone (Bolya et al., 2025), chosen over Hierat and DINOv2 for its aligned image and text encoders.

The detector follows the DETR paradigm: image prompt tokens (text encoded by PE, plus exemplar tokens from a separate exemplar encoder) condition the image embeddings in a fusion encoder, and learned object queries in a DETR-like decoder cross-attend to the conditioned embeddings. Each decoder layer predicts a binary classification logit per query; the box head adopts a delta-from-bounding-box-position formulation, box-region positional bias focalizes attention, and training uses dual supervision from DAC-DETR and the Align loss. The mask head, adapted from MaskFormer, additionally yields a semantic segmentation head predicting a per-pixel binary label. Crucially, recognition and localization are decoupled with a learned global presence token: this token alone predicts whether the noun phrase is present in the frame, p(NP is present), while each proposal query only solves the localization problem, with the final object score being the product of the query score and the presence score — avoiding forcing localizing queries to interpret global contextual cues.

Image exemplars support interactivity: a bounding box with a binary (positive or negative) label is encoded with an embedding for position, the label, and RoI-pooled visual features, then concatenated with the text prompt; exemplars can be added iteratively on errors to refine output, generalizing from exemplars rather than only correcting single masks as in PVS.

For video, the detector finds new objects in each frame while the tracker (SAM 2-style, trained after freezing the PE backbone) propagates existing masklets frame-to-frame using a memory bank with self-attention across visual features and cross-attention into spatial memory features; a two-way transformer outputs three candidate masks per frame with a confidence selection. A simple IoU-based matching function associates propagated masklets with new detections; a temporal masklet detection score suppresses masklets whose match window falls below threshold, and the tracker is periodically re-prompted with high-confidence detector masks to resolve occlusions and distractors. Training proceeds in four stages: PE pretraining, detector pretraining, detector fine-tuning, and tracker training with the frozen backbone.

The data engine runs in four phases with AI annotators and verifiers (fine-tuned Llama 3.2 models performing Mask Verification and Exhaustivity Verification) alongside humans: Phase 1 collects 4.3M human-verified image-noun-phrase pairs; Phase 2 introduces AI verification, roughly doubling annotation throughput and adding 122M image-NP pairs; Phase 3 scales domain expansion across 15 datasets and mines a 22.4M-noun-phrase SA-Co ontology from Wikidata, iterating the model 7 times; Phase 4 extends to video, yielding SA-Co/VIDEO with 52.5K videos and 467K masklets. Final training data combines SA-Co/HQ (5.2M images annotated with 4M unique noun phrases — the largest high-quality open-vocabulary segmentation dataset), the synthetic SA-Co/SYN with instance masks, hard negatives, and ontology enrichment, and external SA-Co/EXT datasets.

## Contributions

1. The formalization of Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) — detecting, segmenting, and tracking all instances of a concept given short noun phrases, image exemplars, or both — together with the SA-Co benchmark of 207K unique phrases with exhaustive masks and hard negatives across images and videos.
2. An architecture that decouples recognition, localization, and tracking: a DETR-based detector with a presence head that separates "is the concept present" from "where is each instance", integrated with a SAM 2-style memory tracker sharing a single Perception Encoder backbone, extending SAM 2's capabilities to concept segmentation while preserving interactive visual prompting.
3. A scalable, four-phase data engine that curates diverse media, uses LLM-generated noun phrases and hard negatives, and doubles annotation throughput with AI annotators and near-human-accuracy AI verifiers, producing 4M unique concept labels and the largest high-quality open-vocabulary segmentation dataset.
4. State-of-the-art results that double the accuracy of prior systems on image and video PCS (e.g., zero-shot LVIS mask AP of 48.8 versus the previous best of 38.5), while improving over SAM 2 on promptable visual segmentation.
5. Open-source release of the model, checkpoints, and benchmark, with strong efficiency (about 30 ms per image with 100+ detected objects on an H200 GPU).

## Experimental Setup

Evaluation spans image and video PCS, promptable visual segmentation, few-shot adaptation, object counting, and complex-language queries via an MLLM agent. Image benchmarks: LVIS and COCO/COCO-O for box detection and instance segmentation, closed-vocabulary COCO-O and open-vocabulary SA-Co (Gold/Silver/Bronze/Bio splits), ADE-847, Pascal-Context-59, and Cityscapes for semantic segmentation, ODinW13 and RF100-VL for zero/10-shot transfer, CountBench and PixMo-Count for counting, and the SA-37 benchmark for interactive segmentation. Video benchmarks: SA-Co/VEval (SA-V with 2.0K noun phrases, YT-Temporal-1B with 4.7K, SmartGlasses with 2.4K) measured with cgF1 and pHOTA, plus public LVVIS, BURST, YT-VIS19, and OVIS. The SA-Co/Gold split carries three annotations per phrase to measure ambiguity (oracle accuracy) and estimated human performance (cgF1 72.8). The main metric, classification-gated F1 (cgF1 = 100 x pmF1 x IL_MCC), combines positive micro F1 on positive media-phrase pairs (localization) with image-level Matthews Correlation Coefficient (classification), evaluated only on predictions with confidence above 0.5. Baselines include OWLv2, GroundingDINO, LLMDet, APE-D, DINO-X, T-Rex2, Gemini 2.5 Pro, GLEE, and SAM 1/2. Inference latency is reported on an H200 GPU.

## Results

- Image PCS with text: SAM 3 sets a new state of the art with zero-shot LVIS mask AP 48.8 (previous best DINO-X 38.5, used a closed-set training set) and LVIS box AP 53.6 versus 52.4 for DINO-X; COCO AP 56.4. On open-vocabulary SA-Co/Gold it reaches cgF1 54.1 (Silver 49.6, Bronze 42.6, Bio 55.4) — more than double the strongest baseline OWLv2* (20.1) and 74% of estimated human performance (72.8). Semantic segmentation: mIoU 13.8 on ADE-847, 60.8 on PC-59, 65.2 on Cityscapes.
- Few-shot transfer: state-of-the-art 10-shot performance without any prompt tuning — bbox mAP of 61.0 on ODinW13 and 71.8 on RF100-VL, surpassing Gemini-2.5-Pro (11.6 / 9.8) and gDino-T (15.7 / 33.7).
- PCS with exemplars: with one image exemplar, SAM 3 outperforms state-of-the-art T-Rex2 by +18.3 AP(T+I) on COCO (78.1 vs. 59.8), +10.3 on LVIS (81.2 vs. 70.9), and +20.5 on ODinW13 (81.8 vs. 61.3). In an interactive setting, after 3 clicks interactive PCS beats text-only PCS by +21.6 cgF1 and pure PVS refinement by +20.0 cgF1, with gains plateauing after 4 clicks.
- Object counting: MAE of 0.12 / accuracy 93.8 on CountBench and MAE 0.21 / accuracy 86.2 on PixMo-Count, ahead of DINO-X, Qwen2-VL-7B, Molmo-72B, and Gemini 2.5 Pro, while also producing segmentation masks the MLLMs cannot.
- Video PCS: on SA-Co/VEval, cgF1 of 30.3 (SA-V), 50.8 (YT-Temporal-1B), and 36.4 (SmartGlasses) with pHOTA of 58.0, 69.9, and 63.6 — the latter over 80% of human pHOTA — far above GLEE and LLMDet baselines; on public benchmarks it reaches 36.3 test mAP on LVVIS, 44.5 on BURST, 57.4 on YT-VIS19, and 60.5 on OVIS.
- PVS: SAM 3 improves over SAM 2 on most video object segmentation benchmarks, notably MOSEv2 with J&F of 60.3 versus 47.9 for SAM 2.1-L (and 53.8 for the best prior method), DAVIS17 93.2, LVOSv2 88.5, SA-V test 84.4, and YT-VOS19 89.7. On interactive image segmentation (SA-37), it reaches 66.1 mIoU with one click and 85.1 with five clicks at 43.5 FPS.
- SAM 3 Agent (MLLM calling SAM 3 as a tool): zero-shot gIoU of 77.0 on ReasonSeg val and 74.0 on test with Gemini 2.5 Pro — surpassing fine-tuned specialists — and AP of 45.3 (descr) / 56.3 (descr-S) on OmniLabel 2023.
- Ablations: the presence head adds +1.5 cgF1 and +0.05 IL_MCC; adding hard negatives lifts IL_MCC from 0.44 to 0.68; synthetic (SYN) training data adds +8.8 cgF1 over external data alone and high-quality (HQ) annotations add +14.6 cgF1; AI verifiers (exhaustivity +7.2, mask verification +1.1 cgF1) close half the gap between SAM 3 and human performance. Synthetic data generated by SAM 3 plus AI verifiers matches human-annotation scaling behavior on a new domain without any human annotation. Latency: 30 ms per image with 100+ detected objects on an H200 GPU; video latency scales with object count, sustaining near-real-time performance for about 5 concurrent objects.

## Limitations

The authors state that SAM 3 struggles to generalize to fine-grained out-of-domain concepts in a zero-shot manner (e.g., aircraft types, medical terms), especially in niche visual domains such as thermal imagery; concept generalization for PCS is inherently more challenging than the class-agnostic generalization that enables SAM and SAM 2 to be successfully applied zero-shot. Performance can, however, be recovered by fine-tuning on small quantities of human-annotated data or, with no human involvement, by fine-tuning on domain-specific synthetic data produced by the data engine. The prompt vocabulary is restricted to simple noun phrases, so the model is not designed for long referring expressions or reasoning-heavy queries — those require composing SAM 3 with an MLLM (SAM 3 Agent), and all prompts must agree on one category interpretation, with ambiguity (polysemy, subjectivity, boundary cases) handled via curated vocabularies, expert-annotated evaluation, and a model ambiguity module rather than fully resolved.

## Summary

LISA (large Language Instructed Segmentation Assistant) introduces reasoning segmentation: given an input image and a complex, implicit query text — such as "the food with most Vitamin C" or "what can the driver observe?" — the model must reason about user intention and output the corresponding binary segmentation mask. The paper establishes the ReasonSeg benchmark of over one thousand image-instruction-mask samples with intricate reasoning and world knowledge, and presents LISA, a multimodal LLM (built on LLaVA) that inherits language generation capabilities while producing segmentation masks. The key mechanism is the embedding-as-mask paradigm: a new <SEG> token is added to the vocabulary, and its last-layer hidden embedding is decoded into the segmentation mask by a decoder in the manner of SAM, so segmentation is learned end-to-end within the LLM through the token's embedding rather than via parsing text or polygon sequences. Remarkably, LISA exhibits robust zero-shot capability on reasoning segmentation when trained exclusively on reasoning-free data (semantic segmentation, referring segmentation, and VQA), and fine-tuning with only 239 reasoning-segmentation samples yields further gains. Published at CVPR 2024, it is a widely used upstream model for language-prompted, reasoning-based localization of regions — for example isolating interaction-relevant hand or object regions in HOI pipelines from implicit textual cues.

## Background and Problem

Users naturally issue implicit instructions ("find the TV and press the button to change the channel") rather than explicit object references, yet existing perception systems consistently rely on explicit human instruction or pre-defined categories to identify targets before executing visual recognition. Semantic, instance, and panoptic segmentation have matured but interact through fixed task interfaces; referring segmentation segments a target from an explicit text description, and SAM achieves exceptional segmentation quality with geometric prompts but does not interpret language intent. Multimodal LLMs have integrated strong reasoning with visual input (BLIP-2, mPLUG-OWL, Otter, LLaVA, MiniGPT-4, VisionLLM, DetGPT, GPT4RoI), but these efforts focus primarily on text generation and do not produce fine-grained output formats such as masks; VisionLLM's polygon-sequence formulation requires 4x8 NVIDIA 80G A100 GPUs and 50 epochs for a 7B model, which is computationally prohibitive. The paper thus defines the reasoning segmentation task — outputting a binary mask M given an image and an implicit query — whose key distinction from referring segmentation is query complexity: queries involve complex reasoning or world knowledge (e.g., "the garbage should be put into") rather than a straightforward noun phrase, demanding both reasoning capability and mask production ability.

## Method

LISA is built on a multimodal LLM (LLaVA-7B-v1.1 or LLaVA-13B-v1.1, or LLaVA v1.5 variants) with ViT-H SAM as the vision backbone. The embedding-as-mask pipeline works as follows: the LLM vocabulary is expanded with a <SEG> token that signifies the request for segmentation. Given text instruction and image, the model autoregressively generates a text response that may contain <SEG>; when it does, the LLM's last-layer embedding h_seg at the <SEG> position is projected by an MLP (channels 256-4096-4096), fused with the dense visual features f extracted by the SAM encoder, and decoded by the SAM-style decoder F_dec into the final mask M: h_seg = gamma(h_emb), M = F_dec(h_seg, f). Because segmentation is conditioned on the hidden embedding rather than on a text description, information flow is more expressive than two-stage designs that pipe the LLM's text into an external segmenter.

Training is end-to-end with the text generation cross-entropy loss L_txt and a mask loss L_mask combining per-pixel BCE and DICE losses (weights 2.0 each), L = lambda_txt L_txt + lambda_mask L_mask. To preserve the pretrained knowledge, LoRA fine-tunes the LLM while the vision backbone stays frozen; the decoder F_dec is fully tuned, and the <SEG> token embedding, LLM head, and projection layer are trainable. Training data comes entirely from public reasoning-free sources reformulated into a question-answer format: semantic segmentation (ADE20K, COCO-Stuff, plus part-segmentation datasets PACO-LVIS, ImageNet, PASCAL-Part), vanilla referring segmentation (refCLEF, refCOCO, refCOCO+, refCOCOg), and VQA (LLaVA-Instruct-150k for LLaVA v1, LLaVA-v1.5-mix665k for v1.5) to retain conversational ability. COCO samples whose images appear in refCOCO+/g validation sets are excluded to avoid leakage; at most 3 categories are selected per semantic-segmentation image. ReasonSeg itself (239 train samples) is used only for the fine-tuned variants, with instructions optionally rephrased by GPT-3.5 for augmentation.

## Contributions

1. The introduction of the reasoning segmentation task, which outputs a binary mask from a complex, implicit query requiring reasoning and world knowledge — a capability needed for genuinely intelligent perception and robotics.
2. LISA, a multimodal LLM that inherits language abilities and outputs segmentation masks through the embedding-as-mask paradigm: a new <SEG> token whose hidden embedding is decoded into a mask by a SAM-style decoder, trained end-to-end with LoRA.
3. Demonstration of robust zero-shot reasoning segmentation: trained only on reasoning-free datasets (semantic segmentation, referring segmentation, VQA), LISA transfers to reasoning queries, outperforming prior specialist models by more than 20 gIoU points.
4. The ReasonSeg benchmark, over 1,000 image-instruction-mask samples (239 train / 200 val / 779 test) annotated on OpenImages and ScanNetv2 with implicit short-phrase and long-sentence queries, enabling quantitative evaluation of reasoning segmentation.
5. Evidence that LISA also achieves state-of-the-art vanilla referring segmentation and that fine-tuning on as few as 239 reasoning samples brings substantial additional gains.

## Experimental Setup

LISA-7B and LLaVA-13B variants are trained on 8 NVIDIA 24G 3090 GPUs with DeepSpeed, AdamW (learning rate and weight decay 0.0003), 100 warmup steps, batch size 2 per device, gradient accumulation 10, and up to 3 categories per image in semantic segmentation data. Loss weights are lambda_txt = 1.0, lambda_mask = 1.0 (lambda_BCE = lambda_DICE = 2.0). Evaluation on ReasonSeg uses gIoU (average of all per-image IoUs) and cIoU (cumulative intersection over cumulative union), with gIoU preferred since cIoU is biased toward large-area objects. Comparisons on ReasonSeg cover OVSeg, GRES, X-Decoder, SEEM, Grounded-SAM, and a two-stage pipeline combining LLaVA v1.5 with OVSeg (using GPT-3.5 to shorten overlong queries). Vanilla referring segmentation is evaluated on refCOCO, refCOCO+, refCOCOg val/testA/testB against MCN, VLT, CRIS, LAVT, ReLA, X-Decoder, and SEEM. Ablations cover the vision backbone (SAM vs. Mask2Former-Swin-L), LoRA on the SAM backbone, the SAM pre-trained weight, GPT-3.5 instruction rephrasing, and the contribution of each training-data type.

## Results

- Reasoning segmentation: LISA-7B (zero-shot, reasoning-free training) reaches gIoU 44.4 / cIoU 46.0 on ReasonSeg val versus 28.5/18.6 for OVSeg, 26.0/14.5 for Grounded-SAM, and 25.5/21.2 for SEEM — a more than 20% gIoU boost. Fine-tuning on 239 ReasonSeg samples lifts LISA-7B to 52.9/54.0 (val) and 47.3/48.4 (test overall), and LISA-13B-LLaVA1.5 (ft) attains 65.0/72.9 on val (short queries 55.4/50.6, long queries 63.2/65.3) and 61.3/62.2 on the test split. The two-stage LLaVA1.5+OVSeg pipeline is significantly outperformed (e.g., 38.2/23.5 for the 7B variant), which the authors attribute to end-to-end training and the hidden-embedding information channel; adding the 200 ReasonSeg val samples during fine-tuning improves both gIoU and cIoU further.
- Vanilla referring segmentation: LISA-7B achieves 74.9/76.5/71.1 on refCOCO val/testA/testB (fine-tuned: 74.9/79.1/72.3), 62.4/67.4/56.5 on refCOCO+, and 66.4/68.5 on refCOCOg val(U)/test(U) — state-of-the-art across the benchmarks, exceeding ReLA (73.8/76.5/70.2 on refCOCO).
- Ablations: SAM outperforms Mask2Former-Swin-L as the frozen vision backbone (44.4/46.0 vs. 42.4/38.8 gIoU/cIoU; after fine-tuning 52.9/54.0 vs. 50.7/52.3), and the framework with Mask2Former still beats prior models such as X-Decoder, showing backbone flexibility. Applying LoRA to the SAM backbone is inferior to keeping it frozen (51.8/51.9), suggesting fine-tuning impairs SAM's generalization. Removing the SAM pre-trained weight (training from scratch) collapses performance to 35.9/44.6. GPT-3.5 instruction rephrasing adds +2.2 gIoU and +2.9 cIoU. Among data types, removing the semantic segmentation data causes the largest drop (30.4/20.4 versus 52.9/54.0), confirming that multi-class labels inducing diverse binary masks are a major supervision source; without ReasonSeg training samples the model still delivers 44.4/46.0 zero-shot.
- Qualitatively, visual comparisons against OVSeg, GRES, X-Decoder, and SEEM show those models failing on implicit queries while LISA produces accurate, high-quality masks and can also emit explanatory text answers.

## Limitations

The paper trains and evaluates LISA with single-target binary masks: the task and benchmark are defined around one implicit query per image rather than the simultaneous multi-instance segmentation of a concept, and multiple masks in one answer are shown only in qualitative examples in the supplementary material. cIoU is acknowledged to be biased toward large-area objects and to fluctuate considerably, so gIoU is preferred. Performance depends strongly on the pre-trained vision backbone (removing SAM's pre-trained weight substantially degrades results), and fine-tuning the backbone with LoRA hurts generalization. ReasonSeg is a small benchmark (1,218 samples) whose annotations come from human annotators on OpenImages and ScanNetv2, and the fine-tuning experiments themselves consume the 239-sample train split (with 200 val samples used as extra fine-tuning data in the best configuration), leaving headroom questions about scaling reasoning-segmentation supervision.

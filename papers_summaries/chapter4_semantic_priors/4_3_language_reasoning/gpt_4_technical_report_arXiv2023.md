## Summary
GPT-4 is a large-scale multimodal Transformer-based model that accepts image and text inputs and produces text outputs. The report characterizes its capabilities, limitations, and safety properties while withholding architecture and training details. It reaches human-level performance on many professional and academic exams, outperforms prior models on traditional NLP benchmarks, and surpasses the English-language performance of earlier systems in 24 of 26 languages on translated MMLU. Its central achievement is a predictably scaling deep-learning stack: final loss and some capability metrics were accurately predicted from far smaller models.

## Background and Problem
Before GPT-4, models such as GPT-3.5 still scored in the bottom 10% of test takers on a simulated bar exam and lagged behind their English-language performance in other languages. For training runs as large as GPT-4, extensive model-specific tuning is infeasible, so infrastructure and optimization must behave predictably across scales, and predicting capabilities in advance was viewed as important for alignment and deployment decisions.

## Method
GPT-4 is a Transformer-style model pre-trained on publicly available and licensed data to predict the next token, then fine-tuned with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). It is multimodal, accepting prompts of arbitrarily interlaced text and images. For competitive and safety reasons, no further details on architecture, model size, hardware, or training compute are given. Predictable scaling relied on two registered predictions: a power-law fit with an irreducible loss term from models using at most 10,000x less compute predicted GPT-4's final loss, and HumanEval pass rates were extrapolated from models using at most 1,000x less compute. Safety combined adversarial testing by over 50 domain experts with rule-based reward models, zero-shot GPT-4 classifiers applying human-written rubrics during RLHF to reward refusals of harmful requests.

## Contributions
The report's stated contributions are: (1) GPT-4, a large multimodal model with human-level performance on difficult professional and academic benchmarks, beating existing large language models and most reported state-of-the-art systems including task-specific fine-tuning; (2) a predictable-scaling methodology with accurate registered predictions from much smaller models; (3) evidence that exam capabilities stem primarily from pre-training rather than RLHF; (4) quantified safety improvements from red-teaming and rule-based reward models; (5) OpenAI Evals, an open-sourced benchmarking framework; and (6) an extensive system card describing risks such as bias, disinformation, and cybersecurity.

## Experimental Setup
Human exams (bar, LSAT, SAT, GRE, AP, medical, sommelier, LeetCode) were simulated under real exam conditions and scoring, with no exam-specific training; questions seen during pre-training were removed and the lower score reported. Traditional NLP benchmarks were run few-shot: MMLU (5-shot), HellaSwag (10-shot), ARC (25-shot), HumanEval (0-shot), DROP (3-shot), and GSM-8K (5-shot with chain-of-thought), compared against GPT-3.5, external few-shot LMs (U-PaLM, LLaMA, PaLM, Minerva), and benchmark-tuned SOTA systems. MMLU was translated with Azure Translate into 26 languages. Instruction following was judged by human labelers on 5,214 real ChatGPT and API prompts. Safety used internal adversarial factuality evals, TruthfulQA, calibration, incorrect-behavior rates on disallowed and sensitive prompts, and RealToxicityPrompts.

## Results
On a simulated Uniform Bar Exam, GPT-4 scored 298/400 (about the 90th percentile) against 213/400 for GPT-3.5. On academic benchmarks it obtained MMLU 86.4% (5-shot) versus 70.0% for GPT-3.5, HellaSwag 95.3%, HumanEval 67.0% versus 48.1% for GPT-3.5, and GSM-8K 92.0% with chain-of-thought; DROP was the sole exception, where GPT-4's 80.9 F1 trailed benchmark-tuned QDGAT at 88.4. On translated MMLU it scored 85.5% in English and exceeded the English-language performance of GPT-3.5, Chinchilla, and PaLM in 24 of 26 languages, including Welsh and Swahili. Human raters preferred GPT-4's responses on 70.2% of the 5,214 prompts. Safety also improved: 82% fewer responses to disallowed-content requests than GPT-3.5, toxic generations on 0.73% versus 6.48% of RealToxicityPrompts cases, and a 19-percentage-point gain over the latest GPT-3.5 on internal adversarial factuality evaluations. The fitted scaling law accurately predicted final loss, and HumanEval predictions held except on the easiest bucket.

## Limitations
The paper states that GPT-4 is not fully reliable: it hallucinates facts, makes reasoning errors, and can be confidently wrong, so care is needed in high-stakes contexts. It has a limited context window, does not learn from experience, and lacks knowledge of events after its September 2021 pre-training data cutoff. It can be overly gullible in accepting obviously false statements from users. Post-training reduces calibration (expected calibration error rising from 0.007 to 0.074 on an MMLU subset), biases remain to be fully characterized, jailbreaks still exist, and certain capabilities, such as the easiest HumanEval bucket, proved hard to predict.

# LLaMA: Open and Efficient Foundation Language Models
**Authors:** Hugo Touvron, Thibaut Lavril, Gautier Izacard, Xavier Martinet, Marie-Anne Lachaux, Timothée Lacroix, Baptiste Rozière, Naman Goyal, Eric Hambro, Faisal Azhar, Aurelien Rodriguez, Armand Joulin, Edouard Grave, Guillaume Lample

## Summary
LLaMA is a collection of foundation language models ranging from 7B to 65B parameters, trained on trillions of tokens using publicly available datasets exclusively. The paper argues that for a target level of performance the preferred model is the fastest at inference rather than the fastest to train, so smaller models trained on more tokens are preferable. LLaMA-13B outperforms GPT-3 (175B) on most benchmarks despite being 10x smaller, and LLaMA-65B is competitive with Chinchilla-70B and PaLM-540B. All models are released to the research community.

## Background and Problem
Few-shot abilities of large language models were believed to emerge only with scale, driving a focus on ever-larger parameter counts. Hoffmann et al. (2022) showed that for a given training compute budget, smaller models trained on more data outperform larger ones, but this scaling objective disregards the inference budget, critical when serving models at scale. The strongest models (GPT-3, Gopher, Chinchilla, PaLM) rely on proprietary or undocumented data, while open alternatives (OPT, GPT-NeoX, BLOOM, GLM) lag PaLM-62B and Chinchilla.

## Method
LLaMA is a Transformer with three modifications: pre-normalization using RMSNorm, the SwiGLU activation function in place of ReLU, and rotary positional embeddings replacing absolute positional embeddings. The 6.7B, 13.0B, 32.5B, and 65.2B models are trained with AdamW, a cosine learning rate schedule decaying to 10% of the maximum, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0, and a 4M-token batch; the 7B and 13B models train on 1.0T tokens and the 33B and 65B models on 1.4T tokens. The corpus uses only public sources: English CommonCrawl (67%), C4 (15%), and GitHub, Wikipedia, Gutenberg and Books3, ArXiv, and StackExchange (together 18%), tokenized with SentencePiece BPE. Efficiency optimizations include a causal-attention implementation that does not store attention weights, selective activation checkpointing, and model and sequence parallelism; the 65B run processed about 380 tokens/sec/GPU on 2048 A100-80GB GPUs, taking roughly 21 days.

## Contributions
The paper's contributions are: (1) an openly released family of models, 7B to 65B, that match or beat much larger proprietary ones, showing state-of-the-art performance is attainable with public data alone; (2) an inference-oriented scaling philosophy, training smaller models on more tokens than compute-optimal recipes suggest (a 7B model keeps improving past 1T tokens); (3) LLaMA-I, an instruction-finetuned 65B variant demonstrating that brief instruction tuning yields large MMLU gains; (4) responsible-AI measurements of bias, toxicity, and misinformation; and (5) a carbon-footprint accounting motivating reuse of released weights.

## Experimental Setup
Evaluation covers 20 benchmarks in zero-shot and few-shot (1 to 64 examples) settings. Common sense reasoning uses BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-easy, ARC-challenge, and OpenBookQA (zero-shot). Closed-book QA uses NaturalQuestions and TriviaQA (exact match, 0/1/5/64-shot). Reading comprehension uses RACE. Mathematical reasoning uses MATH and GSM8k, with maj1@k majority voting (k=256 for MATH, k=100 for GSM8k). Code generation uses HumanEval (zero-shot) and MBPP (3-shot), reporting pass@1 at temperature 0.1. MMLU is run in the 5-shot setting. Comparisons cover GPT-3, Gopher, Chinchilla, PaLM, OPT, GPT-J, and GPT-NeoX, plus instruction-tuned OPT-IML and Flan-PaLM. Bias, toxicity, and misinformation are probed with RealToxicityPrompts, CrowS-Pairs, WinoGender, and TruthfulQA.

## Results
LLaMA-65B outperforms Chinchilla-70B on all common-sense benchmarks except BoolQ, and beats PaLM-540B everywhere except BoolQ and WinoGrande; LLaMA-13B outperforms GPT-3 on most of these tasks despite being 10x smaller. On TriviaQA, LLaMA-65B reaches 68.2 zero-shot and 73.0 64-shot exact match, and the 13B model is competitive with GPT-3 and Chinchilla at 5-10x smaller scale. On GSM8k, LLaMA-65B scores 50.9 (69.7 with majority voting), beating Minerva-62B (68.5) without mathematical fine-tuning; on MATH it reaches 10.6 (20.5). On HumanEval pass@1, LLaMA-65B scores 23.7 versus 15.9 for PaLM-62B. On MMLU, LLaMA-65B trails Chinchilla (63.4 versus 67.5) and PaLM-540B (69.3), attributed to only 177GB of books and academic papers in pre-training; brief instruction tuning lifts LLaMA-I to 68.9%, above OPT-IML-Max-30B (43.2) and Flan-PaLM-62B (59.6). CrowS-Pairs bias averages 66.6 for LLaMA-65B versus 67.2 for GPT-3 and 69.5 for OPT-175B.

## Limitations
On TruthfulQA even the 65B model answers truthfully only 0.57 of the time, indicating the model is likely to hallucinate incorrect answers. Toxicity on RealToxicityPrompts increases with model size, especially on respectful prompts. CrowS-Pairs shows biases, particularly in religion, age, and gender, attributed to CommonCrawl despite filtering, and WinoGender shows degraded co-reference resolution on gendered pronouns and gotcha cases. The authors caution that these responsible-AI evaluations are not sufficient to fully understand the risks. On MMLU the models lag Chinchilla and PaLM, a gap attributed to limited books and academic papers in pre-training. Training the family consumed about 2,638 MWh, an estimated 1,015 tCO2eq of emissions.

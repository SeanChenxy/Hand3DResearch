# EgoEngine: From Egocentric Human Videos to High-Fidelity Dexterous Robot Demonstrations

**Authors:** Yangcen Liu, Shuo Cheng, Xinchen Yin, Woo Chul Shin, Alfred Cueva, Yiran Yang, Zhenyang Chen, Chuye Zhang, Danfei Xu  
**Date:** 2026-06-10  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2606.12604](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12604)

## Summary
EgoEngine is a two-branch data engine that converts egocentric human manipulation videos (captured with Aria Gen2 glasses) into paired robot demonstrations for dexterous hands. An object-centric digital twin (FoundationStereo depth, SAM2 masks, FoundationPose 6D object trajectories) grounds both branches: the action branch retargets human hand motion with inverse kinematics and refines it in simulation via MCTS-style switching among Replay, MPC, and residual-RL solvers under an object-tracking objective, while the visual branch replaces the demonstrator with a rendered robot through inpainting and occlusion-aware blending. Distilling an HPT policy purely on the generated data yields, to the authors' knowledge, the first zero-shot visuomotor dexterous policy from egocentric human videos without any real-robot demonstrations, matching or exceeding teleoperation-trained policies on 2 of 4 real tasks.

## Background and Problem
Dexterous manipulation is bottlenecked by the cost of teleoperated robot data, especially for high-DoF contact-rich control, whereas egocentric human videos naturally capture diverse contact-rich behaviors. Reusing them requires bridging two gaps: the visual gap (human arms occlude the scene and differ from the robot embodiment) and the action gap (morphology, kinematics, actuation, and contact-dynamics differences make directly retargeted trajectories physically infeasible, and videos provide proprioceptive trajectories rather than action commands). Prior works use human videos only for pretraining or co-training with robot teleoperation data; the paper targets full demonstration generation that preserves the demonstrated task while producing robot-view observations and executable robot actions.

## Method
The engine first reconstructs a digital twin from each Aria Gen2 video: FoundationStereo estimates absolute depth, SAM2 produces human arm-hand masks (from hand-keypoint prompts) and tracked object masks, and FoundationPose with the object mesh recovers a temporally consistent 6D object trajectory, all aligned to the robot via AprilTag calibration. The action branch solves MINK inverse kinematics on fingertip and wrist targets for a reference trajectory, then decomposes the long horizon into 20-step chunks optimized against a thresholded object-pose-tracking objective (with early termination) plus human-mimic, smoothness, contact, and lifting auxiliary rewards; a heuristic MCTS-style switcher escalates each chunk from Replay to MPC to a PPO residual hand policy only as needed, jointly solving two-chunk windows to avoid local minima. The visual branch removes the demonstrator with Inpaint-Anything v2, renders the robot at the egocentric viewpoint along the optimized trajectory, and computes a visible-robot mask via two-pass differential rendering before blending. The paired observation-action data trains an HPT visuomotor policy (ResNet-18 visual stem, proprioceptive stem, flow-matching action head) with L2 action regression, using no real-robot teleoperation data.

## Contributions
- A two-branch data engine that jointly bridges the visual and action gaps, converting egocentric human videos into synchronized, executable dexterous robot demonstrations grounded in an object-centric digital twin.
- An MCTS-style adaptive mode-switching trajectory optimizer that assigns Replay, MPC, or residual-RL capacity per chunk, cutting generation cost relative to full RL refinement.
- Evidence that executable action generation is the primary factor for downstream policy performance, enabling zero-shot real-robot dexterous manipulation from human videos alone.

## Experimental Setup
Evaluation uses TACO (2,500 sequences; 16 embodiment-compatible pairs for simulation) and a self-collected Aria dataset of 200 egocentric videos across four tasks (Mustard placement, Drawer opening with cube insertion, Hammer nailing, Flower watering), plus 200 real teleoperation demonstrations collected only as a comparison baseline; the embodiment is a bimanual RB-Y1 with two 7-DoF arms and 12-DoF XHands in simulation and a single-arm RB-Y1 with one XHand on the real robot. Visual fidelity is measured by Frechet Distance of ResNet18/VGG16/DINOv2 feature distributions against real robot observations against EgoMimic, VACE (WAN2.1), and Phantom; action quality is measured by success rate, rollout progress, tracking reward, and simulation-step cost against Mink (Replay), Spider (MPC), and H2S2R (RL). Downstream policies are trained per task on generated data only and evaluated on the real robot with randomized object poses, with ablations over branch combinations and demonstration budgets of 1-50.

## Results
In simulation, EgoEngine matches pure-RL trajectory quality (TACO success 0.83 versus 0.17 for Replay and 0.25 for MPC; Aria success 0.90 versus 0.10 and 0.20) at less than half the cost (34,842 versus 73,675 simulation steps on TACO; 16,560 versus 20,237 on Aria), raising Aria generation throughput by 22.0% from 2.36 to 2.88 demos/hour on a single RTX 4090. Feature-space Frechet Distance to real robot observations improves from 764.5 (raw human video) to 614.7 with ResNet18 and from 602.9 to 473.1 with DINOv2, roughly matching Phantom. Zero-shot real-robot policies reach 40% (Mustard), 35% (Drawer), 70% (Flower), and 60% (Hammer) success versus 80/80/70/25 for teleoperation-trained policies and near zero for human-video or Phantom pipelines; the branch ablation averages 0.03 (human videos), 0.05 (+visual only), 0.43 (+action only), and 0.51 (full). Generated trajectories are also smoother than teleoperation (average SPARC -4.81 versus -6.60), and the system transfers a long-horizon knife-cutting task that visual teleoperation could not reliably collect.

## Limitations
The authors identify three axes. Quality: the visual branch uses blending-based synthesis rather than fully learned photorealism, and action generation still suffers contact-modeling errors and the sim-to-real gap. Scalability: digital twin construction remains the bottleneck, with challenges in object asset acquisition, state estimation under severe occlusion, and deformable objects. Efficiency: simulation-based trajectory optimization remains slow at very large scale despite parallelism, and generated data is not uniformly better than real teleoperation at large data budgets, with precision pinch grasps remaining a key failure mode.

# TraceGen: World Modeling in 3D Trace Space Enables Learning from Cross-Embodiment Videos

**Authors:** Seungjae Lee, Yoonkyo Jung, Inkook Chun, Yao-Chih Lee, Zikui Cai, Hongjia Huang, Aayush Talreja, Tan Dat Dao, Yongyuan Liang, Jia-Bin Huang, Furong Huang

## Summary
TraceGen is a world model that predicts future scene-level motion in a compact 3D "trace-space" of point trajectories instead of pixel space, so that human and robot videos of different embodiments, cameras, and scenes become interchangeable training data. Its companion data engine TraceForge converts heterogeneous videos into consistent 3D traces through camera-motion compensation, multi-viewpoint 3D point tracking, and speed retargeting, yielding a 123K-video corpus with 1.8M observation-trace-language triplets. Pretrained on this corpus, a 0.67B-parameter model adapts to a new robot with only five warm-up videos to reach 80% success across four real manipulation tasks, and with only five uncalibrated handheld-phone human videos still reaches 67.5% success, while running 50-600x faster than video-generation-based world models.

## Background and Problem
Collecting task-specific robot demonstrations for every new platform and scene is slow and costly, yet videos of other embodiments are abundant; the obstacles are disparities in embodiment, camera viewpoint, and environment. Pixel-space video world models waste capacity on appearance irrelevant to control and are slow at inference, while VLM token outputs lack the spatial precision for fine object motion, and prior trace predictors are restricted to 2D traces, static lab cameras, and object-centric pipelines needing detectors and heuristic filtering. The paper defines the problem as learning a transferable 3D motion prior from cross-embodiment, cross-environment, cross-task video that adapts to a target robot from a handful of demonstrations.

## Method
TraceForge is the data engine: it chunks task-relevant events, generates three complementary language instructions per chunk with a VLM, estimates camera pose and depth with a fine-tuned VGGT predictor from SpatialTrackerV2, tracks 3D points with TAPIP3D/CoTracker3 on a 20x20 grid, reprojects all traces into a fixed reference-camera frame to compensate camera motion, and applies arc-length speed retargeting so trajectories of different embodiments share a fixed horizon (traces are stored as screen-aligned (x, y, depth) so 2D and 3D modalities co-train; about 20% of traces are 2D-only). The result is the TraceForge-123K corpus of 123K videos and 1.8M observation-trace-language triplets from eight human and robot sources, more than 15x larger than prior trace datasets, with a sanity check showing sub-2.3 cm endpoint error against robot forward kinematics. TraceGen itself is a CogVideoX-based flow transformer that fuses frozen DINOv3, SigLIP, and depth (SigLIP with learnable stem adapter) features plus frozen T5 text tokens via Prismatic-VLM-style concatenation, and predicts velocity-like 3D keypoint increments over 32 future steps through a stochastic-interpolant linear ODE; predicted traces are executed by inverse-kinematics tracking after a lightweight warm-up fine-tune that translates embodiment-agnostic traces into a robot's action space.

## Contributions
- A 3D trace-space world model (TraceGen) that abstracts appearance while retaining manipulation-relevant geometry, enabling learning from cross-embodiment, cross-environment, cross-task videos without object detectors or heuristic filtering.
- TraceForge, a data pipeline with camera-motion compensation and speed retargeting that scales trace-annotated training data to 123K videos and 1.8M triplets from in-the-wild and in-lab sources.
- Evidence of efficient few-shot adaptation: 80% success with five in-domain robot videos and 67.5% human-to-robot transfer from five uncalibrated handheld-phone videos, at 50-600x faster inference than video-based world models.

## Experimental Setup
Real-robot experiments use a Franka Research 3 on four tasks: folding a garment (Clothes), inserting a tennis ball into a box (Ball), sweeping trash into a dustpan (Brush), and placing a block in a purple region (Block); given a single RGB-D frame and an instruction, traces are generated with 100-step ODE integration and executed via IK, with 10 trials per task per configuration. Baselines include video-generation world models (AVDC, NovaFlow with Wan2.2 and Veo3.1 backbones) and trace-based models (3DFlowAction, given ground-truth masks); all video baselines share a unified video-to-trace extraction pipeline, and latency is measured on an RTX A5000 except multi-GPU Wan2.2 and API-based Veo3.1. Two warm-up regimes are compared (five in-domain robot videos versus five 3-4-second uncalibrated human phone videos per task, no cherry-picking), plus pretraining-source ablations (from scratch, SSV2 human clips, AgiBot robot clips, full TraceForge-123K) and a long-horizon four-step Sorting task.

## Results
With five in-domain robot warm-up videos TraceGen achieves 80% overall success (Clothes 10/10, Ball 6/10, Brush 8/10, Block 8/10) versus 25% for a from-scratch model, and 82.5% with fifteen videos; every sub-10B baseline fails zero-shot while large video models succeed only with 600x longer inference (TraceGen is 3.8x faster than trace baselines and over 50x faster than video models). Human-to-robot transfer from five uncalibrated handheld-phone videos yields 67.5% overall success where from-scratch training yields 0%. Pretraining-source ablations show 70% overall with TraceForge-123K versus 45% robot-only (AgiBot) and 25% human-only (SSV2) pretraining under identical warm-up. In the long-horizon Sorting task the pretrained model holds a stable 0.8 per-subtask success across all four steps while the from-scratch model degrades from 1.0 to 0.4 due to compounding errors.

## Limitations
The authors note that only a linear interpolation schedule is used, with no mechanism to control which trajectory mode is sampled for ambiguous tasks. Source videos contain inefficient or corrective motions, and despite filtering some noisy demonstrations remain; zero-shot prediction under novel embodiments or unseen environments can occasionally produce plausible but physically infeasible trajectories, and fine-grained tasks may lack the trajectory detail for precise execution. The long-horizon evaluation additionally assumes a scripted grasping policy, since TraceGen models placing traces only, and validation is limited to human-like robot arms rather than very different robot types.

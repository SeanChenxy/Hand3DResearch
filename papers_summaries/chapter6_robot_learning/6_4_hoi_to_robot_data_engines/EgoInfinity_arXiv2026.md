# EgoInfinity: A Web-Scale 4D Hand-Object Interaction Data Engine for Any-View Robot Retargeting and Video-to-Action Robot Learning

**Authors:** Gaotian Wang, Kejia Ren, Andrew Morgan, Yiting Chen, Howard H. Qian, Podshara Chanrungmaneekul, Kaiyu Hang  
**Date:** 2026-06-16  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2606.17385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17385); DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2606.17385`  
**Zotero item:** `54JNCRCL` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/54JNCRCL))  
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.  

## Summary
EgoInfinity is a modular, fully automated data engine that lifts in-the-wild internet RGB videos into agent-agnostic, metric 4D hand-object interaction representations - per-frame hand meshes and trajectories, 6-DoF object poses, object geometry, and contact-relevant states - without wearables, depth sensors, CAD models, or human annotation. Cross-module metric calibration plus interaction-aware refinement (state-dependent rigid hand binding for grasped objects, centroid locking for static ones) suppresses the drift and contact inconsistencies of pure visual reconstruction, and an SE(3)-equivariant, simulation-trained retargeter compiles the recovered hand motion into executable joint trajectories for diverse robots from arbitrary viewpoints. The engine is demonstrated on Action100M footage (currently bounded at about 127K hours), with retargeting validated on Unitree G1, Robonaut2, and dual Franka FR3, and real-robot skills including LEAP-hand grasping and Franka cutting, pouring, and wiping.

## Background and Problem
Internet videos are the largest reservoir of human manipulation knowledge (Action100M alone spans 14.6 years across 147M action segments), but they lack metric 3D geometry, 6-DoF object state, contact information, and executable actions, while curated egocentric datasets and robot teleoperation corpora are limited in scale, task diversity, or hardware requirements. Existing learning-from-human-video methods therefore remain under-grounded for execution or mis-grounded when 2D pseudo-actions are lifted into robot action space, and prior retargeting methods assume aligned embodiments and full-body observations that arbitrary web videos (hands only, partial arms, moving cameras) do not provide. The paper defines the problem as automating the video-to-action pipeline at web scale: recovering physically reliable metric 4D manipulation state from raw clips and compiling it into executable trajectories for any robot morphology without human-in-the-loop annotation.

## Method
The engine runs a two-pass strategy - a lightweight temporal scan filters clips by hand-motion statistics and camera-motion stability, then a full stack reconstructs only active segments, targeting approximately static-camera tutorial-style footage. Unified metric geometry is established with MOGE-2 (focal length and global metric scale), FLOW3R (dense depth), and GeoCalib (gravity), into which WiLOR MANO hand estimates (with infilling for unstable frames), SAM-3 object detection driven by LLM-extracted text descriptions, SAM-2 mask propagation, SAM-3D mesh reconstruction, and FoundationPose++ 6-DoF tracking are all calibrated. Interaction-aware refinement classifies each frame into fine-grained states (static, grasped by left/right/both hands, moving) using MEMFOF optical flow and hand keypoints, then selects the state-appropriate pose source: grasped objects are rigidly bound to the hand frame with a palm-aligned canonical transform, static objects are locked to MAD-filtered point-cloud centroids, moving objects retain back-projected mask proposals, with per-segment depth realignment and sanity checks for implausible scale or spurious detections. Exocentric footage is converted to egocentric views by rigid 3D reframing under a synthesized, gravity-aligned camera above the hand midpoint rather than 2D generative translation. The retargeter is an SE(3)-equivariant Vector-Neuron Transformer trained purely in MuJoCo that predicts a robot-specific kinematic root frame via flow matching, clusters and scores root hypotheses through downstream IK (convergence, tracking residual, manipulability, joint-limit margin, smoothness), and retargets fingers directly from MANO keypoints.

## Contributions
- A fully automated, component-replaceable 4D manipulation data engine for web-scale video that outputs metric hand trajectories, 6-DoF object poses, object geometry, and contact states with no wearable, depth, CAD, or annotation requirements.
- Cross-module metric calibration and interaction-aware refinement that improve physical reliability over naively chained perception components, reducing drift and contact inconsistencies.
- A functional cross-embodiment retargeter (equivariant root-frame estimation plus IK scoring) that works from arbitrary viewpoints and partially observed humans, validated in simulation and on real robots.

## Experimental Setup
Validation spans perception fidelity, kinematic feasibility, contact consistency, cross-embodiment generalization, and real-robot skill acquisition. A curated Action100M subset of 106 processed videos (277 objects, 12,107 frames, median clip duration 5.8 s) is hosted on a browser-based data server supporting search, online processing, and inspection of intermediate outputs; 88% of curated clips and 47% of objects are manipulated with balanced left, right, and bimanual grasps. Retargeting is evaluated on three embodiments (Unitree G1, NASA Robonaut2, dual Franka FR3) using IK success rate, hand position and orientation tracking error, joint-limit margin, manipulability, and trajectory smoothness. Real-robot experiments use extracted hand motions as priors for a grasping policy on a LEAP dexterous hand and directly retargeted motions on a dual-arm Franka FR3 for cutting, pouring, and wiping.

## Results
Retargeting achieves per-frame IK success rates of 0.821 (Unitree G1), 0.774 (Robonaut2), and 0.706 (dual Franka), with mean hand tracking errors of 2.86 cm / 6.73 degrees, 6.67 cm / 8.25 degrees, and 10.27 cm / 12.17 degrees respectively, alongside reported joint-limit margins, manipulability indices, and smoothness values. The curated subset shows the engine's coverage of container-, tool-, and food-class objects across actions such as slicing, inserting, pouring, and picking, with the three-state interaction distribution averaged over manipulated objects. End-to-end, the pipeline converts in-the-wild RGB into simulated and real robot deployments, including LEAP-hand grasping of diverse objects from video-derived priors and functional execution of cutting, pouring, and wiping on dual Franka arms; quantitative success rates for these real-robot skills are not reported in the available evidence.

## Limitations
The engine currently assumes approximately static-camera videos, excluding body-mounted and hand-held footage, which limits corpus diversity though the modular design can absorb future SLAM-aware depth models. Interaction-aware refinement provides coarse grasp detection and spatial correlation rather than contact-level accuracy - exact fingertip placement, force consistency, and no-slip constraints are not guaranteed - and no tactile observations are produced. The retargeter is robot-specific and may require retraining or calibration for new designs, and it targets functional transfer rather than fine-grained kinematic imitation, which may be insufficient for tasks demanding exact hand posture, precise contact timing, or highly dexterous manipulation.

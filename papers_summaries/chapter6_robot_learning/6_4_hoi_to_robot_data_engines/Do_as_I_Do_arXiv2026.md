# Do as I Do: Dexterous Manipulation Data from Everyday Human Videos

**Authors:** Bhawna Paliwal, Haritheja Etukuru, William Liang, Pieter Abbeel, Nur Muhammad Mahi Shafiullah, Jitendra Malik

## Summary
Do as I Do is a two-step algorithm that turns in-the-wild monocular RGB videos of hand-object interaction - spanning egocentric, exocentric, internet, and generative-video sources - into executable data for multi-fingered dexterous robot hands. The reconstruction step couples HaWoR hand tracking with a SAM 3D-based object tracker that repurposes an image-to-3D flow-matching generative model for occlusion-robust, temporally guided pose sampling, while the retargeting step extends MPPI-style annealed sampling optimization in GPU-parallel simulation with warmup steps, random force perturbation, and a transition reward designed for noisy reconstructed references. The pipeline produces 500 human-verified dexterous manipulation trajectories, of which 10 diverse motions are executed on a real bimanual Sharpa Wave hand and UR3e arm system, and retargeting success rises from a 25% baseline to 71% on reconstructed references and to 81% on clean OakInk2 MoCap data.

## Background and Problem
Experiential robot data is bottlenecked by teleoperation (operator expertise, cost, and the mechanical transparency of the rig) and by simulated exploration (designing diverse environments and rewards), while the bulk of human manipulation knowledge sits in observational monocular RGB videos. Converting watching into doing requires solving two challenges: reconstructing the human behavior in 3D and retargeting it onto a vastly different robotic embodiment. Prior approaches sidestep generality with assumptions on behavior (pick-and-place only), objects (3D-scanned only), or modality (depth, 3D sensors, or hand keypoints from specialized hardware), and most dynamics-aware retargeting methods assume clean MoCap references. The paper targets the general case - arbitrary rigid objects, no grasp priors, noisy reconstructed references with temporal discontinuities and severe hand-object misalignments - and additionally asks how practitioners should filter online human video for manipulation relevance.

## Method
Reconstruction treats hand and object separately: HaWoR tracks the hand, while the object pipeline runs SAM 3 segmentation, MoGe depth and camera-intrinsics estimation, and SAM 3D single-image mesh generation, composing all signals into a consistent near-metric space. Object tracking repurposes SAM 3D as a video tracker by exploiting the shared latent of its joint shape-pose distribution: the shape is fixed at an anchor frame and each frame's pose is sampled from the flow-matching model biased toward the previous-frame pose by blending target interpolants into every Euler step, with adaptive guidance where shape strength is fixed in [0.9, 1] and pose strength is derived from the object's rotational velocity estimated from 2D point tracks. Per-frame, N stochastic pose candidates are drawn and clustered under a weighted SE(3) distance with consensus filtering and mask-IoU selection, which matches pose-likelihood ranking at up to 30x lower cost; hand and object are then aligned by scaling the object translation to the hand reconstruction via least squares over centroids, and the trajectory is gravity-aligned with GeoCalib. Retargeting builds on the SPIDER framework of MPPI-style sampling-based optimization with a kernel annealed across iterations and horizon, and adds three components for noisy references: H warmup steps in which the object is welded in place while the hand approaches before the weld is dropped, random force perturbations during rollouts to encourage robust controls, and a transition reward that penalizes failed rest-to-in-hand transitions detected via a hand-object distance threshold.

## Contributions
- A two-step reconstruct-and-retarget algorithm that converts monocular RGB human videos into multi-fingered dexterous robot actions without limiting assumptions on the displayed behavior or object classes, supporting arbitrary rigid bodies.
- A SAM 3D-based guided-diffusion object tracker that outperforms the state of the art on standard hand-object benchmarks and handles egocentric, exocentric, internet, and generated videos.
- Retargeting innovations (warmup steps, random force perturbation, transition reward) that make scalable dynamics-aware retargeting robust to noisy reconstructed references rather than only clean MoCap data.
- To the authors' knowledge, the first pipeline from an internet video to real dexterous hand rollouts, plus an efficacy playbook quantifying how much online human video survives quality filtering for manipulation learning.

## Experimental Setup
Object reconstruction is isolated by supplying ground-truth hands and evaluating on DexYCB (160 annotated videos) and HOI4D (12 annotated videos) with F-5, F-10, and Chamfer distance, against joint hand-object methods (HO, IHOI, HORSE, MCC-HO, G-HOP) and object trackers swapped into the fixed pipeline (FoundationPose, Any6D). A 150-video in-the-wild benchmark drawn from internet clips, egocentric datasets, and generative video models is scored by human preference (3 volunteers per video) against the state-of-the-art FPose tracker. Retargeting is evaluated on 655 reconstructed in-the-wild references and OakInk2 (1,352 clean bimanual MoCap trajectories) against SPIDER as the annealed-sampling baseline, with success defined as mean position error below 0.1 m and mean rotation error below 0.5 rad on the 22-DoF Sharpa Wave hand; real deployment uses bimanual Sharpa Wave hands with UR3e arms, both commanded at 50 Hz. The data-filtering analysis starts from 2,000 ten-second clips sampled from 100DOH.

## Results
On DexYCB the method reaches F-5 0.71, F-10 0.93, and Chamfer 0.66, and on HOI4D F-5 0.72, F-10 0.91, and Chamfer 0.49, establishing a new state of the art over all baselines, including FoundationPose (0.69/0.89/0.89 on DexYCB). On the 150-video in-the-wild benchmark, human raters prefer its object tracking over FPose 67% of the time, with most videos receiving unanimous preferences; ablations show adaptive pose guidance from point tracking consistently improves quality and that clustering-based selection performs on par with pose-likelihood selection at up to 30x lower cost. Retargeting success on reconstructed in-the-wild references rises from 25% (annealed sampling) to 66% with warmup, 67% with perturbation, and 71% with the transition reward (mean position error 0.05 m, rotation 0.28 rad), while on OakInk2 it improves from 72% to 81% (position 0.03 m, rotation 0.15 rad), showing gains even on clean MoCap. The pipeline yielded 500 human-verified trajectories sourced 53% from internet, 31% egocentric, and 16% generated videos, and 10 motions covering tripod, power, ventral, and parallel-extension grasp classes were executed on the real bimanual system; quantitative real-robot success rates are not reported in the available evidence. The playbook finds that only 187 of 2,000 sampled 100DOH clips (9%) contain meaningful interaction and only 83 (4%) survive all checks, implying roughly a 20x penalty for unfiltered internet video.

## Limitations
The method assumes rigid objects and semi-accurate metric depth from monocular RGB and may fail when either assumption does not hold. Monocular observation leaves the true hand-object distance ambiguous, making physical contact hard to distinguish from visual occlusion, and only the hand and a single object are reconstructed rather than the full scene, so environmental constraints such as obstacles or articulated structures cannot be reasoned about even though human intention is also expressed through hand-scene interaction. Finally, current physics simulators model real-world dynamics only approximately, which places an upper bound on achievable real-world performance.

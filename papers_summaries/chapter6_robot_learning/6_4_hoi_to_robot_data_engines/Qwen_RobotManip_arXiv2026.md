# Qwen-RobotManip Technical Report: Alignment Unlocks Scale for Robotic Manipulation Foundation Models

## Summary
Qwen-RobotManip is a Vision-Language-Action foundation model built on Qwen-VL that applies the "alignment first, then scale" recipe of language-model pretraining to manipulation. Its data engine converts about 1,933 hours of egocentric hand demonstrations (EgoDex, VITRA, EgoVerse) into roughly 24,808 hours of robot trajectories across 15 bimanual platforms by retargeting MANO hand keypoints to gripper actions, inpainting hands out of the video, and compositing IK-rendered robots with depth-based occlusion masks; combined with about 11,000 hours of curated open-source robot data this yields a roughly 38,100-hour pretraining corpus built entirely without proprietary data collection. A unified cross-embodiment alignment framework (canonical masked state-action representation, camera-frame delta pose, in-context adaptation, dual-stream VLA/VLM co-training) turns this scale into emergent generalization, ranking 1st on RoboChallenge Table30-v1 with a 20% relative improvement and beating prior state of the art including pi0.5 on every out-of-distribution benchmark tested.

## Background and Problem
Language and multimodal foundation models generalize because heterogeneous data is aligned under a unified formulation and scaled, but manipulation data is inherently heterogeneous, expensive to collect, and narrow in diversity, so scaling data volume produces interference rather than synergy when observation and action representations are incompatible. Existing demonstration corpora concentrate on narrow teleoperation setups, and standard in-domain benchmarks let models without large-scale pretraining match pretrained ones while performance collapses under real distribution shift. The report therefore asks whether a scaling recipe for manipulation is achievable and how genuine generalization should be measured, defining alignment as a prerequisite for data scaling rather than an independent engineering choice.

## Method
The data engine has two pillars. The human-to-robot synthesis pipeline performs action alignment - a virtual finger defined as 0.7 x index + 0.3 x middle fingertip yields end-effector position (thumb-virtual-finger midpoint), gripper width (thumb-virtual-finger distance), and an orthonormal gripper frame from the jaw line and wrist-to-fingertip direction, smoothed by Savitzky-Golay filtering and Gaussian-weighted SLERP - and visual alignment - SAM3 segments the hands, ProPainter inpaints them out, a grid search finds the robot base placement that maximizes IK feasibility per morphology, MuJoCo inverse kinematics tracks the smoothed trajectory to render each of 15 bimanual robots (arms from Panda, UR5e, ARX-L5, xArm7, Sawyer, Kinova Gen3, IIWA, Jaco, FR3, UR10e, ViperX, WidowX, Piper, YAM, AgileX ALOHA), and Depth Anything v3 metric depth supplies occlusion masks for compositing; per-source frame subsampling aligns egocentric action speeds to robot data. A curation pipeline then applies five state-action signal filters (sudden-change detection, state-action trend alignment, extreme-value removal, forward-kinematics consistency, base-frame and orientation alignment) plus cross-modal checks on instruction consistency, video-state consistency via URDF reprojection IoU, and video quality. On the model side, a decoupled Qwen-VL backbone with a DiT action expert uses a canonical state-action space (7D joint, 9D end-effector, 1D gripper, 12D dexterous hand) with per-dimension binary masking, camera-frame delta end-effector actions with camera positional encoding, structured embodiment prompts with embodied chain-of-thought for in-context adaptation, and dual-stream co-training over manipulation and vision-language data.

## Contributions
- A unified alignment framework - canonical masked state-action representation, camera-frame delta pose parameterization, and in-context policy adaptation - that makes large-scale multi-source, cross-embodiment training coherent, with ablations showing naive representations fail to exhibit scaling behavior.
- A scalable multi-source corpus of roughly 38,100 hours assembled from open-source robot datasets, egocentric human videos, and a human-to-robot synthesis pipeline spanning 15 platforms, plus a multi-stage curation pipeline and a curated vision-language mixture.
- New out-of-distribution evaluation standards (RoboTwin-IF for genuine language conditioning and RoboTwin-XE for zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer, alongside LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin-Clean2Rand, RoboCasa365, and EBench), with state-of-the-art results across all of them and real-robot validation on four platforms.

## Experimental Setup
Pretraining uses about 11,420 hours of robot data from nine open datasets (OXE, RoboMIND, DROID, RH20T, AgiBotWorld-Beta, RoboCOIN, RDT, InternData-A1, Galaxea Open-World), about 1,933 hours of egocentric video (EgoDex 732 h, VITRA 247 h, EgoVerse 954 h), and about 24,808 hours of synthesized human-to-robot data. Evaluation contrasts standard in-domain benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboTwin-Easy/Hard) with OOD settings - LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin-Clean2Rand, RoboCasa365, EBench, the new RoboTwin-IF instruction-following benchmark, and RoboTwin-XE cross-embodiment transfer - against pi0, pi0.5, StarVLA, GR00T-N1.5/1.6, RLDX-1, X-VLA, InternVLA-A1, and others. Real-robot validation covers AgileX ALOHA, Franka, UR, and ARX platforms in in-domain, out-of-domain, few-shot adaptation, and zero-shot cross-embodiment settings, plus the RoboChallenge Table30-v1 generalist track.

## Results
In-distribution, Qwen-RobotManip reaches 99.1% on LIBERO, 93.4% on RoboTwin-Easy, and 92.5% on RoboTwin-Hard, with the Context variant at 99.2/93.7/94.0. On OOD settings it reaches 45.6% overall success on EBench (versus 27.1% for pi0.5), 35.9% total on RoboCasa365 (versus 33.2% for RLDX-1, with 68.6% on Atomic and 14.9% on Composite-Unseen where prior models score at most 5.4%), and improves on the previous state of the art by 1.68x on EBench, about 2.76x on RoboCasa365-Unseen, and 1.08x on LIBERO-Plus; instruction following rises from 44 to 79 on Pick, 20 to 71 on Diverse Place, and 57.7 to 75 on Relative Operate, and zero-shot cross-embodiment transfer improves 3.73x on ARX-X5, 2.28x on UR5-WSG, and 5.36x on Franka. The model ranks 1st on RoboChallenge Table30-v1 with a 20% relative improvement. Directly relevant to the data engine, a controlled ablation replacing raw egocentric auxiliary data with pipeline-synthesized robot demonstrations raises RoboTwin-Clean2Rand Hard success from 54.7% (robot-only) and 55.0% (raw ego) to 58.7%.

## Limitations
The authors state that the human-to-robot synthesis pipeline introduces distributional gaps from retargeting approximations and inpainting artifacts that bound the effective quality of synthesized data. The OOD evaluations, though harder than standard benchmarks, remain predominantly simulation-based, and broader real-world evaluation across deployment conditions is needed. The fixed action chunk length and inference latency of the current system also constrain applicability to tasks requiring reactive sub-second control.

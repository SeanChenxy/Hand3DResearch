# Wan: Open and Advanced Large-Scale Video Generative Models

**Authors:** Wan Team, Alibaba Group (75 contributors, listed alphabetically)  
**Date:** 2025-02 (arXiv v1); 2025-04-19 (arXiv v2)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2503.20314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20314)  
**Evidence status:** No Zotero record; the summary was compiled from the full arXiv PDF (v2, 49-page technical report; all core sections read, including model design, benchmarks, ablations, and limitations).

## Summary

Wan is a comprehensive, fully open-source suite of video foundation models built on the mainstream diffusion transformer (DiT) paradigm with flow matching, released in 14B (text-to-video and image-to-video) and 1.3B (text-to-video, 480p/720p) variants. The 14B model, trained on billions of images and videos amounting to O(1) trillion tokens, demonstrates scaling laws of video generation with respect to both data and model size and outperforms open-source models and the state-of-the-art commercial solutions across internal and external benchmarks. Key innovations include a novel spatio-temporal variational autoencoder (Wan-VAE) — a 3D causal VAE with 4x8x8 compression and a feature-cache mechanism supporting arbitrarily long videos, with 2.5x faster reconstruction than the prior SOTA — a DiT design with cross-attention text conditioning via umT5 and parameter-sharing modulation layers (23% fewer parameters at equal performance), a large-scale data pipeline with motion-quality tiering and dedicated visual-text data that makes Wan the first model to generate legible visual text in both Chinese and English, extensive training/inference acceleration (2D context parallelism, diffusion cache 1.62x, FP8 GEMM and 8-bit FlashAttention), and an automated human-aligned evaluation benchmark (Wan-Bench, 14 metrics across dynamic quality, image quality, and instruction following). The 1.3B model needs only 8.19 GB of VRAM for consumer GPUs, and the framework extends to eight downstream tasks including image-to-video, instruction-guided video editing, personalization, real-time streaming generation (8-16 FPS after consistency-model distillation), and video-to-audio generation.

## Background and Problem

Since Sora, video generation has advanced rapidly, but a persistent gap separates open-source models from closed-source leaders in three respects: suboptimal performance (commercial development pace exceeds open-source capacity), limited capabilities (most open foundations handle only basic text-to-video, while real requirements are multifarious), and insufficient efficiency (large models are impractical for resource-limited creative teams). Prior open models also lag in specific competencies such as visual text rendering and multilingual (Chinese) prompt understanding. The report addresses the full stack: data construction, VAE, model architecture, scaling, acceleration, evaluation, and downstream applications, with everything — code, weights, and the complete training methodology — released openly.

## Method

Wan-VAE: a 3D causal VAE compressing a video [1+T, H, W, 3] to latents [1+T/4, H/8, W/8, 16] (4x8x8), with the first frame spatially compressed only (MagViT-v2 style) to handle images. GroupNorm is replaced by RMSNorm to preserve temporal causality and enable a feature-cache mechanism for chunk-wise streaming encode/decode (max 4 frames per chunk, cached frame features from preceding chunks fed into causal convolutions; single-frame cache for 2x temporal downsampling), supporting stable inference on infinite-length videos; the spatial upsampling layer's input channel is halved for 33% memory reduction, yielding a compact 127M model. Training is three-stage: train a 2D image VAE, inflate it into the 3D causal VAE and train on low-resolution 512-frame 128x128 videos with L1+KL+LPIPS (weights 3, 3e-6, 3), then fine-tune on high-resolution variable-duration videos with a GAN loss.

DiT: the Wan-VAE latents are patchified by a 3D convolution with kernel (1,2,2) into (1+T/4)x(H/16)x(W/16) tokens processed by N transformer blocks (self-attention, cross-attention to text, FFN) with timestep embedding via an MLP+SiLU layer predicting six modulation parameters. The modulation parameters are shared across all transformer blocks (each block learns distinct biases), cutting parameters ~23% at equal quality — ablations show full-sharing beats non-sharing with 400M more parameters and matches a deeper 1.5B variant. Text is encoded with umT5 (512 tokens): strongest multilingual (Chinese/English) capability, better composition than unidirectional LLMs, superior convergence at equal scale; a bidirectional token refiner is incorporated following HunyuanVideo's strategy.

Training: flow matching with rectified flow (linear interpolation x_t = t x_1 + (1-t) x_0, velocity target, MSE loss). Pre-training starts on low-resolution 256px text-to-image (joint image+video training of high-resolution long sequences at once starves throughput and destabilizes batch sizes), then staged image-video joint training with a resolution-progressive curriculum: 256px images + 192px 5-second 16-fps videos, then 480px, then 720px. Post-training uses curated high-quality data at 480px/720px. Optimizer: AdamW with bf16 mixed precision, weight decay 1e-3, initial learning rate 1e-4 dynamically reduced on FID/CLIP-score plateaus.

Data pipeline: candidate data from internal and public sources passes a four-step fundamental filtering (OCR text-coverage, LAION-SB aesthetics, NSFW, watermark/logo, black-border, overexposure, synthetic-image, blur detection; duration >= 4s; resolution thresholds per stage), removing ~50%; visual quality via clustering into 100 clusters with expert scoring (1-5); motion quality into six tiers (optimal, medium, static — retained at reduced sampling; camera-driven, low, shaky — excluded). Visual-text data combines hundreds of millions of synthetic Chinese characters on white backgrounds with real-world OCR-derived text-image pairs captioned by Qwen2-VL. Post-training data: expert-based top-20% scoring plus manual collection (millions of images; millions of simple- and complex-motion videos over 12 categories). A dense video caption model (LLaVA-style ViT + 2-layer perception + Qwen LLM; images adaptively pooled to 12x12 grids; video slow-fast encoding at 3 fps up to 129 frames, improving VideoMME 67.6% -> 69.1%) recaptions the corpus, evaluated on ten dimensions against Gemini 1.5 Pro (better on event, camera angle/motion, style, color; comparable elsewhere).

Scaling and efficiency: the DiT accounts for >85% of training compute and attention dominates at long sequences (~1M tokens); training combines FSDP with a 2D Context Parallelism design (Ring Attention as outer layer, Ulysses as inner layer), reducing communication overhead from >10% (Ulysses alone) to <1% at 256K tokens with 16 GPUs; activation offloading plus gradient checkpointing for memory; intelligent cluster scheduling for reliability. Inference reuses the 2D CP design plus FSDP for near-linear multi-GPU scaling, diffusion caching (attention cache + CFG cache with residual compensation) for 1.62x speedup on 14B T2V, FP8 GEMM (per-tensor weights, per-token activations) for 1.13x, and a custom 8-bit FlashAttention (Int8 Q/K, FP8 V, FP8 WGMMA with Float32 cross-block accumulation fused via rescale_o; 95% MFU on NVIDIA H20) for >1.27x. Prompt alignment augments training captions in multiple lengths/styles and rewrites terse user prompts with an LLM (Qwen2.5-Plus) into structured, motion-aware descriptions matching the training caption distribution.

## Contributions

1. An open suite of state-of-the-art video foundation models (14B T2V/I2V; 1.3B T2V needing only 8.19 GB VRAM for consumer GPUs) that surpasses open-source competitors and rivals or beats leading commercial systems, with complete code, weights, and methodology released.
2. Wan-VAE: a compact (127M) 3D causal spatio-temporal VAE with 4x8x8 compression, feature caching for arbitrarily long videos, and 2.5x faster reconstruction than the prior SOTA, plus ablation-backed architecture choices (fully-shared AdaLN, umT5 text encoding).
3. A large-scale automated data construction pipeline with fundamental/visual/motion-quality filtering, a dense video captioner rivaling Gemini 1.5 Pro, and dedicated visual-text data enabling the first video model to generate both Chinese and English visual text.
4. Training and inference acceleration (2D context parallelism, diffusion cache, FP8 GEMM, 8-bit FlashAttention), a human-feedback-weighted automated benchmark (Wan-Bench), and eight downstream applications spanning image-to-video, unified video editing, personalization, camera control, real-time streaming generation (10-20x faster via LCM distillation with the Streamer sliding-window framework; 8 FPS on a single RTX 4090 after int8+TensorRT quantization), and video-to-audio generation.

## Experimental Setup

Wan-Bench: 1,035 prompts scored on three dimensions with 14 metrics — dynamic quality (large-motion via RAFT optical-flow magnitude; human artifacts via a YOLOv3-based detector trained on 20,000 human-annotated AI-generated images; physical plausibility and smoothness via Qwen2-VL with physics-expert prompts; pixel-level stability via optical-flow differences in static regions; ID consistency via DINO features), image quality (MANIQA + LAION aesthetic + MUSIQ; scene consistency/alignment; stylization via Qwen2-VL), and instruction following (object counts and spatial positions, 130 tailored camera-movement prompts, action alignment via Qwen2-VL with keyframes). Dimension weights derive from >5,000 human pairwise comparisons via Pearson correlation with human-rated scores. External evaluation uses VBench (16 dimensions) and a human study of 700+ tasks annotated by 20+ individuals across alignment, image quality, dynamic quality, and overall quality against CN-TopA/B/C and Runway (5,560-5,785 pairwise rounds). VAE comparison: PSNR and frame/latency on 200 videos (720x720, 25 frames) against HunYuanVideo, Mochi, CogVideoX, SVD, Open-Sora-Plan, CVAE, Step Video. Ablations (on the 1.3B model, 200K text-to-image steps at batch 1536): AdaLN sharing configurations (1.3B/1.5B/1.7B variants, latent L2 loss), text encoders (umT5 versus Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, GLM-4-9B, Qwen-VL-7B — FID), and autoencoder (L1-reconstruction VAE versus diffusion-loss VAE-D — FVD).

## Results

- Wan-Bench weighted score: Wan 14B 0.724 (best), Sora 0.700, Wan 1.3B 0.689, CN-TopA 0.690, CN-TopB 0.690, CN-TopC 0.693, Hunyuan 0.673, Mochi 0.639. Wan 14B leads ID consistency (0.946), physical plausibility (0.933), single-object accuracy (0.952), spatial position (0.590); Sora leads large-motion generation (0.482) and artifact suppression (0.786); CN-TopB leads camera control (0.605).
- VBench: Wan 14B 86.22% (visual quality 86.67%, semantic consistency 84.44%) ahead of Sora 84.28%, Wan 1.3B 83.96%, Gen-3 82.32%, CogVideoX1.5-5B 82.17%, Hunyuan open 83.24%, Kling 2024-07 81.85% — the 1.3B variant surpasses HunyuanVideo and Kling 1.0.
- Human evaluation (T2V win rates vs. competitors): versus Runway, Wan 14B wins 48.1% visual quality, 40.3% motion, 69.1% matching, 67.6% overall; versus CN-TopA/B/C overall 44.0%/44.0%/48.9%.
- Wan-VAE: highest PSNR (~37.3 at 720x720/25 frames) with the best efficiency among competing VAEs; qualitative superiority on texture, face, text, and high-motion reconstructions.
- Ablations: fully-shared AdaLN 1.5B attains the lowest training loss (depth of network matters more than AdaLN parameter volume; non-shared 1.7B does not outperform); umT5 clearly beats Qwen2.5-7B and GLM-4-9B (FID 43.01 vs 43.72 and worse), while pre-trained Qwen-VL-7B achieves comparable FID (42.91) at larger model size; the L1-reconstruction VAE beats the diffusion-loss VAE-D (FVD 42.60 vs 44.21 at 10K steps).
- Acceleration: diffusion cache 1.62x, FP8 GEMM 1.13x, 8-bit FlashAttention >1.27x (95% MFU on H20); 2D context parallelism achieves near-linear inference scaling; real-time variant reaches 8-16 FPS after Streamer+LCM integration (10-20x speedup) and 8 FPS on a single 4090 with int8+TensorRT.
- Applications: strong I2V with both 14B and 1.3B; unified instruction-guided video editing and text-to-video editing; personalized video generation; camera-motion controllability; video-to-audio producing stereo 12-second 44.1 kHz soundtracks with better long-term consistency and cleaner audio than MMAudio on matched examples.

## Limitations

The authors themselves note four open problems: preserving fine-grained details under large motion amplitude remains challenging (video fidelity for large-scale motion needs substantial improvement); the computational cost of large-scale models is still prohibitive — unoptimized inference on the 14B model takes about 30 minutes on a single high-end GPU on a single clip; and as a foundational model Wan still lacks domain-specific expertise, with performance insufficient in localized vertical scenarios such as education and medicine. The V2A extension cannot generate human vocal sounds (laughter, crying, speech) because speech-related vocal data was deliberately excluded from training. The quantization path introduces measurable side effects — int8 + TensorRT FP8 quantization can cause slight deviations (artifacts, flickering, temporal incoherence) even when built-in error checking passes — and the reported human evaluation compares against a limited competitor set that does not include all frontier systems.

# DynamiCrafter: Animating Open-domain Images with Video Diffusion Priors

**Authors:** Jinbo Xing, Menghan Xia, Yong Zhang, Haoxin Chen, Wangbo Yu, Hanyuan Liu, Xintao Wang, Tien-Tsin Wong, Ying Shan (The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Tencent AI Lab; Peking University)  
**Date:** 2023-10 (arXiv v1); 2023-11-27 (arXiv v2); published at ICLR 2024  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2310.12190](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12190)  
**Evidence status:** No Zotero record; the summary was compiled from the full arXiv PDF (v2, 9 pages, all sections and tables).

## Summary

DynamiCrafter animates an arbitrary open-domain still image into a short video that inherits the image's visual content while exhibiting plausible natural dynamics, by leveraging the generative prior of a pre-trained text-to-video (T2V) diffusion model. Its core observation is that image animation is a special case of image-conditioned video generation with high visual conformity, which prior attempts (VideoComposer, I2VGen-XL) handle poorly — abrupt temporal changes or loss of input details — because their visual injection is not comprehensive enough. The method proposes a dual-stream image injection paradigm: (1) a text-aligned context representation, produced by a lightweight learnable query transformer that translates the full patch-level features of a pre-trained CLIP image encoder into the T2V model's text embedding space, fused through gated dual cross-attention so the model digests the image content "in a compatible fashion"; and (2) visual detail guidance (VDG), which concatenates the full-resolution image with the initial noise to feed precise visual details directly. A dedicated three-stage training paradigm progressively adapts the context network from a T2I to a T2V model, fine-tunes only the context branch and spatial layers to preserve the pre-trained temporal prior, and jointly trains with VDG using decoupled random-frame conditioning. On zero-shot UCF-101 and MSR-VTT image-to-video evaluation, DynamiCrafter outperforms open-source competitors in visual quality and input conformity, and a user study shows preference rates comparable to or better than the proprietary Pikalabs and Gen-2, alongside pioneer demonstrations of text-based motion control for open-domain animation.

## Background and Problem

Image animation — converting a static image into a dynamic video while preserving its appearance — has been studied mostly for stochastic motions (fluids, natural scenes) or specific categories (human hair, bodies, portraits) using physical simulation, GAN perturbation, learned implicit motion fields, or video prediction, all of which impose strong assumptions that limit open-domain applicability. Recent text-to-video diffusion models carry diverse dynamic visual content, and conditioning them on an image seems natural, but the task is non-trivial: the injection mechanism must both understand the image context (for dynamics synthesis) and preserve its visual details. Existing image-conditioned video diffusion models underperform on exactly these two fronts — their context understanding is insufficient (yielding frames that deviate from the input or temporally incoherent content) and their detail preservation lossy (blur or drift from the initial frame). The paper formulates open-domain animation as T2V with tailored designs for semantic understanding and conformity, and asks how a T2V prior can be exploited without disturbing it.

## Method

Text-aligned context representation: rather than feeding only the CLIP global token (semantic-level, incomplete), the model extracts the last-layer ViT features of all image patches from the pre-trained CLIP image encoder and maps them, via a learnable lightweight query transformer P (N stacked blocks of cross-attention, self-attention, and feed-forward layers, adopting the query-transformer design from multi-modal representation learning), into the T2V model's text embedding space. The resulting context embedding F_ctx and text embedding F_txt interact with U-Net intermediate features through dual cross-attention layers whose outputs are fused with a learned coefficient λ: F_DUI = Softmax(QK_txtᵀ/√d)V_txt + λ·Softmax(QK_ctxᵀ/√d)V_ctx. Analysis of the learned λ shows it concentrates at intermediate U-Net layers (7-9 of 12), which associate with object shapes and poses — so rich-context information mainly influences these layers and preserves shape, while artificially raising λ at intermediate layers causes shape distortion.

Visual detail guidance (VDG): since the CLIP encoder cannot preserve every pixel detail, the conditional image is concatenated with the initial noise and fed to the denoising U-Net directly, supplying frame-aligned low-level information that avoids the spatial misalignment risk of injecting the image into the random noise space.

Training paradigm (three stages): (i) train the context network P on a lightweight T2I model (Stable Diffusion v2.1) with new cross-attention layers — 1000K steps, learning rate 1e-4, batch 64; (ii) adapt P to the T2V model (VideoCrafter, 256×256) by fine-tuning P and the spatial layers only, keeping temporal layers frozen to avoid destroying the pre-trained temporal prior — 30K steps, learning rate 5e-5, batch 64; (iii) joint fine-tuning with VDG, using decoupled training that randomly selects a video frame as the image condition (instead of the fixed first frame) to prevent the network from learning a shortcut that maps the concatenated image to a specific frame location, which would cause "content sudden change" at inference. Data: WebVid-10M, 16 frames per video, dynamic FPS, 256×256. Inference uses a DDIM sampler with multi-condition classifier-free guidance and two adjustable scales s_img and s_txt balancing image and text influence.

Motion control using text: because images imply multiple plausible dynamics, the authors construct a dataset by filtering and re-annotating WebVid10M with ChatGPT/GPT-4 into captions with purer dynamic wording (e.g., "Man doing push-ups") plus category tags, and train DynamiCrafter_CP on it for stronger text-based control over dynamics.

## Contributions

1. An open-domain image animation approach that leverages the video diffusion prior of pre-trained T2V models, significantly outperforming contemporary open-source competitors in visual quality and conformity.
2. A comprehensive analysis of the conditional space of text-to-video diffusion models and a dual-stream image injection paradigm (text-aligned context representation + visual detail guidance) that ensures dynamic content synthesis with visual conformity.
3. A dedicated three-stage training paradigm — T2I pre-adaptation, spatial-layer-only T2V adaptation, and decoupled VDG joint training — that preserves the pre-trained temporal prior while integrating both conditioning streams.
4. A pioneer study of text-based motion control for open-domain image animation via a GPT-re-annotated dynamic-caption dataset (DynamiCrafter_CP), plus demonstrations that the framework extends to storytelling with shots, looping video generation at higher resolution (320×512), and generative frame interpolation.

## Experimental Setup

Zero-shot image-to-video evaluation follows the standard protocol on UCF-101 and MSR-VTT: FVD and KVD for spatial and temporal quality, plus a proposed Perceptual Input Conformity (PIC) metric computed with DreamSim as the distance between the input image and video frames (256×256, 16 frames). Open-source baselines: VideoComposer and I2VGen-XL; proprietary comparisons (Pikalabs, Gen-2, as of Nov 2023) are qualitative. A user study with 49 participants measures preference rates for Motion Quality (M.Q.), Temporal Coherence (T.C.), and selection rate for visual conformity (L.C.). Ablations cover the dual-stream injection (removing context, removing VDG, removing adaptive gating λ) and the training paradigm (one-stage training, fine-tuning the entire network, first-frame conditioning, and DynamiCrafter_CP), with FVD/PIC on MSR-VTT.

## Results

- Quantitative (zero-shot): UCF-101 FID-vid (FVD) 429.23 / KVD 62.47 / PIC 0.6078 versus VideoComposer 576.81/65.56/0.5269 and I2VGen-XL 571.11/58.29/0.5313; MSR-VTT FVD 234.66 / KVD 13.74 / PIC 0.5803 versus VideoComposer 377.29/26.34/0.4460 and I2VGen-XL 289.10/14.70/0.5352 — superior in all metrics except KVD on UCF-101.
- User study (49 participants): M.Q. 38.84% and T.C. 33.14% — far ahead of VideoComposer (2.09%/2.21%) and I2VGen-XL (7.56%/6.51%) and above Pikalabs (28.60%/32.09%) and Gen-2 (22.91%/26.05%); input conformity L.C. 79.88% versus Pikalabs 79.07%, Gen-2 64.77%, I2VGen-XL 15.00%, VideoComposer 18.14%.
- Qualitative: VideoComposer produces consistent but lower-quality frames that deviate from the input; I2VGen-XL resembles the input but loses detail and aesthetic; Gen-2 suffers abrupt content changes ("The Beatles", "Windmill" examples); Pikalabs blurs when producing larger dynamics; DynamiCrafter preserves conformity while allowing text-driven dynamics ("Girl talking").
- Ablation (MSR-VTT FVD/PIC): full method 234.66/0.803; w/o context 372.80/0.4916 (temporal inconsistencies); w/o VDG 150.24/0.6943 — better FVD but severe shape distortion and lost visual details, confirming context carries semantics while VDG carries details; w/o adaptive gating λ 241.38/0.5708; one-stage training converges slowly to coarse-grained context conditioning; fine-tuning the entire network collapses (FVD 364.11, disrupted temporal prior); first-frame conditioning 309.23/0.4573 due to the content-sudden-change shortcut, versus random-frame decoupled conditioning.
- Motion control: DynamiCrafter_CP improves CLIP-Sim between prompt and video from 0.17 to 0.19 and visibly follows prompts ("Man waving hands" vs "Man clapping") where Gen-2 and Pikalabs cannot accept motion control via text.
- Applications: storytelling by animating ChatGPT+DALL-E-3-generated shots; looping video generation by feeding the image as both VDG streams with empty text (320×512); generative frame interpolation by conditioning the first and last frames differently.

## Limitations

The framework is built on and inherits the 256×256 resolution and capacity of VideoCrafter, so fine detail and duration are bounded by the underlying T2V model; evaluation against proprietary systems is qualitative and version-dependent (results as of Nov 2023). VDG alone achieves better FVD but distorts shapes, showing the two streams must be jointly trained — the ablation indicates the learned gating is layer-specific and manually perturbing λ degrades results. Text-based motion control remains preliminary: it relies on a re-annotated dataset of human-centric videos and improves CLIP-Sim only modestly, and ambiguous images still admit multiple interpretations the text prompt may not fully resolve. The decoupled random-frame conditioning prevents first-frame shortcuts but leaves the model's temporal prior sensitive — fine-tuning the entire network destabilizes it, constraining adaptation capacity.

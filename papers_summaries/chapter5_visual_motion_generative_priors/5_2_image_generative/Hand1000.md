# Hand1000: Generating Realistic Hands from Text with Only 1,000 Images

**Authors:** Haozhuo Zhang, Bin Zhu, Yu Cao, Yanbin Hao

## Summary
Hand1000 addresses the persistent anatomical errors that text-to-image models make when generating human hands. It adapts a text-conditioned image generator using a carefully curated set of 1,000 hand images so that prompts describing gestures produce more realistic hand imagery. The paper reports improved realism and gesture matching relative to an unadapted text-to-image model under the evaluated setting. Details such as the complete training schedule and representative metric values are not reported in the available evidence.

## Background and Problem
Hands are difficult for general image generators because many valid poses contain thin structures, self-occlusion, and strict anatomical relationships. The task takes a text prompt describing a desired hand gesture and produces an image of a hand matching that description. The paper focuses on hand-only image generation rather than reconstructing a hand-object scene or predicting a 3D label.

## Method
The method uses a text-to-image diffusion generator as the visual prior and adapts it with a small, curated hand-specific dataset. The 1,000 images are selected to provide gesture, viewpoint, and appearance coverage, and are paired with text descriptions for text-to-hand alignment. The available evidence does not specify enough implementation detail to identify the exact fine-tuning schedule or architecture changes.

## Contributions
- A hand-focused adaptation of text-to-image generation using only 1,000 training images.
- A curated image-and-text resource intended to improve gesture and anatomical fidelity.
- An evaluation showing the value of targeted data for a known failure mode of general image generators.

## Experimental Setup
The paper trains or adapts a text-conditioned generator with 1,000 curated hand images and evaluates generated hands for visual quality and gesture fidelity against an unadapted baseline. Exact evaluation datasets, prompt sets, splits, metrics, and baseline checkpoints are not reported in the paper evidence available here.

## Results
Under the reported evaluation, Hand1000 produces more realistic hands and better matches target gestures than the vanilla text-to-image baseline. The available evidence does not provide representative FID, gesture-accuracy, or human-study values, so no numerical comparison is stated.

## Limitations
The method's diversity is bounded by the coverage of the 1,000-image set and by the underlying generator. Performance on extreme poses, rare appearances, or hand-object interactions is not established in the paper. The effect of alternative curation choices and training schedules is also not reported in the available evidence.

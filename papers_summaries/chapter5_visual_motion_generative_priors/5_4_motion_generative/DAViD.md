# DAViD: Modeling Dynamic Affordance of 3D Objects Using Pre-trained Video Diffusion Models

**Authors:** Hyeonwoo Kim, Sangwon Baik, Hanbyul Joo  
**Date:** 2025-01-14  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2501.08333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08333)  
**Zotero item:** `M26P4YZU` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/M26P4YZU))

## Summary

DAViD learns object-specific *dynamic* affordance — how humans and objects move together over time during interaction — from synthetically generated 4D HOI samples instead of expensive motion-capture datasets. A pre-trained video diffusion model generates 2D HOI videos of a given 3D object, which are lifted to 4D human-object motion pairs; a LoRA-adapted human motion diffusion model plus a conditioned object-pose diffusion model then generate full 4D HOI motions, outperforming OMOMO and CHOIS on contact quality.

## Background and Problem

Prior affordance modeling mostly captures static HOI patterns such as contact regions or spatial relations, while dynamic patterns — the characteristic movement of humans and objects over time (e.g., pushing a shopping cart forward rather than lifting its handle) — remain underexplored. Existing dynamic HOI methods rely on lab-captured 3D datasets (e.g., GRAB, BEHAVE, InterCap) covering few object categories. The paper defines Dynamic Affordance as the object-specific, time-varying human and object motion patterns during HOI and asks how to learn it across diverse, unbounded object categories without real 3D capture; its key insight is that pre-trained video generation models already encode plausible dynamic object usage, so synthetic videos can substitute for captured 4D HOI data.

## Method

The pipeline has two stages. (1) **4D HOI sample generation:** a 3D object mesh is rendered from multiple viewpoints (chosen by object type, inferred via a GPT-4o query on whether the object stays on the ground while used); a ControlNet-conditioned image diffusion model synthesizes a human interacting with the rendered object plus background; low-quality images are filtered with roughly three minutes of human effort per category; an image-to-video diffusion model animates the scene; the 2D videos are then lifted to 3D with the world-grounded human mesh recovery method GVHMR, producing 4D human-object motion samples. (2) **DAViD model:** human motions are generated by the pre-trained MDM adapted with a Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) module, treating each object-specific dynamic HOI pattern as a distinct motion concept learnable from limited samples; object motions are then synthesized by a score-based diffusion model over 9-DoF object poses (GenPose architecture) conditioned on the generated human motion. Inference uses Temporal Guidance Sampling (TGS), adding smoothness and contact (Chamfer-distance proximity of human and object vertices) guidance to a Probability Flow ODE solved with RK45. Separately trained LoRAs can be merged by weighted summation to combine multiple HOI concepts.

## Contributions

1. DAViD, a generative motion model that learns dynamic, object-specific affordance covering both static (contact, spatial) and temporal HOI patterns. 2. A pipeline that synthesizes diverse 4D HOI training samples for a target object mesh by combining pre-trained video diffusion generation with 3D lifting. 3. A LoRA-based concept-learning scheme that merges newly learned HOI motion concepts with pre-trained human motions (and even multiple LoRAs together), enabling novel HOI motion generation without full-model fine-tuning.

## Experimental Setup

Qualitative evaluation uses 30 3D object meshes from BEHAVE, InterCap, ShapeNet, SAPIEN, the FullBodyManipulation Dataset, and SketchFab. Quantitative evaluation uses the FullBodyManipulation Dataset (OMOMO) with sequences cropped from contact start to end. Baselines are OMOMO, CHOIS, and MDM; because baselines are conditioned on object motion or waypoints while DAViD is conditioned on human motion, comparison is restricted to contact precision, recall, and F1 (contact defined when the mean distance of 25 SMPL-X hand joints to object vertices is below 0.05), plus Foot Sliding and foot height (cm). Ablations measure object-motion jitter and FID/diversity on HumanML3D.

## Results

Against the CHOIS setting, DAViD reaches contact F1 of 0.634 versus 0.330 (precision 0.850 vs 0.760; recall 0.517 vs 0.221); against the OMOMO setting, F1 of 0.616 versus 0.552, with lower Foot Sliding and foot height than the baselines (foot height 2.30-2.35 versus 2.58-3.47). TGS ablations show the smoothness guidance cuts object-motion jitter from about 2.95e3 to 30.9, close to the real-capture level of 10.7, and contact guidance is crucial for fine hand contact. LoRA ablation on HumanML3D shows the LoRA module preserves pre-trained MDM knowledge (FID 1.30, diversity 8.90) compared with full fine-tuning (FID 2.93, diversity 8.59); real data show FID 0.00160 and diversity 9.48.

## Limitations

The paper has no dedicated limitations section. It acknowledges that learning from generated data raises reliability concerns relative to real-world data, and its pipeline still requires a human image-filtering pass (about three minutes per category). Its 3D lifting relies on GVHMR, which the authors state can fail to predict plausible camera motion, requiring a TRAM fallback. Because baselines use different conditioning, the quantitative comparison is limited to contact-quality metrics, and quantitative evaluation covers only the FullBodyManipulation Dataset while the broader 30-object evaluation is qualitative.

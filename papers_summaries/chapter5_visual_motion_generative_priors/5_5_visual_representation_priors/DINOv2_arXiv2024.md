# DINOv2: Learning Robust Visual Features without Supervision

## Summary

DINOv2 is a family of self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViT-S/14, ViT-B/14, ViT-L/14, ViT-g/14, the last with 1.1B parameters) trained on LVD-142M, a purpose-built curated dataset of 142 million images produced by an automatic retrieval-and-deduplication pipeline, and published in TMLR in January 2024. Its recipe combines the DINO image-level and iBOT patch-level losses with SwAV-style Sinkhorn-Knopp centering, untied projection heads, a KoLeo feature-spreading regularizer, and a short high-resolution training phase, while systems contributions (custom FlashAttention, sequence packing, efficient stochastic depth, FSDP) make training about 2x faster with 3x less memory than the iBOT implementation. The resulting frozen features surpass the best available general-purpose alternatives, including OpenCLIP, on most image- and pixel-level benchmarks without finetuning, for example 86.5 percent top-1 linear accuracy on ImageNet-1k for ViT-g versus 86.2 for OpenCLIP ViT-G/14, and DINOv2 has become the de facto image backbone of recent monocular hand-object reconstruction systems, serving as the image encoder in HORT, the per-frame feature extractor in ForeHOI, and the frozen RGB encoder in HUG.

## Background and Problem

The paper pursues NLP-style foundation models for vision: features that work out of the box, across image distributions and tasks, at both image level (classification) and pixel level (segmentation, depth). Text-guided pretraining is limited because captions only approximate the information in images, complex pixel-level signals may not surface through textual supervision, and aligned image-text corpora are required, removing the flexibility to learn from raw data. Self-supervised learning can capture image- and pixel-level information, but most advances were developed on the small, curated ImageNet-1k, and attempts to scale on uncurated data cause significant feature-quality drops because data quality and diversity are uncontrolled. The central question is whether self-supervised pretraining on a large quantity of curated data can produce general-purpose frozen features competitive with weakly supervised ones, and the technical focus is on stabilizing and accelerating discriminative self-supervised learning at scale.

## Method

The data pipeline builds LVD-142M without metadata or text. Curated seed sources (ImageNet-22k, the ImageNet-1k train split, Google Landmarks, and several fine-grained datasets) are embedded together with 1.2 billion deduplicated web images using a self-supervised ViT-H/16 pretrained on ImageNet-22k; the uncurated pool is clustered with k-means, and for each curated query image the pipeline retrieves typically 4 nearest neighbors (or samples from the matching cluster for small queries), with near-duplicates of benchmark test sets removed via a copy-detection pipeline; Faiss indices on 20 nodes of 8 V100-32GB GPUs produce the dataset in under two days. The training objective is a cross-entropy loss between student and teacher class tokens over different crops (DINO) plus a masked-patch prediction loss (iBOT), with the teacher built as an exponential moving average; at scale the authors find that untying the DINO and iBOT head weights, contrary to iBOT's shared heads, works better, they replace softmax centering with 3-iteration Sinkhorn-Knopp normalization, and they add the KoLeo regularizer, which encourages a uniform span of l2-normalized features within a batch, plus a short phase at 518 by 518 resolution at the end of pretraining. Efficiency work includes a custom FlashAttention implementation (motivating a ViT-g with embedding dimension 1536 and 24 heads of 64), sequence packing that forwards large and small crops as one block-diagonally masked sequence, stochastic depth that skips computation of dropped residuals at a 0.4 drop rate, and FSDP with float16 backbone communication that roughly halves communication cost versus DDP. Smaller models are not trained from scratch but distilled from the frozen ViT-g teacher using the same loop without masking or stochastic depth and with the iBOT loss applied to the two global crops, evaluating the EMA student. All models train for 625k iterations with AdamW, a 100k-iteration warmup, and batch sizes of 2048 for distilled models and 3072 for ViT-g.

## Contributions

The paper delivers four coupled contributions. First, an automatic, supervision-free data curation pipeline showing that curated, diverse data, rather than raw uncurated scale, is what lets self-supervised features generalize: ViT-g trained on LVD-142M beats the same model trained on ImageNet-22k on every benchmark except ImageNet-1k and beats a model trained on 142M randomly sampled uncurated images on all seven probes, including domains not used for curation such as iNaturalist and Places205. Second, a set of training-recipe and systems improvements that make discriminative self-supervised training stable and cheap at 1B-parameter scale, about 2x faster and using a third of the memory of iBOT. Third, a distillation procedure under which smaller students outperform from-scratch training of the same architecture on all twelve evaluation benchmarks, with the distilled ViT-L reaching 86.3 percent ImageNet-1k linear accuracy versus 84.5 from scratch. Fourth, an extensive evaluation establishing that frozen DINOv2 features match or beat the best openly available weakly supervised features at image and pixel level, together with emerging properties such as unsupervised foreground-background separation, cross-image part correspondence from PCA on patch features, and patch matching across domains, poses, and styles.

## Experimental Setup

Frozen-feature evaluation covers ImageNet-1k (linear and k-NN probes), the domain-generalization sets ImageNet-A, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-Sketch, and ImageNet-C, fine-grained classification (iNaturalist 2018 and 2021, Places205, and the 12 SimCLR transfer benchmarks including Food-101, Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircraft, and CUB), instance retrieval (Oxford, Paris, Met, AmsterTime), semantic segmentation (ADE20k, CityScapes, Pascal VOC, with linear and multiscale protocols), monocular depth estimation (NYUd, KITTI, and zero-shot NYUd to SUN RGB-D transfer with linear, multi-layer, and DPT-decoder probes), and video classification (Kinetics-400, UCF-101, Something-Something v2 from 8 sampled frames). Baselines include self-supervised MAE, DINO, SEERv2, MSN, EsViT, Mugs, and iBOT, and weakly supervised CLIP, OpenCLIP, SWAG, and EVA-CLIP, with OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 as the weakly supervised reference; linear probes are trained on frozen backbones without any backbone finetuning, and the evaluation code is released. Additional analyses include a finetuning sanity check on ImageNet-1k, dataset-size and model-size scaling on ImageNet-22k versus LVD-142M, loss-component ablations, resolution ablations, and fairness studies on Dollar Street and Casual Conversations.

## Results

With frozen features and a linear probe, DINOv2 ViT-g/14 reaches 86.5 percent top-1 on ImageNet-1k (ViT-L/14 86.3, ViT-B/14 84.5, ViT-S/14 81.1), a 4.2 point improvement over the previous self-supervised state of the art (iBOT ViT-L/16, 82.3) and above OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 (86.2) and EVA-CLIP ViT-g/14 (86.4), with stronger generalization on ImageNet-V2 (78.4 versus 77.4). Robustness gaps are large: 75.9 on ImageNet-A, 78.8 on ImageNet-R, and 62.5 on Sketch for ViT-g, roughly 29.6, 22.1, and 23.0 points above iBOT. On fine-grained tasks ViT-g attains 81.6 on iNaturalist 2018 and 85.7 on iNaturalist 2021, about 8.6 and 9.7 points above OpenCLIP-G, and on instance retrieval it raises Oxford-Hard mAP by 34 points over OpenCLIP-G. At the pixel level, linear segmentation probes reach 49.0 mIoU on ADE20k (53.0 multiscale), 71.3 on CityScapes, and 83.0 on Pascal VOC, and plugging the frozen backbone into a ViT-Adapter with Mask2Former yields 60.2 mIoU on ADE20k in 28 hours on 16 V100 GPUs, close to the 62.9 state of the art; monocular depth with a DPT decoder on frozen features attains 0.298 RMSE on NYUd and 2.11 on KITTI, and transfers zero-shot from indoor NYUd to outdoor SUN RGB-D at 0.338. On video, frozen features give 78.4 on Kinetics-400 and 91.2 on UCF-101, matching OpenCLIP, and 38.3 on Something-Something v2, 2.5 points above it. The recipe ablation walks from an iBOT reproduction at 74.5 k-NN accuracy to 82.0 for the full DINOv2 recipe, with KoLeo contributing 2.3 points of k-NN accuracy and 8 points of Oxford-M retrieval mAP, and the iBOT masked-image-modeling term contributing nearly 3 points of segmentation mIoU.

## Limitations

The paper's own fairness analyses reveal substantial bias: on Dollar Street, performance drops by 25.7 percent in Africa relative to Europe and by 31.7 percent between high- and low-income households, and the authors explicitly state the model remains biased toward Western countries and wealthy households; the label-association study on Casual Conversations finds no harmful Non-Human or Crime predictions across gender, skin tone, and age groups (beyond two instances with prison-bar-like backgrounds), but the authors caution that more thorough bias evaluations may reveal flaws. The training cost is considerable: a single ViT-g pretraining run consumes about 22,000 A100 GPU-hours (9.7 MWh, an estimated 3.7 tCO2eq), and the whole project is estimated between 0.5k and 1k tCO2eq, on the order of 200,000 GPU-days. Dense-task performance depends on the short high-resolution adaptation phase, and the paper notes that its evaluation of finetuned encoders is deliberately a sanity check without hyperparameter tuning. As future work the authors point to continued scaling of models and data and to grounding language-enabled systems in these visual features; a dedicated limitations section beyond the bias and cost discussion is not reported in the paper.

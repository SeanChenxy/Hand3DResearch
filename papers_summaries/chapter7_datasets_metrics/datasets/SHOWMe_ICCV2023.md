# SHOWMe: Benchmarking Object-agnostic Hand-Object 3D Reconstruction

**Authors:** Anilkumar Swamy, Vincent Leroy, Philippe Weinzaepfel, Fabien Baradel (NAVER LABS Europe; Inria centre at the University Grenoble Alpes)

## Summary
Existing hand-object interaction (HOI) datasets offer limited object variability and typically derive ground-truth hand shapes by fitting the MANO parametric model, which caps reconstruction accuracy. The paper introduces SHOWMe, a benchmark of 96 monocular RGB-D videos (87,540 frames, 15 subjects, 42 everyday objects) in which a hand holds an object rigidly, each sequence annotated with a real, sub-millimeter-precision textured 3D mesh of the hand-object (HO) system registered to every frame. Under this rigidity assumption, a video becomes multiple observations of one static HO system, so reconstruction is cast as a two-stage pipeline: rigid transformation (camera pose) estimation followed by multi-view reconstruction (MVR). The authors benchmark non-trivial baselines for both stages and find that high-quality reconstructions are achievable (Fscore@5mm of 82.2% with ground-truth registration for the strongest MVR baseline), but results with estimated registration drop sharply for textureless objects and heavy hand occlusions, leaving substantial room for improvement.

## Background and Motivation
Joint HO 3D reconstruction from RGB has been addressed mostly from single images, with methods either relying on MANO priors — which cannot represent detailed, unseen hand shapes — or assuming the object template is known a priori, limiting generalization to unseen objects. Multi-camera rigs add deployment complexity, and prior video-based work (HOD/HHOR) was restricted to close-up fingertip grasps of small objects or to known objects. The authors argue that the field lacked both an evaluation protocol and a specifically designed dataset for object-agnostic reconstruction of a rigidly held object from a monocular video. Existing datasets such as HO3D and DexYCB have few objects (10 and 20, both YCB sets) and limited motion patterns; ContactPose uses artificially textureless, marker-equipped objects with MANO-fit hands; HOD provides unregistered scans for only a subset of objects. SHOWMe is presented as the first dataset providing real ground-truth 3D shapes at this accuracy level for both hand and object.

## Dataset Construction
- **Source and capture:** Each subject grasps one object in one of three evenly represented grasp types — power-grasp, use-grasp, or handover-grasp — and shows every part of the grasp to the camera while wearing a distinctive sleeve (to ease arm segmentation; no other body parts visible). Videos are recorded with a single Intel RealSense L515 RGB-D camera (up to 30 fps, 1280x720, pixel-aligned and synchronized streams). Immediately after recording, the same static HO configuration is scanned with an Artec Eva 3D scanner (resolution up to 0.1 mm, accuracy 0.2 mm).
- **Scale and composition:** 96 sequences, 87,540 frames, 15 subjects diverse in gender, skin color, and hand shape, 42 everyday objects of varied sizes, shapes, and textures. Average sequence length is 48 seconds. Across ground-truth meshes, vertex counts range from 4K to 263K and face counts from 7.5K to 524K. Twenty interactions can be classified into 20 of the 33 classes of the Feix et al. grasp taxonomy.
- **Annotations:** The scanned textured HO mesh is registered to every frame in two steps: robust ICP against masked depth point clouds (first frame manually initialized, subsequent frames initialized from the previous result), followed by refinement with a differentiable rendering pipeline that optimizes per-frame pose corrections and mesh appearance with temporal smoothness and weight-decay regularizers. This refinement improved annotated poses in 47 of the 96 sequences. Additionally, object-only scans registered together with the MANO hand model to the HO meshes yield independent MANO pose/shape annotations for hand and object, with a sub-millimeter residual registration error, enabling benchmarking of hand pose estimators.

## Evaluation Protocol
The task is object-agnostic reconstruction of the HO system from a monocular RGB video, decomposed into two benchmarked stages. Stage 1, rigid transformation estimation, is evaluated either via the generic SfM toolbox COLMAP (using silhouettes to filter background keypoints) or via hand pose as a proxy: an off-the-shelf estimator (Minimal Hand, Frankmocap, HandOccNet, or DOPE, with variants adding fixed median pose, median filtering, or PoseBERT smoothing) supplies 3D keypoints, from which relative frame transforms are recovered by PnP plus Procrustes alignment. Stage 1 metrics: hand MPJPE and PA-MPJPE (mm), PCK (30 mm threshold), geodesic rotation error (degrees), translation error (MSE), and detection rate. Stage 2, MVR, is evaluated with visual-hull silhouette reconstruction, fast differentiable rendering (FDR), and HHOR (NeuS-based implicit surface with camera refinement), plus single-image IHOI for reference, under both ground-truth and estimated rigid transformations. Stage 2 metrics: reconstruction rate, accuracy and completeness (cm), accuracy/completeness ratios at 5 mm, and Fscore@5mm, computed after Procrustes alignment (rotation, translation, scale) to the GT scans. Evaluation is reported over the dataset's sequences; the paper does not define a train/test split, as the benchmark evaluates reconstruction methods rather than training them.

## Findings and Analysis
- With ground-truth rigid transforms, all three MVR baselines exceed 60% Fscore@5mm: HHOR 82.2 (acc 0.34 cm, comp 0.31 cm, rec. rate 98.9%), FDR 73.5, VH 63.6. Single-image IHOI reaches only 39.3 Fscore@5mm despite near-perfect hand-joint alignment.
- Reconstruction quality collapses when registration is estimated: FDR drops from 73.5 to 37.6 Fscore@5mm with COLMAP poses and to 20.0 with DOPE poses; COLMAP+HHOR reaches 54.2 with a reconstruction rate of only 72.9%.
- COLMAP yields the best rigid transforms on average (rotation error 14.6°, translation 0.06) but a 78.2% detection rate, failing completely on roughly 20% of sequences; hand-based estimates (DOPE: 21.0°, 0.15-0.17, 99-100% detection) are more reliable overall and embed approximate metric scale.
- Object size interacts with method choice: COLMAP+FDR scores 47.93 Fscore@5mm on larger objects versus 30.23 on small ones, while DOPE+FDR is better on small objects (23.43 versus 17.85), since small objects offer few SfM features but less hand occlusion.
- Texture matters for SfM: COLMAP+HHOR Fscore@5mm is 49.47 on textured versus 34.46 on less-textured objects, with much higher variance for less-textured ones. HHOR's camera refinement corrects noisy COLMAP poses (a 16.6% Fscore boost over FDR with estimated poses versus 9% with GT poses) but costs about 1 GPU-day per sequence versus under a minute for FDR.

## Contributions
1. The SHOWMe dataset: 96 RGB-D videos of rigid hand-object configurations with real, sub-millimeter-precision textured 3D meshes of both hand and object registered to every frame, plus MANO and semantic grasp annotations — the first HOI dataset with ground-truth 3D shape at this accuracy for both hand and object.
2. An automatic annotation pipeline (ICP plus differentiable-rendering refinement) for registering scanned meshes to monocular RGB-D sequences.
3. A two-stage benchmark (rigid registration then multi-view reconstruction) with carefully evaluated baselines, showing that current object-agnostic video reconstruction is highly sensitive to registration quality and fails for textureless objects and heavy occlusions.

## Limitations
The paper's stated limitation is the rigidity assumption: the hand pose remains constant with respect to the object throughout each sequence, which excludes dynamic hand-object manipulation scenarios and confines the benchmark to showing one static HO configuration, though the authors argue this is a reasonable step toward dynamic object-agnostic reconstruction for applications such as object hand-over and object scanning. The experiments additionally expose that reconstruction quality hinges on initial camera pose estimates, which are unreliable for textureless objects and heavy hand occlusions, and that the strongest implicit baseline (HHOR) is computationally expensive.

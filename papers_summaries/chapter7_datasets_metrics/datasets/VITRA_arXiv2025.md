# Scalable Vision-Language-Action Model Pretraining for Robotic Manipulation with Real-Life Human Activity Videos

**Authors:** Qixiu Li, Yu Deng, Yaobo Liang, Lin Luo
**Date:** 2025 (arXiv)
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2510.21571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21571)
**Zotero item:** `9GNQRWJ2` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/9GNQRWJ2))
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.

## Summary
Robotic VLA pretraining data is scarce, laboratory-bound, and especially thin for dexterous hands, while unscripted real-life human videos are abundant but unstructured. This paper introduces a fully automatic pipeline that converts arbitrary human hand activity videos into atomic-level VLA episodes aligned with robot data conventions — language instruction, video frames, and framewise 3D hand and camera motion — by combining 3D hand reconstruction and SLAM, wrist-speed-minima action segmentation, and GPT-4.1 instruction captioning with trajectory overlays. Processing raw videos from Ego4D, Epic-Kitchen, EgoExo4D, and Something-Something-V2 yields a hand-VLA training dataset of 1M episodes and 26M frames (the dataset behind the VITRA project; the paper text itself calls it "our hand V-L-A dataset" and never uses the acronym). Pretraining a PaliGemma-2 plus diffusion-action-expert VLA model on this data yields strong zero-shot hand action prediction in unseen environments, and after fine-tuning on 1.2K teleoperated robot trajectories the model reaches a 71.0% average success rate on seen real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks and 64.6% on unseen ones.

## Background and Motivation
Existing manipulation V-L-A data is teleoperated in lab settings, limiting scale and diversity in skills, object categories, and scenes, and no large-scale dexterous hand action dataset exists for pretraining. Human videos could close this gap but are unscripted, unsegmented, noisy, and lack language and 3D action labels; prior approaches either learn only representations or latent actions (which provide no explicit 3D supervision) or, in concurrent works with 3D hand labels, rely on scripted laboratory captures with privileged sensing. The paper targets two alignments with robot V-L-A data: task alignment (segmenting atomic short-horizon actions) and label alignment (metric 3D hand motion plus precise instructions from single, uncalibrated, possibly moving cameras).

## Dataset Construction
The dataset is built by a three-stage automatic analysis of raw egocentric videos, with no use of the source datasets' human annotations. Stage 1 (3D motion labeling): static-versus-moving camera detection via background optical flow, intrinsics estimation (DroidCalib for moving cameras; MoGe-2 and DeepCalib for static cameras) and undistortion, framewise camera-space hand reconstruction with HaWoR (6D wrist pose plus MANO joint angles), and camera pose tracking with a modified MegaSAM whose depth prior is replaced by MoGe-2; world-frame hand trajectories are spline-smoothed and outlier-removed, and can be re-expressed in any frame's camera space. Long videos are chopped into overlapping 20-second clips. Stage 2 (atomic action segmentation): local minima of world-space 3D wrist speed serve as cutting points, segmenting each hand independently without extra model inference; over-segmented repetitive actions can be merged after captioning. Stage 3 (instruction labeling): 8 evenly sampled frames per clip with projected 3D hand-palm trajectories overlaid are fed to GPT-4.1, prompted to output an imperative action description or "N/A" for non-manipulation clips. The resulting dataset aggregates 1M episodes and 26M frames, 77% from Ego4D, 12% from Epic-Kitchen, 6% from EgoExo4D, and 5% from SSV2, spanning cooking, cleaning, construction, repairing, crafting, and painting. Episodes follow robot-data conventions: instructions formatted as "Left hand: <action>. Right hand: <action>.", with framewise relative wrist translation and rotation and 15 MANO joint-angle actions in the observation camera frame. Diversity analyses against OpenImages (DINOv2 feature similarity, R@0.5) and GPT-4.1-extracted noun/verb/adjective statistics (h-index and i100-index) show higher visual and linguistic coverage than EgoDex, OXE, DROID, and AgiBot World beta. Project website: https://microsoft.github.io/VITRA/.

## Evaluation Protocol
Zero-shot hand action prediction benchmark: (1) Grasping — RGB-D images from 47 unseen environments captured with Azure Kinect, 396 annotated objects with captions and segmented 3D point clouds; synthetic hands are rendered at graspable distances and the metric is the minimum distance between predicted finger trajectories and target object points (d_hand-obj, cm). (2) General action — 117 unseen real-life environments captured with mobile phones; 23 participants rank the top-3 of rendered predicted actions for 30 randomly selected scenes, scored 3/2/1, reporting average user score. Robot evaluation: a Realman arm with a 12-DoF XHand dexterous hand and RealSense head camera; 1.2K teleoperated trajectories (teleoperation arms plus MANUS gloves) are collected for four tasks — general pick and place, functional grasping (e.g., by the handle), pouring, and sweeping. Success rates are reported over 40/24/8/8 trials on seen objects and backgrounds, and on unseen objects and backgrounds (16/16/8 trials) plus unseen categories (24 pick-and-place trials). Baselines fine-tuned on the same robot data include VPP, pi0, no VLA pretraining, latent-action pretraining (LAPA), and OXE pretraining; ablations cover pretraining data (EgoDex, human annotations, Being-H0), segmentation and captioning strategies, augmentation, attention type, and data scale (50%, 20%, 10%, 1%).

## Findings and Analysis
Zero-shot grasping in unseen environments: the full model attains an average/median d_hand-obj of 8.8/6.2 cm versus 20.0/20.0 cm for the initial hand position and 19.1/18.4 cm for Being-H0 (8B); general-action user score is 1.91 versus 0.15 for Being-H0. Pretraining on EgoDex lab data reaches only 17.6/18.3 cm, and using the source datasets' own human annotations degrades to 14.1/14.1 cm with user score 0.96, confirming the value of automatic atomic segmentation. Removing trajectory-aware augmentation raises the error to 11.6/10.7 cm, and bidirectional attention degrades it to 9.3/7.2 cm versus causal attention. On a 350K-episode subset, fixed-interval 1-second segmentation (10.5/8.8 cm) and captioning without trajectory overlay (11.7/10.7 cm) both underperform the pipeline (9.9/8.1 cm), validating 3D-trajectory-guided episode construction. Grasping error decreases approximately linearly on the log scale from 1% to 100% of the data; EgoDex, despite more episodes and 130M frames versus 2.6M, underperforms even the model trained on 10% of this dataset. Real-robot results: on seen tasks the fine-tuned model averages 71.0% success (pick and place 80.0%, functional grasp 66.7%, pour 75.0%, sweep 62.5%) versus pi0 at 46.9%, VPP at 24.8%, no pretraining at 32.1%, latent-action pretraining at 46.0%, and OXE pretraining at 41.3%. On unseen settings it averages 64.6% (68.8% pick and place with unseen objects and background, 68.8% functional grasp, 50.0% pour, and 70.8% pick and place with unseen categories) versus pi0's 16.1%, while latent-action pretraining drops to 0.0%. Robot success rates correlate positively with pretraining hand-prediction accuracy, suggesting the hand benchmark is a proxy for downstream robot performance.

## Contributions
- A fully automatic holistic human activity analysis framework (3D motion labeling, speed-minima atomic segmentation, trajectory-overlay GPT captioning) that converts unscripted human videos into robot-aligned VLA episodes without human annotation.
- A hand-VLA pretraining dataset of 1M episodes and 26M frames with framewise 3D hand and camera motion and natural-language instructions, of higher measured visual and linguistic diversity than existing robot and human VLA datasets.
- A dexterous hand VLA architecture (PaliGemma-2 backbone with DiT action expert, unified single/dual-hand masking, causal action denoising) plus trajectory-aware augmentation.
- A zero-shot hand action prediction benchmark (47 grasping environments, 396 objects; 117 general-action environments with a 23-person user study), and real-robot demonstrations that human-video pretraining lifts unseen-task success from 16.1% to 64.6% over robot-data-only pretraining, with clear data-scaling behavior.

## Limitations
The pipeline inherits inaccuracies from current 3D reconstruction algorithms and VLM captioning, and the authors plan additional filtering; some noise in 3D labels is accepted as tolerable for pretraining. Data construction and training target short-horizon atomic skills, leaving long-horizon planning and higher-level task structures to future work. Robotic experiments focus on single-handed tasks with only a preliminary bimanual hand-over demonstration, and multi-view or tactile inputs are not yet integrated. The dataset is currently drawn from four existing egocentric corpora rather than newly collected footage, and pretraining accuracy on 3D motion is bounded by single-camera monocular estimation quality.

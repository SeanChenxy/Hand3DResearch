# RH20T: A Comprehensive Robotic Dataset for Learning Diverse Skills in One-Shot

## Summary

RH20T is a large-scale robotic manipulation dataset of more than 110K robot episodes paired with more than 110K corresponding human demonstrations, totaling over 50M frames and about 20TB of data across 147 tasks and 42 skill verbs. Seven robot configurations spanning multiple arms, grippers, force/torque sensors, and tactile skins record RGB, depth, audio, joint states, and force/torque signals, and every robot episode is paired with a human demonstration of the same task to support learning from human video. The paper also introduces a haptic-feedback teleoperation interface for efficient collection and an ACT-based baseline study showing that pretraining on a few same- or similar-task demonstrations improves one-shot generalization.

## Background and Motivation

The authors motivate RH20T by the scarcity of diverse, real-robot manipulation data at the scale needed for general robot learning. Existing collections either cover few tasks, few robot embodiments, or lack paired human demonstrations, which prevents exploiting human videos as a supervision source. They emphasize "one-shot" learning: a robot should accomplish a novel task with at most a handful of demonstrations, which demands data diversity across tasks, skills, scenes (via more than 50 different table covers), and agents (humans and robots), as well as multi-modal sensing beyond vision.

## Dataset Construction

Episodes were contributed by tens of volunteers operating seven robot configurations, including Flexiv, UR5, Franka, and Kuka arms with four different grippers, three force/torque sensor setups (including the ATI Axia80-M20 and OptoForce sensors), and a uSkin tactile sensor on one configuration. Each episode records synchronized RGB at 1280 x 720 and 10 Hz, depth, binocular infrared video, joint angles and torques at 100 Hz, gripper pose and width at 10 Hz, six-axis force/torque at 100 Hz, audio at 30 Hz, and tactile readings (2 x 16 x 3 taxels at 200 Hz on the tactile-equipped configuration). Collection uses a haptic teleoperation system that renders contact forces back to the operator, enabling more than one hour of operation after less than one hour of training; operators rate each episode from 0 to 9, where 0 marks emergency stops and 1 marks failures, yielding roughly a 10:1 ratio of successful to failed episodes. Tasks comprise 48 RLBench-based, 29 MetaWorld-based, and 70 self-proposed real tasks (147 total) labeled with 42 skill verbs. The data are organized in a tree hierarchy so that each robot manipulation episode is linked to a human demonstration of the same task, producing dense human-robot paired data, and the dataset also contains longer compositional sequences that chain multiple skills.

## Evaluation Protocol

The baseline study trains ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers) in a one-shot imitation setting: for each evaluated task, 75 fresh demonstrations are collected as the base training set, and pretraining variants add 335 same-task demonstrations or 195 demonstrations drawn from three similar tasks. Policies are evaluated over 20 trials per task (10 trials for generalization tests) using staged success metrics covering reach, grasp, and place subgoals. Generalization is probed with unseen objects and unseen table covers, and the effect of demonstration quantity is measured by varying the number of pretraining episodes.

## Findings and Analysis

Pretraining on a small number of demonstrations before the one-shot fine-tuning consistently improves success over training on the 75-episode base set alone, with gains already visible from 10 to 40 pretraining episodes, demonstrating that even modest amounts of task-relevant data improve one-shot performance. Policies pretrained on demonstrations of similar tasks transfer to the target task, indicating that skill-level similarity, not just task identity, supplies useful priors. On generalization trials, exposure to more diverse objects and scenes during pretraining improves robustness to novel objects and novel table covers, supporting the paper's diversity-centric design. The paired human demonstrations are presented as the key resource for scaling, since human data can be collected at far higher volume than robot rollouts.

## Contributions

The largest real-robot manipulation dataset at publication time in terms of episodes and tasks (110K+ episodes, 147 tasks, 42 skills, 7 configurations, about 20TB); systematic multi-modal sensing including force/torque, tactile, and audio alongside multi-camera RGB-D; dense pairing of every robot episode with a human demonstration; a haptic teleoperation interface with episode quality ratings; and a one-shot imitation baseline protocol with staged success metrics and generalization tests.

## Limitations

The authors state that data collection remains expensive despite the teleoperation interface, so the human-to-robot demonstration ratio is bounded by hardware availability. Due to compute limits, large foundation models were not evaluated on RH20T, leaving the value of the full corpus for such models unquantified. The dataset is collected with teleoperated single-arm configurations and grippers rather than dual-arm or dexterous hands, which the authors list as future extensions.

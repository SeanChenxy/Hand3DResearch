# GigaHands: A Massive Annotated Dataset of Bimanual Hand Activities

**Authors:** Rao Fu, Dingxi Zhang, Alex Jiang, Wanjia Fu, Austin Funk, Daniel Ritchie, Srinath Sridhar  
**Date:** 2024-12-05  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2412.04244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04244)  
**Zotero item:** `IAPHIEFL` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/IAPHIEFL))  
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.  

## Summary

GigaHands (CVPR 2025 Highlight) is, to the authors' knowledge, the largest annotated 3D bimanual hand activity dataset: 34 hours (2,034 minutes) of hand-object interactions, gestures, and self-contacts from 56 subjects manipulating 417 real objects, captured markerlessly with 51 RGB cameras, yielding 183 million frames, 13.9k motion sequences refined into 14k motion clips, 3.7M bimanual 3D hand poses, and 84k text annotations covering 1,467 verbs. A procedural instruct-to-annotate pipeline keeps annotation effort low, and experiments on text-driven motion synthesis, motion captioning, and dynamic radiance field reconstruction show that the dataset's scale and diversity directly improve downstream model performance.

## Background and Motivation

Large models in language, vision, and robotics are trained on massive corpora, but no comparably scaled dataset exists for natural hand activities, which blocks progress on bimanual action understanding, motion generation, and robot learning. The two standard acquisition routes both fail at scale: in-the-wild video (egocentric or internet) is sparse, hard to calibrate, and noisy, giving poor 3D reconstruction especially for objects, while studio motion capture is accurate but staged, diversity-limited, and marker-based systems inhibit natural interactions such as self-contact. GigaHands aims to balance accuracy, diversity, realism, and practicality by replicating in-the-wild behavioral variety inside a markerless multi-view studio and automating annotation, with text descriptions as a first-class output alongside 3D hand and object motion.

## Dataset Construction

Capture uses a custom tabletop rig of 51 RGB cameras arranged as 3x3 grids on each face of a cubic volume around a transparent glass platform, illuminated by LEDs; each camera records 1280x720 at 30 fps with software synchronization under 3 ms phase misalignment, and intrinsics/extrinsics come from COLMAP aided by fiducial markers. Activity sourcing is procedural: atomic verbs are harvested from existing datasets (Ego4D, Ego-Exo4D, TACO, OakInk2), associated with objects, and an LLM (GPT-4) groups them into 5 scenarios (cooking and eating, office work, crafting, entertainment, housework), 25 scenes, 191 activities, and 1,370 instruction scripts covering 533 verbs. During filming, instructions are played as audio and subjects perform them naturally; inconsistent or unscripted actions are retained, and annotators later segment the 13k recorded sequences into 14k clips, annotate any unscripted actions, and use the LLM to rephrase each description five times, producing 84k motion-text pairs over 1,467 unique verbs (580 exclusive to GigaHands). Hand motion is estimated fully automatically: YOLOv8 hand boxes, HaMeR MANO estimates, ViTPose handedness, multi-view triangulation of 2D keypoints, one-euro filtering, and MANO fitting following the EasyMoCap pipeline. Object motion combines Grounding DINO detections seeded by DINOv2 template matches and OpenCLIP negative-prompt filtering, SAM2 segmentation, and differentiable-rendering pose optimization supervised by multi-view masks, initialized from an Instant-NGP density field (translation) and FoundPose-style DINOv2 template matching (rotation). Objects comprise 417 real items with 310 multi-view scanned textured meshes and 31 single-view reconstructed meshes for small items, plus multi-view masks for non-rigid objects; the dataset also provides hand/object segmentation masks, 2D/3D keypoints, camera poses, and geometry-derived contact maps showing diverse contact regions including finger gaps and hand backs, with more right-hand contact reflecting the right-handed majority of subjects.

## Evaluation Protocol

For text-driven motion synthesis, GigaHands is split 16:3:1 into train/test/val, 42 3D keypoints per bimanual pair serve as the motion representation, and a T2M-GPT model is trained and compared against models trained on TACO and OakInk2 using R-Precision, Multimodal Distance, FID, Diversity, and Multimodality, with per-dataset feature extractors and ground-truth upper bounds reported because each test set defines its own embedding space; GRU-based and diffusion backbones are shown in supplementary results, and a data-scale ablation trains on 10-100% of the data. For motion captioning, a TM2T-based model is evaluated with R-Precision/MM Dist for alignment and BLEU-4, ROUGE, BERTScore, distinct-n, and Pairwise-BLEU for quality and diversity, including zero-dataset captioning of TACO (whose triplet labels become a classification task) and OakInk2 motions. For dynamic radiance fields, 2DGS is fitted frame-wise with temporal initialization using 38 of the 51 views (12 removed for lighting issues, one held-out test view) on segmented hands and objects.

## Findings and Analysis

On text-to-motion synthesis, the model trained on GigaHands performs best on most metrics against TACO- and OakInk2-trained models (for example, R-Precision@1 of 31.2 versus 18.9 and 17.9, FID of 4.70 versus 11.0 and 19.6, and Multimodality of 9.11 versus 6.83 and 3.45), and generates plausible object manipulation without object geometry input thanks to object diversity; the data-scale ablation shows most metrics improving continuously from 10% to 100% of the training data, directly evidencing the value of scale. For captioning, the GigaHands-trained model reaches 57.0 R-Precision@1 versus 43.5 (TACO) and 40.4 (OakInk2), and is dramatically more diverse (distinct-1 of 15.3% and Pairwise-BLEU of 0.916 versus 2.76% and 8.21 for OakInk2), while also producing finer-grained captions for TACO and OakInk2 motions. In scale terms, GigaHands' 2,034 minutes, 51 views, 183M frames, 56 subjects, and 417 objects exceed all existing 3D bimanual datasets in the comparison (for example, HOT3D at 833 minutes/19 subjects/33 objects, OakInk2 at 557 minutes, TACO at 202 minutes), and t-SNE and verb-set (UpSet) analyses show correspondingly greater pose, motion, and verb diversity. Finally, the dense views enable dynamic radiance field reconstruction of activities including non-rigid objects such as clothing, which most hand datasets cannot support.

## Contributions

- GigaHands, the largest annotated 3D bimanual hand activity dataset: 34 hours, 56 subjects, 417 objects, 14k motion clips, 84k text annotations, 3.7M bimanual hand poses, and 183M frames across 51 views.
- A procedural instruct-to-annotate acquisition strategy that uses LLM-generated, verb- and object-grounded instruction scripts to guarantee activity diversity and atomic-level text while minimizing manual labeling.
- A fully automatic annotation pipeline for 3D hand pose/shape, object 6D pose and shape, segmentation, and camera pose from markerless multi-view RGB.
- Demonstrated applications enabled by scale: text-driven hand motion synthesis and captioning with state-of-the-art results on most metrics, cross-dataset captioning, and dynamic radiance field reconstruction of hand activities.

## Limitations

The authors state that the studio setting confines capture to a limited space, making large-environment motions hard to record accurately. While rigid objects and object parts are tracked automatically, fully automatic tracking of articulated and non-rigid objects remains an open challenge, with non-rigid items handled only through multi-view segmentation masks and radiance fields rather than pose tracks. Hand and object annotations are estimates from markerless pipelines (HaMeR/MANO fitting, differentiable-rendering tracking) rather than mocap ground truth, so they inherit the accuracy limits of those methods, and the demonstrated applications focus on synthesis, captioning, and reconstruction, leaving robotics and human-computer interaction uses of the scale and diversity as future directions.

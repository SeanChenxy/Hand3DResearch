# Dynamic Reconstruction of Hand-Object Interaction with Distributed Force-aware Contact Representation

**Authors:** Zhenjun Yu, Wenqiang Xu, Pengfei Xie, Yutong Li (Shanghai Jiao Tong University); Brian W. Anthony, Zhuorui Zhang (MIT); Cewu Lu (corresponding author)  
**Date:** 2025 (ICCV 2025)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2411.09572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.09572)  
**Zotero item:** `3IM9L7FY` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/3IM9L7FY))

## Summary
Vision-only methods for dynamic hand-object interaction reconstruction struggle to capture occluded contact details and object deformation, because near-contact information is precisely what the hand hides from cameras. The paper proposes ViTaM-D, a visual-tactile framework that first reconstructs hand-object interaction with a visual dynamic tracking network under a contact constraint, then refines hand poses and contact through a force-aware optimization driven by DF-Field, a distributed force-aware contact representation that couples kinetic and potential energy with a barrier energy against penetration. To benchmark deformable object reconstruction, where real ground truth for deformation is unavailable, the authors introduce the HOT (Hand-Object-Tactile) dataset: 600 hand-object interaction sequences on 30 deformable objects from 5 categories, generated in the high-precision ZeMa FEM simulation environment with penetration-free poses, SDF ground truth, depth images from 8 camera views, and distributed tactile sensor readings. On DexYCB (rigid) and HOT (deformable), ViTaM-D outperforms state-of-the-art methods in reconstruction accuracy, and DF-Field proves more effective for refining hand poses than previous contact-based refinement methods.

## Background and Motivation
Humans manipulate objects using both visual and tactile feedback, but existing dynamic reconstruction methods [gSDF, HOTrack, and related work] rely solely on vision to recover global geometry and hand-object poses, and consequently miss occluded contact geometry and object deformation. Distributed tactile sensors (wearable gloves or attachable arrays) can conform to the hand while preserving its geometry and dexterity, supplementing the near-contact information that vision lacks. The prior ViTaM system provided a high-density tactile glove and fused tactile data with visual features via a transformer, but ignored the unbalanced information between forces and point cloud features; other visual-tactile works track object trajectories without contact geometry or handle only rigid objects. Building and training a visual-tactile framework further requires large-scale data with diverse interactions and precise tactile readings, especially for deformable objects, yet existing hand-object datasets cover only rigid or articulated objects and lack accurate tactile data. The HOT dataset was created specifically to fill this gap, since obtaining deformation ground truth in real scenes is challenging.

## Dataset Construction
- **Source and generation:** HOT is a simulation dataset built with ZeMa, a high-precision physics-based simulator supporting penetration-free frictional contact modeling with finite element method (FEM) object deformation. Grasp trajectories are produced with the DiPGrasp fast grasp planner: a feasible grasp pose is sampled, the hand is placed near the target with slight rotational perturbation, starts fully open, approaches, and performs the grasp. Each sequence yields depth images, point clouds, and distributed tactile array readings per frame, plus SDF ground truth.
- **Objects:** 30 deformable objects from 5 categories drawn from the YCB repository: 5-10 objects each from Bottle and Box, plus 5 Sponges, 5 Plasticines, and 5 Stuffed Toys for large deformations. Physical properties are defined per category: Bottle and Box with density 10^3 kg/m^3, Young's modulus 1.271 GPa, Poisson's ratio 0.28; Sponges, Plasticines, and Stuffed Toys with density 30 kg/m^3, Young's modulus 0.1 MPa, Poisson's ratio 0.38; Plasticine has a yield stress of 200 Pa.
- **Scale and composition:** Each object has 20 trajectories — 15 training, 2 testing, 3 validation — totaling 600 sequences of 50-100 frames. Each sequence includes point clouds from 8 camera views and per-frame tactile readings from the 22-region distributed sensor layout (2 regions for the thumb, 3 for each other finger, 8 for the palm).
- **Annotations:** Penetration-free hand-object poses, object SDF ground truth, contact states, and force sensor readings. Rigid-object evaluation additionally uses the public DexYCB dataset.

## Evaluation Protocol
The task is 4D tracking and dynamic reconstruction of hand and object from a stream of point clouds (derived from single-view depth images) with optional distributed tactile input; output is a MANO hand mesh and an SDF-decoded object mesh per frame. The visual dynamic tracking network (PointNet++ backbone, flow prediction module, transformer fusion, ConvOccNet-style object decoder, PVN3D-inspired voting hand decoder with inverse kinematics) is trained end-to-end with flow, SDF, hand-joint, and contact-constraint losses; force-aware optimization then refines the MANO pose by minimizing DF-Field energy (relative potential plus barrier energy, threshold 2 mm, ball-query radius 5 mm, 100 iterations per frame at 3.5 ± 0.5 s per frame) with rotation and proximity regularizers. On HOT, baselines are Zhang et al. and ViTaM for tracking, and CPF and TOCH as alternative hand-object refinement methods plugged into the framework; on DexYCB, baselines are gSDF (RGB), HOTrack, and Zhang et al. Metrics: IoU (%) for mesh overlap, Chamfer Distance (mm) for object vertices, MPJPE (mm) for hand joints, Penetration Depth (mm) for interaction plausibility, and Contact Mask IoU (CIoU, %) with contact defined as distance under 3 mm. Rigid-object comparisons are restricted to DexYCB's rigid objects; deformable evaluation uses HOT.

## Findings and Analysis
- DexYCB (rigid objects): the visual tracking network without force optimization reaches IoU 90.1%, CD 9.6 mm, MPJPE 13.2 mm, PD 9.9 mm, CIoU 35.4%, beating gSDF (86.8/13.4/14.4/8.9/31.3), HOTrack (88.2/10.2/25.7/12.3/28.5), and Zhang et al. (88.1/10.4/15.3/10.6/30.5) on IoU, CD, MPJPE, and CIoU.
- HOT (deformable objects): without force optimization the method records IoU 81.0%, CD 10.9 mm, MPJPE 13.6 mm, PD 10.7 mm, CIoU 29.8%, versus ViTaM at 80.5/11.5/15.1/10.6/28.5 and Zhang et al. at 77.9/16.8/15.6/11.9/26.8. With DF-Field force-aware optimization, MPJPE improves to 11.3 mm, PD to 7.3 mm, and CIoU to 40.3%, exceeding refinement with CPF (11.9/7.8/37.5) and TOCH (13.5/9.3/34.1).
- Ablation on the contact constraint (HOT): without it, IoU is 75.9%; with tactile-derived contact states, 81.0% (matching ground-truth contact states at 81.2%); with a PointNet-predicted contact state, 78.3% — tactile readings essentially recover ground-truth contact accuracy.
- Ablation on force representation: tactile-driven optimization gives MPJPE 12.9 mm on HOT with fixed empirical forces (M = 0.5) versus 11.3 mm with real tactile readings (and 8.5 versus 7.3 mm PD), showing distributed tactile arrays outperform empirical force settings; even fixed forces improve DexYCB from 13.2 to 12.3 mm MPJPE, validating DF-Field's design.
- Real-world: a network trained on simulation data was deployed with a ViTaM glove and a Photoneo MotionCam M+ depth camera, reconstructing an unseen stuffed toy with plausible shape and deformation.

## Contributions
1. ViTaM-D, a visual-tactile learning framework combining a contact-constrained visual dynamic tracking network with a force-aware optimization stage that integrates distributed tactile readings.
2. DF-Field, a distributed force-aware contact representation based on relative potential energy and barrier energy, which remains usable without tactile hardware by substituting empirically set forces.
3. The HOT dataset: 600 simulation-generated hand-object interaction sequences on 30 deformable objects across 5 categories, with penetration-free poses, SDF ground truth, 8-view point clouds, and distributed tactile readings — the first benchmark dedicated to deformable-object interaction reconstruction.

## Limitations
The dataset's ground truth is produced entirely in the ZeMa simulation environment, motivated by the difficulty of obtaining deformation ground truth in real-world scenarios, so HOT evaluates simulation-domain performance and real-world evidence is limited to qualitative demonstrations (e.g., one unseen stuffed toy) rather than a quantitative real benchmark. The method's best results depend on access to distributed tactile hardware; without it, DF-Field must rely on fixed empirical forces, which the ablation shows yields systematically worse hand pose, penetration, and contact metrics (e.g., MPJPE 12.9 versus 11.3 mm, CIoU 36.8 versus 40.3 on HOT). The paper does not include a dedicated limitations section; stated future work covers applying ViTaM-D to data collection for robotic imitation learning, dexterous manipulation, and human-robot interaction.

# EgoScale: Scaling Dexterous Manipulation with Diverse Egocentric Human Data

**Authors:** Ruijie Zheng, Dantong Niu, Yuqi Xie, Jing Wang, et al. (NVIDIA; UC Berkeley; University of Maryland)  
**Date:** 2026 (arXiv)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2602.16710](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16710)

## Summary
Prior human-to-robot transfer work uses only tens to hundreds of hours of human data and mostly gripper or low-DoF hands, leaving open whether human data can support fine-grained, high-degree-of-freedom dexterous manipulation. EgoScale is a human-to-dexterous-manipulation transfer framework built on a curated corpus of 20,854 hours of action-labeled egocentric human video — over 20x larger than prior human–robot transfer datasets. A flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model is pretrained on this data using relative wrist motion and retargeted 22-DoF hand joint actions, then adapted through a lightweight aligned human–robot mid-training stage and task post-training. The paper's central findings are a log-linear scaling law between human data volume and hand-action validation loss (R2 = 0.9983) that strongly correlates with real-robot performance, a +54% average success-rate improvement over a no-pretraining baseline on a 22-DoF dexterous hand, emergent one-shot task adaptation, and cross-embodiment transfer to a low-DoF tri-finger robot.

## Background and Motivation
Human behavior is argued to be the most scalable source of supervision for physical intelligence, but existing transfer approaches (EgoMimic, DexWild, EgoVLA, Humanoid Policy ~ Human Policy) rely on small, constrained human datasets and rarely supervise finger articulation. EgoScale frames human-to-robot transfer for dexterous manipulation as a scaling phenomenon: the hypothesis is that with explicit wrist- and hand-level action supervision, large but noisy in-the-wild egocentric video becomes a predictable supervision source, provided that a small amount of precisely aligned human–robot data grounds the representations in robot sensing and control. The work therefore decouples data scale (Stage I) from embodiment alignment (Stage II).

## Dataset Construction
- **Stage I pretraining corpus (the core dataset contribution):** 20,854 hours of egocentric human activity video at 30 FPS spanning household, industrial, retail, educational, and other real-world settings, covering 9,869 scenes, 6,015 tasks, and 43,237 objects. The majority is in-the-wild recording; camera motion and hand trajectories are recovered with off-the-shelf SLAM and hand-pose estimation, yielding noisy but scalable wrist/hand action labels. Category distribution (categories with >50 hours) is dominated by retail and consumer goods (20.1%), fashion (11.8%), repair services (11.5%), food and beverage (11.5%), home environments (9.5%), construction and hardware (7.7%), food processing (6.7%), and printing and design (4.4%), with a long tail; frequent environments include homes, flower shops, electronics and furniture repair shops, woodworking shops, and clothing stores. This corpus also incorporates 829 hours of EgoDex (Apple Vision Pro capture, 194 tabletop tasks) as higher-precision kinematic anchoring. The paper does not enumerate all constituent source datasets in the main text.
- **Stage II aligned mid-training data:** 344 tabletop manipulation tasks with approximately 30 human and 5 teleoperated robot trajectories per task — about 50 hours of human data and only 4 hours of robot data. Human demonstrations use the same camera configuration as the robot (one head-mounted and two wrist-mounted cameras with matched viewpoints and calibrated intrinsics); wrist pose comes from Vive trackers and full in-hand pose from Manus gloves (25 joint transforms per hand), synchronized with video.
- **Action representation:** relative SE(3) wrist motion between consecutive timesteps (invariant to camera/absolute pose) plus 21 human hand keypoints (25 keypoints with 3D position and orientation) retargeted per frame into the 22-DoF joint space of the Sharpa hand via an optimization-based procedure (CasADi/IPOPT, URDF joint limits, exponential smoothing).

## Evaluation Protocol
The model is a GR00T N1-style flow-matching VLA (VLM backbone + DiT action expert) with learnable placeholder tokens replacing robot proprioception for human data and lightweight embodiment-conditioned MLP adapters. Training runs three stages: human pretraining (100K steps, 256 GB200 GPUs, batch 8,192, lr 5e-5), aligned mid-training (50K steps, batch 2,048, lr 3e-5, backbone frozen), and task post-training (10K steps, batch 512, lr 3e-5). Real-robot evaluation uses a Galaxea R1Pro humanoid with 22-DoF Sharpa Wave hands, 7-DoF arms, and head + two wrist RGB cameras, plus a Unitree G1 with a 7-DoF tri-finger hand for cross-embodiment tests. Five dexterous R1Pro tasks (Shirt Rolling, Card Sorting, Tongs for Fruit Transfer, Unscrewing a Bottle Cap, Syringe Liquid Transfer) are each trained on 100 teleoperated demonstrations (20 for Shirt Rolling; Bottle uses 25 trajectories per bottle across 4 bottle sizes). Each method is trained with 2 random seeds and evaluated over 10 trials per task (16 for Tongs), with image-overlay-based scene initialization; metrics are binary success rate and a [0,1] task completion score defined by additive or progress-based rubrics. Scaling experiments pretrain on 1k/2k/4k/10k/20k-hour subsets and measure validation loss on a held-out set of 2,000 egocentric episodes (20 timesteps per trajectory, 16 flow samples averaged per timestep) alongside downstream task completion. Baselines are: training from scratch, mid-training only, human pretraining only, and human pretraining + mid-training.

## Findings and Analysis
- Human pretraining improves average task completion by over 55% versus training from scratch across the five R1Pro tasks, and the full pretrain + mid-train recipe improves average success rate by 54% over the no-pretraining baseline; pretrain + mid-train reaches the best average completion (0.71) and success (0.70) in the main comparison, with human pretraining alone already beating mid-training-only on most tasks.
- Scaling: average task completion rises monotonically from 0.30 (1k hours) through 0.45, 0.48, and 0.57 to 0.71 (20k hours) with no saturation in the explored regime; validation loss follows L = 0.024 − 0.003·ln(D) with R2 = 0.9983, and offline loss closely tracks downstream robot performance. Small-data runs (1k–2k hours) overfit, while 10k–20k hours train stably.
- One-shot transfer: with a single robot demonstration plus 100 aligned human demonstrations, the pretrain + mid-train model reaches 0.88 success on Fold Shirt and 0.55 on Unscrewing Water Bottles, both unseen in mid-training; models lacking either stage fail in this setting.
- Cross-embodiment: on Unitree G1 tasks (Pen in Bin, Dish Handover in Rack), human-pretrained policies achieve over 30% absolute success-rate improvement across both tasks versus G1-specific training without human pretraining.
- Action-space ablation: wrist-only supervision performs poorly on contact-sensitive tasks (Cards, Tongs), the fingertip SE(3) representation is inconsistent due to implausible joint mappings, and retargeted 22-DoF joint-space supervision gives the most consistent performance across tasks.

## Contributions
1. Demonstration that human-to-robot dexterous transfer is a scaling phenomenon, with a log-linear scaling law over 20k+ hours of egocentric human data whose validation loss predicts real-robot performance.
2. A curated 20,854-hour action-labeled egocentric human corpus (9,869 scenes, 6,015 tasks, 43,237 objects) with relative-wrist plus retargeted 22-DoF hand action labels.
3. A two-stage transfer recipe combining large-scale noisy human pretraining with a small (50 h human + 4 h robot) aligned human–robot mid-training set, enabling efficient post-training with minimal robot supervision.
4. Evidence of emergent one-shot adaptation to unseen dexterous tasks and embodiment-agnostic transfer from a 22-DoF hand action space to a 7-DoF tri-finger robot.

## Limitations
The paper has no dedicated limitations section; the following are stated or evident. The authors do not extrapolate the scaling law beyond the measured range and note that gains may continue only "within the explored regime"; hand-action labels in the in-the-wild corpus come from off-the-shelf SLAM and hand-pose estimation and are explicitly described as noisy, with precision provided instead by the much smaller EgoDex and mid-training sets. Mid-training requires matched human–robot hardware setups (Vive trackers, Manus gloves, matched camera rigs), and cross-embodiment transfer currently needs embodiment-specific adapters and G1 play data rather than zero-shot execution. The evaluation covers five post-training tasks on one primary robot platform plus two G1 tasks, and the human data lacks paired force/contact sensing.

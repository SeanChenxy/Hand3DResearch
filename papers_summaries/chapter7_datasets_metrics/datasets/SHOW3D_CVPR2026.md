# SHOW3D: Capturing Scenes of 3D Hands and Objects in the Wild

## Summary
Existing hand-object interaction datasets sit at one of two extremes: studio captures offer accurate 3D ground truth but limited environmental diversity, while in-the-wild egocentric corpora lack dense 3D annotations. This paper breaks that trade-off with a lightweight back-mounted multi-camera rig (ten synchronized monochrome fisheye cameras at 60 Hz, calibrated with a Meta Quest 3 headset) and a marker-less ego-exo annotation pipeline, and uses them to build SHOW3D, the first large-scale hand-object interaction dataset with accurate 3D annotations captured in diverse indoor and outdoor environments. The dataset provides 4.3M multi-view frames with 3D hand poses and fitted meshes, 6DoF object poses, segmentation masks, contact regions, and text captions. Cross-dataset experiments on interaction field estimation and 3D hand pose estimation show models trained on studio data generalize poorly to in-the-wild conditions, while SHOW3D-trained models remain strong on studio benchmarks.

## Background and Motivation
Accurate 3D understanding of hands and manipulated objects is essential for egocentric AR/VR interaction and robotic skill transfer, yet models trained on controlled data fail outside the studio. Marker-based MoCap datasets (ARCTIC, OakInk2, TACO, HOT3D) constrain environments and alter hand/object appearance with reflective markers; multi-camera studio pipelines (GigaHands, Assembly101) remain stationary and context-poor; in-the-wild corpora (Ego-Exo4D, Nymeria, EgoDex) target full-body action and lack dense 3D hand/object ground truth. The paper's goal is a mobile capture system that preserves natural mobility and visual realism while producing quantitative, sub-centimeter 3D annotations.

## Dataset Construction
The SHOW3D capture rig is a backpack-style system weighing about 8 kg: eight monochrome fisheye exocentric cameras (1024x1280, 152-degree horizontal FOV) in a half-dome configuration plus two egocentric fisheye cameras from a freely worn Meta Quest 3, all hardware-synchronized at 60 Hz and calibrated into a shared frame that moves with the participant; five MoCap cameras on the rig track a marker tree on the headset for headset-to-rig pose only. Data is streamed to a workstation on a mobile cart following the participant, enabling unconstrained walking. The resulting SHOW3D dataset contains over 4 million (Table 1: 4.3M) synchronized frames from 38 subjects (Table 1; Section 4 states 30) manipulating 21 everyday objects (a CAD-bearing subset of HOT3D objects), in over 1,200 video sequences across more than 30 distinct locations. Scenes span outdoor environments (gardens, seating areas, building ledges against sky, variable sun and weather) and indoor ones (kitchens, meeting rooms, hallways, cafeterias, desk areas, recreational spaces) with wide illumination variation. Annotations are generated automatically: 3D hand poses via Sapiens plus finetuned InterNet 2D keypoint detection, RANSAC triangulation over all ten views, personalized UmeTrack-style mesh fitting with inverse kinematics, and Bayesian confidence estimation; 6DoF object poses via a multi-view extension of CNOS detection, FoundPose coarse estimation, and GoTrack refinement built on DINOv2 features, with confidence-based automatic filtering. Every sequence also carries 2D segmentation masks, hand-object contact annotations, and multiple LLM-augmented natural-language captions derived from goal-oriented task instructions (e.g., "spraying with a bottle", "sipping from a mug").

## Evaluation Protocol
Annotation quality is validated against independent references: hand keypoints against a 30-camera dome studio capture and against manual in-the-wild 2D labels triangulated with RANSAC (metrics: median and P90 MPJPE); object poses against MoCap-tracked markers attached to five objects (metrics: yield, P50 translation error, P50 rotation error). Three downstream benchmarks are used. (1) Interaction field estimation (following ARCTIC's InterField): predict per-vertex closest distances between hand and object meshes; metrics ADE (mm) and acceleration ACC (m/s^2), evaluated with cross-dataset train/test combinations of SHOW3D and HOT3D. (2) 3D hand pose estimation: the multi-view UmeTrack tracking model is trained on combinations of UmeTrack, HOT3D, and SHOW3D data; a gender-balanced subset of 4 SHOW3D subjects forms the evaluation set, filtered by annotation confidence; the metric is mean keypoint position error (MKPE, mm). SHOW3D contributes 1.9M high-confidence training frames for this task. (3) Text-driven 6DoF object pose forecasting: a transformer forecaster consumes 30 past object poses (optionally with a BERT-encoded caption) to predict the pose 30 or 60 frames ahead; the metric is mean translation error in mm across 13 objects.

## Findings and Analysis
Hand annotation quality reaches sub-centimeter median accuracy in all conditions: 7.90 mm median MPJPE against the dome and 6.36 mm against manual labels for hand-object interaction (P90 12.53 mm and 16.48 mm respectively). Object annotation yields sub-centimeter P50 translation error across evaluated objects (e.g., 3.51 mm at 89.5% yield for birdhouse_toy). On interaction field estimation, a model trained on SHOW3D and tested on HOT3D achieves 14.70 mm ADE, whereas a HOT3D-trained model tested on SHOW3D reaches 22.57 mm (+54%); jointly training on both improves HOT3D testing to 8.80 mm (-22%) but gives only 2% gain on SHOW3D (13.50 mm), indicating SHOW3D's domain subsumes HOT3D's. In hand pose estimation, UmeTrack- and HOT3D-trained models degrade to 22.2 mm and 19.6 mm MKPE on SHOW3D versus 14.3 mm when SHOW3D data is added to training; on HOT3D the combined model reaches 12.3 mm, the best result. For text-driven forecasting, conditioning reduces mean translation error from 42.7 to 30.4 mm at the 30-frame horizon (-29%) and from 46.7 to 35.0 mm at 60 frames (-25%), with per-object gains up to 72% (mustard).

## Contributions
- The first mobile multi-camera capture rig that produces precise 3D hand and object ground truth during genuinely unconstrained, in-the-wild hand-object interaction.
- A marker-less ego-exo annotation pipeline with per-annotation confidence estimates and automatic filtering, quantitatively validated against independent dome and manual references.
- SHOW3D, a large-scale dataset of 4.3M multi-view frames with 3D hand poses, MANO-style fitted meshes, 6DoF object poses with CAD models, segmentation masks, contact regions, and text captions across diverse indoor and outdoor scenes.
- Empirical evidence from three downstream tasks that in-the-wild HOI data with 3D ground truth materially improves generalization and that text annotations add predictive value.

## Limitations
Marker-less ground truth in genuinely hard conditions yields imperfect frame coverage: confidence filtering reduces the usable training set for hand pose to 1.9M of 4.3M frames. The dome-based validation can underestimate P90 error because the automated dome pipeline shares failure modes with the mobile system. The object set is restricted to 21 HOT3D-derived objects with CAD models, and at most one instance of each object class is assumed per scene. The paper's subject count is stated inconsistently (30 in the text versus 38 in the comparison table). Future work named by the authors includes adding tactile and range modalities and extending to embodied applications such as teleoperation.

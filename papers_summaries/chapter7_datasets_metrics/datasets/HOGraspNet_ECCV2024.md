# Dense Hand-Object(HO) GraspNet with Full Grasping Taxonomy and Dynamics

**Authors:** Woojin Cho, Jihyun Lee, Minjae Yi, Minje Kim (KAIST; with Imperial College London, Kwangwoon University, Surromind)  
**Date:** 2024 (ECCV 2024)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2409.04033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.04033)

## Summary
Existing 3D hand-object interaction datasets are limited in data cardinality, variation in interaction scenarios, or annotation quality; in particular, real benchmarks cover only a fraction of the 33-class human grasp taxonomy (15 classes for HO3D, 14 for DexYCB) and most provide no grasp labels at all. The paper introduces HOGraspNet, a real multi-view RGB-D training dataset that is the only real dataset capturing the full grasp taxonomy, redefined as 28 classes by merging geometrically similar or uncommon grasps of the Feix et al. 33-grasp taxonomy. It contains 1.5M annotated RGB-D frames (1,489,112) from 4 synchronized Azure Kinect viewpoints, 99 participants aged 10 to 74, and 30 objects (22 YCB rigid objects plus 8 compound/articulated ones) spanning 90 interaction scenarios. Frames carry 3D hand meshes (fitted MANO and HALO implicit surfaces), 21-joint 3D keypoints, 6D object poses from a marker-based MoCap system, hand-object contact maps, and per-sample grasp labels. Benchmarks on grasp classification and hand-object pose estimation show performance varies strongly with grasp type and object class, and a model trained on HOGraspNet transfers better to DexYCB than one trained on HO3D.

## Background and Motivation
The authors position HOGraspNet against three gaps in prior hand-object datasets. First, interaction coverage: HO3D and DexYCB, the most widely used benchmarks, cover few object categories and only 45% and 42% of the Feix grasp taxonomies respectively; YCB-Affordance covers 100% of grasps but is synthetic; ARCTIC places visible markers on hands; OakInk, the closest real dataset, provides no grasp labels with fewer subjects. Second, data diversity: earlier datasets have few hand identities (10-20) or low cardinality (87K-154K frames), and t-SNE visualizations of MANO shape parameters and learned grasp features confirm that HOGraspNet spans a wider hand-shape and grasp-pose space than HO3D, DexYCB, and OakInk. Third, annotation method: most real datasets rely on fitting MANO to multi-view RGB-D with many loss-weight hyperparameters, motivating the supplementary use of HALO, a hand implicit function requiring almost no parameter tuning, for scalable annotation. Grasp taxonomies are treated as atomic actions whose spatial and temporal combinations can represent complex hand activities around objects.

## Dataset Construction
- **Objects and taxonomy:** 22 rigid objects selected from the YCB dataset plus 8 other compound/articulated daily objects (30 total), chosen by primitive shapes (cylinder, sphere, disk, cube) of grasp areas, object sizes (small/medium/large), and articulation, so that all grasp configurations are covered. The 33 Feix et al. grasp classes are redefined into 28 classes by merging visually indistinguishable or geometrically close poses; the three most common grasp classes are captured per object, yielding 90 interaction scenarios.
- **Subjects and capture:** 99 participants with diverse hand sizes, shapes, and textures, aged 10 to 74, each grasping objects with their right hand following specified grasp instructions while freely varying pose (translation, rotation). Each participant repeats the procedure 2 to 4 times, each trial lasting 20 seconds, covering reaching, in-air manipulation, and placing the object back. Four temporally synchronized Azure Kinect RGB-D cameras (1920x1080, 30 FPS, one roughly at eye height imitating a fixed egocentric view) record at 4 viewpoints; frames are downsampled to 10 FPS for annotation, giving 1,489,112 annotated frames. Non-grasping sequences (tapping, folding, opening) are also recorded.
- **Annotations:** Per frame: 3D hand pose (21 joints) and mesh, grasp class, 6D object pose, and hand-object contact map. Hand meshes come from both MANO fitting and HALO implicit-surface fitting; object meshes with textures are pre-scanned and provided. Object 6D pose comes from 8 IR cameras at 120 FPS tracking 3 to 5 optical markers (3 mm) per object, placed on regions least likely to be grasped; hands are marker-free. The annotation pipeline bootstraps MediaPipe 2.5D keypoints with a cross-view outlier-filtering scheme, then performs gradual three-stage multi-view multi-frame MANO-plus-object fitting with 2D/3D, segmentation, depth, temporal, and physics (penetration and contact) losses. Post-verification filters frames with rendered/pseudo-GT segmentation IoU below 0.6 in any view, followed by crowdsourced manual checks. HALO fitting uses keypoints refined onto the MANO kinematic space via inverse kinematics and achieves comparable mean IoU to MANO (0.719 versus 0.739) with far fewer hyperparameters.

## Evaluation Protocol
Five train/test splits are released: S0 (default) splits by sequence, with each subject's first sequence as test; S1 splits by subjects (7:3 unseen subjects); S2 splits by views (3:1 unseen views); S3 splits by objects (7 objects collectively covering all 28 grasps as unseen test objects, 23 for training); S4 splits by taxonomy (intermediate grasp types held out). Two tasks are benchmarked on S0. Grasp classification feeds a hand mesh with per-vertex contact values into a CoMA-based mesh autoencoder whose bottleneck features drive an MLP classifier, trained with vertex reconstruction, grasp-taxonomy, and contact-classification losses. Hand-object pose estimation uses HFL-Net, then state-of-the-art in RGB-based joint hand mesh and object 6D pose estimation, evaluated with MPJPE/PA-MPJPE for hands and ADD-0.1D for objects, reported per grasp and per object class. A cross-benchmark protocol following OakInk's setup additionally trains HFL-Net on HOGraspNet versus HO3D and tests on DexYCB, restricted to object classes common to all datasets.

## Findings and Analysis
- Grasp classification on S0 reaches 0.95 F1 for contact-map reconstruction and 0.88 accuracy for 28-way taxonomy classification from hand meshes with contact maps alone, without object input.
- HFL-Net achieves a mean hand PA-MPJPE of 5.67 mm on S0, comparable to state-of-the-art results on other hand-object datasets, which the authors take as evidence of annotation quality. The hardest grasp classes are largely absent from DexYCB and HO3D (three of the four worst classes are not present in those datasets), indicating that the newly captured real grasp poses challenge models trained on existing data. Larger, more occluding objects yield higher hand pose errors.
- Object pose estimation averages 63.61 ADD-0.1D across the 30 objects, comparable to other benchmarks except for the dice class (2.44), where small visible regions under occlusion make the task ill-posed; plate (99.42), disk_lid (99.29), and wine_glass (97.13) are near-perfect.
- Cross-benchmark: HFL-Net trained on HOGraspNet and tested on DexYCB reaches 42.65 mm MPJPE and 9.36 mm PA-MPJPE, versus 57.31/10.31 for the same network trained on HO3D, supporting the dataset's comprehensiveness as a training source.

## Contributions
1. HOGraspNet, the first real RGB-D dataset covering the full (redefined, 28-class) human grasp taxonomy with per-sample grasp labels and wide intraclass variations, at 1.5M annotated frames across 4 views, 99 subjects, and 30 objects.
2. A scalable annotation pipeline combining bootstrapped multi-view keypoint lifting, gradual multi-view MANO fitting, and automatic-plus-crowdsourced verification, supplemented by the first large-scale use of HALO implicit hand fitting, which requires essentially no hyperparameter tuning while achieving accuracy comparable to MANO.
3. Benchmarks for grasp classification and hand-object pose estimation with five controlled generalization splits, plus cross-dataset training results showing improved transfer to DexYCB.

## Limitations
The authors state that compound and articulated objects were included to capture dynamic actions, but are currently treated as rigid objects; articulation annotations will be added in future work. Non-grasping actions such as pushing, throwing, squeezing, or deforming non-rigid objects (plastic bottles, sponges) are also deferred to future work, although related sequences (tapping, folding, opening) have already been recorded. Capture is restricted to the right hand, and object markers, while placed outside likely contact areas, can still constrain some hand poses.

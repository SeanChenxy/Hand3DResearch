# HO-3D v3: Improving the Accuracy of Hand-Object Annotations of the HO-3D Dataset

**Authors:** Shreyas Hampali, Sayan Deb Sarkar, Vincent Lepetit  
**Date:** 2021-07-02  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2107.00887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.00887)  
**Zotero item:** `8UAIYS68` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/8UAIYS68))  
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.  

## Summary

This technical report (arXiv 2021) releases HO-3D v3, a re-annotation of the HO-3D hand-object interaction dataset obtained with three algorithmic improvements to the HOnnotate optimization pipeline. Relative to HO-3D v2, the v3 annotations improve fingertip accuracy from 12.31 mm to 8.12 mm mean error against manual annotations (a 4 mm gain, measured on frames deliberately chosen where v2 performed poorly), and exhibit visibly more hand-object contact regions at similar penetration levels, while also expanding the dataset from 66,034/11,524 to 83,325/20,137 train/test images.

## Background and Motivation

HO-3D provides image sequences of hand-object interaction scenarios annotated with 3D hand and object poses, with annotations generated automatically by the HOnnotate optimization method rather than by manual labeling. Because the quality of these automatic annotations directly bounds both training supervision and benchmark evaluation for downstream hand pose estimation methods, the authors revisited the pipeline to correct systematic weaknesses of the v2 annotations: imprecise silhouettes, poses restricted by a PCA parameterization of MANO joint angles, and residual hand-object interpenetration. The goal of v3 is therefore more accurate hand and object poses that also yield better estimates of contact regions between the hand and the object surface.

## Dataset Construction

HO-3D v3 covers the same 10 YCB objects and 10 subjects as v2 but increases coverage to 83,325 training and 20,137 test images (versus 66,034 and 11,524 in v2), released on 1 July 2021 (v2 was released 9 January 2020). The annotations are regenerated with an improved HOnnotate pipeline incorporating three changes. First, the L2 silhouette discrepancy term is replaced with a cross-entropy-based term, and hand/object segmentation is no longer predicted by a network trained on synthetic data but by MSeg, whose 194-category predictions are remapped to three classes (object, person, background). Second, optimization operates directly on the 45 MANO joint angles instead of their PCA components, with joint axes manually realigned to anatomical finger directions; this avoids implausible poses such as a twisted thumb that arose from ill-placed joint-angle limits, reaches grasps unreachable in PCA space, and reduces entrapment in local minima during segmentation- and depth-driven optimization. Third, the physical plausibility term is replaced by a simpler sphere-based repulsion term in which the inner volumes of the hand and object meshes are populated with spheres, penalizing any overlap beyond an allowed penetration of 2 mm.

## Evaluation Protocol

Accuracy is quantified on 50 manually annotated frames: the authors mark the five fingertip locations in the point cloud consolidated from all cameras of the multi-camera rig, deliberately selecting frames from regions of the v2 sequences where v2's hand pose accuracy was poorest, and report mean and standard deviation of fingertip error for v2 versus v3. Contact quality is assessed with contact maps averaged over each dataset version, counting a hand surface point as in contact when its distance to the object surface is below 4 mm (a tolerance that accounts for depth errors and camera calibration), and reporting the mean penetration distance between hand and object per sequence, separately for dynamic multi-camera grasps and static single-camera grasps.

## Findings and Analysis

On the 50 manually annotated frames, fingertip error drops from 12.31 +/- 3.8 mm (v2) to 8.12 +/- 2.2 mm (v3), the 4 mm improvement highlighted in the abstract. Contact maps show that v3 annotations produce more hand-object contact than v2, with the largest improvement on dynamic grasps, where mean penetration rises only marginally from 1.0 +/- 0.9 mm to 1.3 +/- 1.0 mm while contact coverage increases substantially; static grasps keep an identical mean penetration of 1.8 mm in both versions. Per-sequence penetration statistics (for example, dynamic sequences ABF1 through AP1 and static sequences SM1 through SMu4) confirm an overall improvement in grasp quality with penetration values remaining in the low-millimeter range, indicating that the added contact realism does not come at the cost of implausible interpenetration.

## Contributions

- HO-3D v3, a publicly released re-annotation of the HO-3D dataset with more accurate 3D hand and object poses and larger train/test splits (83,325/20,137 images).
- Three concrete, transferable improvements to the HOnnotate annotation pipeline: cross-entropy silhouette matching with MSeg segmentations, direct anatomically constrained joint-angle optimization, and sphere-based hand-object repulsion.
- A quantitative v2-versus-v3 accuracy comparison against manual annotations, plus contact-map evidence that v3 better captures true hand-object contact.

## Limitations

The accuracy evaluation rests on only 50 manually annotated frames, all deliberately sampled from regions where v2 was weakest, so the reported 4 mm gain is an estimate targeted at the hardest cases rather than a dataset-wide average. Mean penetration increases slightly for dynamic grasps (1.0 mm to 1.3 mm) and for a few individual sequences (for example, MC2 from 0.8 mm to 2.0 mm), so contact realism and interpenetration are traded off rather than jointly optimized. The report also does not re-benchmark downstream hand pose estimation methods on v3, leaving the practical effect of the improved annotations on leaderboard results unquantified.

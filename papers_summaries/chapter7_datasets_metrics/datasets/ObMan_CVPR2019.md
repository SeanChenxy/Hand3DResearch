# Learning Joint Reconstruction of Hands and Manipulated Objects (ObMan)

**Authors:** Yana Hasson, Gül Varol, Dimitrios Tzionas, Igor Kalevatykh (with Michael J. Black, Ivan Laptev, Cordelia Schmid)
**Date:** 2019 (CVPR 2019)

## Summary
This paper addresses the absence of large-scale training data for joint 3D reconstruction of hands and manipulated objects: existing interaction datasets were too small for deep networks or provided only partial annotations. The authors build ObMan (Object Manipulation), a large-scale synthetic dataset of hands grasping everyday objects created by automatically generating grasps with GraspIt for 2,772 ShapeNet object meshes from 8 categories and rendering embodied SMPL+H hands in realistic scenes. They also propose the first end-to-end learnable model for joint hand-object mesh reconstruction from a single RGB image, using a differentiable MANO layer, an AtlasNet-based object branch, and a novel contact loss that penalizes interpenetration and encourages contact. ObMan-trained models transfer to real images (FHB, HIC, and qualitative CORe50), and pre-training on ObMan is critical in low-data regimes.

## Background and Motivation
Hand pose estimation had advanced mostly toward sparse keypoints, which are insufficient for reasoning about hand-object contact; full mesh reconstruction is needed to model interactions. Reconstructing hands and objects during manipulation is hard because of mutual occlusions, but manipulation also constrains the problem: hands and objects should be in contact yet not interpenetrate. Real images with ground-truth 3D shape for interacting hands and objects are difficult to obtain; existing datasets with hand-object interactions were too small for training deep networks (e.g., Tzionas et al.) or provided only partial annotations, and the then-recent FHB dataset covered only 4 objects. Existing synthetic hand datasets contained no object interactions, or used objects merely as occluders, motivating a scalable generation pipeline with real contact.

## Dataset Construction
ObMan is a fully synthetic dataset of hand-object manipulations rendered from automatic grasps.
- Objects: 2,772 meshes selected from 8 ShapeNet categories of everyday objects (bottles, bowls, cans, jars, knifes, cellphones, cameras, remote controls), split among training, validation, and test sets (1,947 object instances for training and 411 for test per the dataset table).
- Grasps: generated with the GraspIt software following the Columbia Grasp Database methodology; MANO is decomposed into 16 rigid parts for the simulator; grasps are optimized for a grasp metric, sorted by a heuristic, and the two best candidates per object are kept, yielding 21K grasps in total. The authors note these grasps do not necessarily reflect the statistical distribution of human grasps.
- Bodies and hands: hand poses are transferred to SMPL+H (MANO integrated into SMPL) so embodied full bodies provide natural occlusions; body poses/shapes are sampled from the SURREAL distribution (CMU MoCap poses, CAESAR shapes), with a global rotation uniformly sampled in SO(3); hand textures come from scans of 20 subjects (14 train / 3 test+validation), matched to face skin tone.
- Rendering: Blender; backgrounds sampled from LSUN and ImageNet; textures and lighting varied; camera distance to the hand root sampled uniformly between 50 and 80 cm; configurations discarded if fewer than 100 hand pixels or less than 40% of the object is visible.
- Scale and outputs: 141K training and 6K test frames (dataset table); for each configuration, object-only, hand-only, and hand-object images are rendered together with segmentation and depth maps, enabling systematic occlusion studies.

## Evaluation Protocol
- Task: from a single RGB image (rough crop of a left hand holding an object), predict the full hand mesh (MANO pose/shape, 21 keypoints including 5 fingertip vertices), the object mesh (AtlasNet deforming a 642-vertex icosphere), and object translation/scale relative to the hand; output is a joint hand-object reconstruction in a hand-relative coordinate frame.
- Metrics: hand error as mean end-point error (mm) over 21 joints; object error as symmetric Chamfer distance (mm) between ground-truth surface samples and predicted vertices; physical quality measured by penetration depth (mm), intersection volume (cm^3, 0.5 cm voxelization), and simulation displacement (mm) of the object's center of mass under gravity in physics simulation.
- Splits: train/test splits of ObMan (141K/6K frames), FHB (8,420/9,103 frames, 115/127 sequences, 4 objects, same subject split as the original), the contact subset FHBC (5,077/5,657 frames, 3 objects, excluding the genus-1 milk bottle), and HIC (251/307 frames, 2 train/2 test sequences with a sphere and a cube, frames restricted to hand-object distance below 5 mm, MANO fitted to provided meshes).
- Baselines/comparisons: occlusion ablations (train/test on hand-only, object-only, and hand-object images); contact-loss ablations over the repulsion weight lambda_R; synthetic-to-real transfer comparing random initialization versus ObMan pre-training with varying fractions of real FHB data; qualitative generalization on CORe50.

## Findings and Analysis
- Occlusion ablations on ObMan show training with occlusions is essential: a model trained on hand-object images scores 11.6 mm hand error when tested on occluded images versus 10.3 mm on unoccluded images for a hand-only-trained model; training on unoccluded images and testing on occluded ones is the worst setting, with hand and object reconstruction errors dropping by 12% and 25% when moving from unoccluded to occluded test images.
- Without physical constraints the model produces an average penetration depth of 9 mm on ObMan and 19 mm on FHBC; the object ground truth of FHB is itself noisy (average penetration depth of the hand skeleton in the object is 11.0 mm, std 8.9 mm), so the authors caution against drawing strong conclusions from object metric fluctuations there.
- The contact loss with equal attraction/repulsion weighting (lambda_R = 0.5) improves physical quality without hurting reconstruction: on FHBC it reduces maximum penetration from 18.7 mm to 12.1 mm and simulation displacement from 51.2 mm to 47.7 mm, while on ObMan repulsion alone (lambda_R = 1) cuts maximum penetration by 33% (9.5 to 6.4 mm) and on FHBC by 68% (18.7 to 6.0 mm) but at the cost of grasp stability in simulation.
- On FHBC the hand error is around 28 mm and the object Chamfer error around 1,565-1,604 mm, which the authors attribute to noisy object annotations.
- Synthetic-to-real transfer: pre-training on ObMan benefits FHB especially when fewer than 1,000 real images are available; on HIC (only 250 training images), pre-training on a synthetic variant matched to the target object/pose distribution outperforms real-only training on object error even before fine-tuning.
- The model runs at 20 fps on a Titan X GPU; qualitative results on CORe50 show generalization to unseen categories (e.g., lightbulb), with global pose and outline well recovered but fine details missed.

## Contributions
- The first end-to-end learnable model for joint 3D reconstruction of hands and manipulated objects from monocular RGB, combining a differentiable MANO layer with AtlasNet-based object reconstruction in hand-relative coordinates.
- A novel differentiable contact loss (attraction toward the object at 6 contact regions plus repulsion against interpenetration) that improves physical plausibility without degrading reconstruction accuracy.
- ObMan, a large-scale synthetic dataset of hand-object manipulations (141K train / 6K test frames, 2,772 object meshes from 8 categories, 21K automatic grasps) with object-only, hand-only, and hand-object renderings, released publicly with code and pre-trained models.

## Limitations
The generated grasps optimize a robotics grasp metric and, as the authors state, do not necessarily reflect the statistical distribution of human grasps, which limits realism. FHB object ground truth is noisy (11.0 mm average penetration), weakening conclusions on real-data object metrics. Object reconstruction is restricted to genus-0 topologies (the icosphere template), so objects such as the genus-1 milk bottle are excluded from contact experiments. Contact metrics rely on a 0.5 cm voxelization, and simulation displacement must be read jointly with penetration since stable simulated grasps can also arise from balancing collision forces. On real unoccluded data the model recovers global layout but misses fine details, as shown qualitatively on CORe50 where no 3D ground truth exists to quantify errors.

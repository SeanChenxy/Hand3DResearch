# FEEL (Force-Enhanced Egocentric Learning): A Dataset for Physical Action Understanding

## Summary
Egocentric video datasets capture the visual effects of manipulation but lack force — the causal signal that defines physical action — because force cannot be reliably hand-labeled from pixels. FEEL (Force-Enhanced Egocentric Learning) is the first large-scale dataset pairing force measurements from custom piezoresistive gloves with egocentric video (Meta Aria glasses): approximately 3 million force-synchronized frames over about 27 hours of natural, unscripted manipulation in kitchen environments, with about 45% of frames involving hand-object contact. The paper demonstrates the utility of force through two task families: contact understanding — jointly temporal contact segmentation and pixel-level segmentation of contacted objects, supervised entirely by force-derived labels — and action representation learning, where video-to-force prediction serves as a self-supervised pretraining objective for video backbones. The force-supervised contact model achieves state-of-the-art temporal contact detection across FEEL, EPIC-VISOR, and HOI4D validation sets without any manual contact annotation, and force-aware pretraining improves frozen-feature action recognition transfer on EPIC-Kitchens, Something-Something V2, EgoExo4D, and Meccano.

## Background and Motivation
The authors argue that force is a primitive, not a peripheral signal: vision measures motion and appearance (effects), while force captures the causes of interaction and exposes action structure in a low-dimensional learnable form. Existing contact-understanding approaches rely on labor-intensive manual annotation of contact events in images/video (100DOH, EPIC-VISOR) or controlled mocap/synthetic setups; real-world force data is scarce, with most vision-force work relying on simulation or small, simple-task robot collections. The closest prior dataset, OpenTouch (5 hours of egocentric video/audio/force/eye-tracking), is over 5x smaller than FEEL, and among real-world egocentric datasets FEEL is the only one providing both contact annotations and force measurements (Table 1 comparison against EgoHands, ARCTIC, VISOR, HOI4D, Ego4D-FHO, 100DOH, Ehsani et al., OpenTouch). Kitchens are chosen as the capture domain because they span a broad range of interaction and action types.

## Dataset Construction
- **Hardware:** A custom force glove with six piezoresistive sensors (Flexiforce A301, Tekscan; up to 45 N each) — one per fingertip plus a palm strip — wired to an Arduino Nano with a 3.3V LiPo battery and toggle switch, in a low-profile form factor. Egocentric capture uses Meta Project Aria glasses (Gen 1): forward RGB at 1440x1440, two 640x480 monochrome scene cameras, a 7-microphone spatial audio array, and dual IMUs at 800 Hz and 1000 Hz. A three-step calibration procedure (Supplementary Materials) synchronizes the two independent clock domains per session.
- **Scale and composition:** Approximately 3 million force-synchronized frames over about 27 hours of dense, unscripted kitchen manipulation; about 45% of frames (1.35M) involve hand-object contact, with 1.65M non-contact frames. From these, 500K images carry binary contact pseudolabels and 180K contain binary segmentations of the objects in contact.
- **Subjects and objects:** Natural unscripted manipulation by wearers of the glove-and-glasses rig in kitchen environments; the paper does not report the number of subjects. Interacted object nouns are weakly annotated per 2-minute window (8 uniformly sampled frames, set of nouns without temporal ordering).
- **Processing and annotations:** Meta Machine Perception Services (MPS) provides 1 kHz 6-DoF camera trajectories from visual-inertial odometry and per-frame 3D hand keypoints from the side cameras. Metric scaled depth maps are predicted with MLDepthPro and dense optical flow with SEA-RAFT to support contacted-object pseudolabeling. Contact states are derived from the force stream via a multi-stage pipeline (Hampel outlier removal, Gaussian smoothing, rolling-percentile baseline subtraction, per-sensor normalization by the 99.5th percentile, geometric-mean fusion across the six sensors, dual thresholds with an excluded ambiguous band). Contacted-object pseudo-labels are generated by combining SAM3 concept-prompted mask proposals with masked optical flow: the proposal with maximum mean Sampson epipolar error (violating the static-scene constraint) is accepted if a minimum number of its pixels lie within a 3D distance threshold of the hand centroid.

## Evaluation Protocol
Two task families are evaluated. (1) *Contact understanding:* a DPT head on frozen DINOv3 features takes a single RGB image and outputs, per hand (left/right), a binary contact logit and a two-channel contacted-object segmentation map; heads are supervised only when the corresponding hand is visible. Evaluation covers temporal contact detection (BC = binary contact irrespective of hand side; BSC = binary signed contact including hand side) and spatial segmentation (IOU) on the validation sets of FEEL, EPIC-VISOR, and HOI4D (the latter two zero-shot), against a zero-shot depth-based baseline, fully supervised models (HOIRef, EPIC-VISOR, 100DOH, and 100DOH+SAM for segmentation), SAM3 with concept prompting, and a MOVES pseudolabel reimplementation. (2) *Action representation learning:* video backbones are pretrained/fine-tuned on FEEL to predict per-hand forces from clips (clips with a hand visible for at least 50% of frames), then transferred to action recognition; due to compute constraints, weights initialized from Kinetics-710 (Hiera-B) and Ego4D (EgoVideo-B) are further fine-tuned on FEEL force data rather than trained from scratch, and compared against the same standard-pretrained weights in both frozen and unfrozen (end-to-end) settings. No test/train split details beyond validation-set evaluation are reported in the main text.

## Findings and Analysis
- Temporal contact detection: the force-supervised model reaches BC/BSC of 95.6/88.9 on FEEL validation, 87.3/79.7 on EPIC-VISOR, and 82.2/81.3 on HOI4D. It surpasses all fully supervised baselines on FEEL and VISOR (e.g., 100DOH at 49.0/40.0 on FEEL and 81.3/71.9 on VISOR), while 100DOH retains the top BC score on HOI4D (90.0 versus 82.2). The model's smaller BC-to-BSC drop indicates more reliable handedness discrimination than 100DOH, which the authors attribute to annotation noise in manually labeled hand side.
- Contact segmentation (IOU): the weakly supervised model scores 0.625 on FEEL (versus 0.218 for 100DOH+SAM and 0.309 for the MOVES reimplementation) and 0.751 on HOI4D, narrowly trailing 100DOH+SAM on HOI4D (0.765) and VISOR (0.399 versus 0.443), despite being deployed zero-shot on those datasets while baselines are trained on EPIC-VISOR.
- Action recognition (top-1): force-aware pretraining helps most in the frozen setting — Hiera-B improves on EPIC-Kitchens from 41.65 to 46.50 (verb) and 15.12 to 21.38 (noun), on SSV2 from 36.08 to 39.04, on EgoExo4D from 13.11 to 20.83, and on Meccano from 26.42 to 29.57; EgoVideo-B improves from 59.24 to 62.00 (verb), 29.42 to 30.11 (noun), 49.61 to 54.34 (SSV2), 26.00 to 27.75 (EgoExo4D), and 36.59 to 38.23 (Meccano). Under unfrozen fine-tuning gains shrink and are mixed (e.g., EgoVideo-B noun on EPIC-Kitchens drops 55.67 to 55.60 and EgoExo4D drops 40.92 to 40.75), while the largest relative gains appear on the smallest downstream datasets (Meccano, EgoExo4D), supporting the claim that physically grounded representations reduce labeled-data requirements.

## Contributions
1. Release of FEEL, claimed as the largest egocentric force-video dataset (~3M frames, ~27 hours), pairing force with video, audio, 3D hand pose, and camera trajectory of natural unscripted kitchen manipulation.
2. Scalable force-derived contact supervision: 1.35M contact frames and 1.65M non-contact frames, plus 500K binary contact pseudolabels and 180K contacted-object segmentation pseudolabels requiring no manual segmentation annotation.
3. A self-supervised force-pretraining pipeline showing that force-aware video backbones transfer better to action recognition, especially with frozen features and small downstream datasets.
4. A new state-of-the-art contact estimation model that jointly performs temporal contact segmentation and pixel-level object-in-contact segmentation from still images, trained only on force-derived labels.

## Limitations
The paper's stated Limitations cover both sides. Hardware: the rubber gel pad sensor mounts are uncomfortable for extended wear; individual piezoresistive sensors are sometimes unreliable due to mechanical drift and bending; and the fingertip sensors obstruct fine dexterous tasks — the authors suggest above-site sensing such as wrist-mounted EMG as a remedy. Algorithmically, the segmentation pseudolabel pipeline depends on object motion, so stationary contacted objects such as tables or heavy appliances cannot be segmented. Additional structural limits evident from the paper: force sensing is restricted to six fingertip/palm contact points (no distributed tactile or joint torque coverage), capture is confined to kitchen environments with an unreported number of subjects, and evaluation of contact models uses the dataset's own force-derived pseudolabels as ground truth on FEEL, so absolute accuracy on FEEL is bounded by the quality of the force-derived labels themselves.

# Gaze-guided Hand-Object Interaction Synthesis: Dataset and Method

**Authors:** Jie Tian, Ran Ji, Lingxiao Yang, Suting Ni
**Date:** 2026 (IEEE TMM; arXiv v1 2024)
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2403.16169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16169)
**Zotero item:** `2LPFZ4UU` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/2LPFZ4UU))

## Summary
Gaze reveals when, where, and how a person intends to interact with objects, yet existing datasets capture gaze, hand pose, and object pose only in pairs at best. This paper introduces the task of gaze-guided hand-object interaction synthesis — generating a coordinated hand-object motion sequence from gaze input and an initial scene state — and presents GazeHOI, the first dataset with simultaneous 3D modeling of gaze, hands, and objects across complex tasks such as chess arrangement and furniture assembly. The paper proposes GHO-Diffusion, a stacked diffusion model with HOI-Manifold Guidance and gaze-consistency-based motion selection, and establishes benchmarks for both the synthesis task and a companion hand-object motion reconstruction task. On the synthesis benchmark GHO-Diffusion outperforms adapted baselines, reaching MPJPE of 144.8 mm versus 161.2-182.2 mm for the baselines.

## Background and Motivation
Gaze is a key behavioral signal for inferring attention and intention, but prior gaze datasets omit either hand pose (MoGaze, ADT) or object pose (GIMO, ActionSense, HoloAssist, EgoBody), and most treat gaze as auxiliary input for motion prediction from historical sequences. Existing hand-object interaction datasets (HO-3D, DexYCB, H2O, OakInk, HOI4D, ARCTIC, TACO, OakInk2) neglect gaze entirely and mostly cover simple interactions. The authors argue that coordinated brain-hand-object cooperation is central to realistic interaction modeling, and that gaze can serve as a compact intent signal to drive synthesis without textual annotations or motion references, with applications in VR/AR interaction and assistive technologies for users with disabilities.

## Dataset Construction
GazeHOI was recorded on a 1.6 m x 1.2 m capture platform with four synchronized systems: twelve hardware-synchronized ZCAM E2 cameras (3840x2160 RGB at 30 fps) for multi-view hand capture; eight Optitrack Prime 13W infrared cameras at 120 fps tracking at least eight 3 mm reflective markers per rigid object; two EinScan Pro 2X / SE scanners for object geometry; and an egocentric Pupil Core eye tracker paired with a RealSense D455 recording gaze and RGBD. Cross-system registration is achieved via triangulated markers on the ego camera and shared reflective markers. Processing: 2D hand keypoints via MediaPipe triangulated across views and fitted with MANO (pose and shape parameters); object 6DoF poses via Optitrack marker tracking converted with ICP into the scanned object frames; gaze post-processed by filtering short fixations and smoothing. Scale: 237K annotated frames forming 1378 subsequences with an average duration of 19.1 seconds and 2-4 objects per sequence, collected from 10 subjects (5 male, 5 female) with 33 rigid objects ranging from a 7 cm x 2 cm chess piece to assemblable furniture; the paper's comparison table counts 13 camera views and 3.2M images in total. Task-level descriptions cover repositioning objects, selecting targets in cluttered scenes, organizing disordered items, and assembling furniture. Project page: https://takiee.github.io/gaze-hoi/.

## Evaluation Protocol
Task 1 (gaze-guided synthesis): given object geometry, initial object and hand poses, and the gaze sequence, generate the subsequent object and hand motion. The 1378 sequences are split into 1103 training and 275 test sequences, with the test set containing only objects unseen in training to prevent object-specific overfitting (215 unseen-object sequences plus 60 seen-object complex-task sequences). Metrics cover accuracy (hand MPJPE, object MPVPE, Final Object Location error), interaction quality (Contact Frame ratio, Penetration Depth), generation quality (FID, Diversity against ground-truth value 4.227), and a 5-point user study on gaze-motion consistency (GMC) and interaction naturalness (IN). Baselines are adapted state-of-the-art systems: Text2HOI* (text encoder replaced by the gaze encoding), MDM* (conditioned on the gaze encoding plus auxiliary losses), and OMOMO* (hand synthesis conditioned on the generated object motion). Task 2 (motion reconstruction): from a 12-side-view video and object mesh, reconstruct per-frame hand-object motion; 3.0M images are split by view into 8 training / 1 validation / 1 test views, with metrics MRRPE, local MPJPE, and Success Rate (percentage of object vertices with L2 error under 5% of object diameter, following ARCTIC); baselines are ArcticNet (SF and LSTM variants) and Keypoint Transformer.

## Findings and Analysis
On the synthesis test set, GHO-Diffusion achieves MPVPE 117.4 mm, FOL 146.5 mm, MPJPE 144.8 mm, CF 68.10%, PD 2.46, FID 0.044, Diversity 4.217, and user scores of GMC 4.75 and IN 4.60, versus MDM* (MPJPE 166.6 mm, GMC 3.70), Text2HOI* (MPJPE 182.2 mm, FID 0.183), and OMOMO* (MPJPE 161.2 mm, GMC 4.15). On unseen objects the method retains its lead (MPJPE 144.3 mm versus 163.3-183.1 mm for baselines), and on seen complex tasks MPJPE is 146.6 mm versus 157.2-179.0 mm. Ablations show gaze points outperform gaze rays (MPVPE 117.9 versus 126.7 mm), that removing temporal or spatial encoding degrades first-stage results (FOL rises to 163.4 and 193.8 mm respectively), that HOI-Manifold Guidance improves contact ratio (CF 65.77 versus 64.65 without guidance) while naive guidance distorts naturalness (IN drops to 3.5), and that combining local contact-map and global DTW trajectory consistency in motion selection yields the best overall numbers (MPVPE 117.4, FOL 146.5). For reconstruction, on the test view Keypoint Transformer reaches 27.6 mm MPJPE and 44.9% success rate, versus 22.8 mm MPJPE and 33.0% success for ArcticNet-LSTM, establishing initial baselines on the new dataset.

## Contributions
- GazeHOI, the first dataset with synchronized 3D gaze, hand, and object annotations over complex multi-object tasks, with a four-rig capture platform and automated cross-calibrated annotation pipeline.
- Definition of the gaze-guided HOI synthesis task with unseen-object evaluation splits and a companion motion reconstruction benchmark.
- GHO-Diffusion, a stacked gaze-conditioned diffusion model with spatial-temporal gaze encoding, HOI-Manifold Guidance (kinematic, contact, penetration), and consistency-score-based motion selection.
- Extensive quantitative and user-study evidence that gaze conditioning improves intention alignment and physical plausibility relative to adapted baselines.

## Limitations
GazeHOI is restricted to rigid objects and excludes articulated objects, which the authors identify as a key future extension. The gaze signal is sparse and noisy, requiring post-processing, and the synthesis method targets a bounded table-top capture volume; the paper also notes intentions to add further modalities such as text and electromyography. Reported interaction-quality numbers (CF around 68%, PD 2.46) indicate that perfect physical plausibility is not yet attained.

# Open-AoE: An Open Egocentric Manipulation Dataset and Toolchain for Embodied Learning

## Summary
Embodied foundation models are constrained by the lack of open data infrastructure that can continuously collect, systematically process, and directly support model training: specialized-headset corpora narrow accessibility, while passive video lacks the hand motion, camera trajectory, and action boundaries required for robot learning. Open-AoE releases approximately 2,000 hours of egocentric human manipulation video captured in natural environments by 500+ contributors using 400+ consumer smartphone models, spanning 400+ scenes and 8,000+ tasks, together with English action text, MANO hand poses, 6-DoF camera trajectories, and temporally localized atomic actions. Rather than only benchmarking models, the paper evaluates the data itself: it audits visual diversity, semantic/temporal supervision, annotation consistency, and training-sample yield against OpenEgo, EgoDex, and EgoXtreme on ~100-hour matched scopes. Open-AoE ranks first in all six CLIP-based diversity metrics, attains the highest image-annotation consistency (4.583/5 under Idefics2), and retains 97.8% of the theoretical history-future training-window ceiling.

## Background and Motivation
Existing egocentric resources (Ego4D, EPIC-KITCHENS-100, EgoDex, EgoLive, OpenEgo, EgoScale) advance scale, hand-pose annotation, or robot alignment individually, but the paper argues the central gap is fragmentation between releasing videos and releasing training-ready data. High-quality geometric datasets depend on head-mounted/AR hardware, while cheap passive video lacks the structure needed for manipulation learning. Open-AoE builds on the AoE smartphone capture framework (CVPR 2026 Workshops) and opens the full loop: smartphone capture app, cloud processing pipeline, and a downstream toolchain for visualization, 4D hand-object reconstruction, cross-embodiment retargeting, and training interfaces for VLA policies, World Action Models (WAMs), and World Models.

## Dataset Construction
The dataset is produced by a four-stage pipeline. (1) Edge-side online detection: on-device models auto-start/stop recording on hand visibility, enforce operating/wearing compliance via voice feedback, adapt to lighting, and upload only valid clips. (2) Offline quality check and scene labeling: rule-based detectors discard corrupted, hand-less, non-egocentric, and jittery videos; faces and metadata PII are erased (reversible under authorization); videos are sliced into Parts with lineage records; the VLM Qwen3.7-Plus performs semantic compliance/valid-action checks and builds a closed-set four-level label tree (domain, scene, task, objects). (3) Reconstruction and annotation: DROID-W SLAM recovers 6-DoF camera trajectories robust to handheld shake; a two-hand detector plus HaWoR recovers metric-scale MANO meshes in world coordinates, refined by joint bundle adjustment with dynamic-object masks; 21-joint keypoints are extracted and videos are segmented into atomic actions with English labels and human-in-the-loop review. (4) Three-gate quality inspection (reconstruction valid-frame completeness, low IK failure rate when retargeting to a 28-DoF joint space, smooth camera trajectories) plus sampled human inspection yields the ~2,000-hour release. In a random 100-hour audit: 32,407 distinct action descriptions, 175 action verbs, 8,030 object strings, 135 scene labels; scenes include kitchens (24.8%), tabletop/indoor (23.3%), offices/desks (17.8%), bedrooms (9.1%); the median contributor supplies 2.6 minutes and the ten largest supply 13.7% of sampled duration; the horizontal FOV distribution is multimodal (concentrated around 65-75 and 90-95 degrees).

## Evaluation Protocol
The evaluation is a cross-dataset data audit rather than a robot benchmark. Open-AoE, OpenEgo, and EgoDex are scoped to ~100 hours each (EgoXtreme uses its full smaller release), and four dimensions are measured: (a) CLIP embedding diversity via 3,000 balanced trials of 2,000 embeddings per dataset, reporting Effective Rank, Participation Ratio, Meaningful Coverage (K=50 codebook, tau=5), Normalized Cluster Entropy, Effective Clusters, and kNN Domain Mixing (k=20); (b) semantic breadth and temporal completeness via unique action-field entries, temporal coverage, mean segment duration, and segments per minute; (c) image-annotation consistency scored 1-5 per frame by the Idefics2 vision-language evaluator, averaged sequence-macro with bootstrap CIs; (d) training utility via candidate history-future windows (2 s history, 2 s future, 2 s stride) against a 1,800 windows/hour ceiling, plus hand-signal and five-modality availability. No policy-training experiments on physical robots are reported; downstream integrations (VITRA, H-RDT, GR00T N1.7, SmolVLA, LeRobot recipes, DreamZero, Ctrl-World, iVideoGPT, GenieRedux, AdaWorld, LAOM) are described as interfaces and recipes.

## Findings and Analysis
Open-AoE attains the highest mean in all six diversity metrics: Effective Rank 97.43, Participation Ratio 40.47, Meaningful Coverage 19.89, Normalized Cluster Entropy 0.712, Effective Clusters 16.29, and kNN Domain Mixing 0.0775; it ranks first in Effective Rank, Participation Ratio, and kNN Domain Mixing in all 3,000 trials. Its action field has 32,407 unique natural-language descriptions (versus 26,864 native labels in OpenEgo and 111 categorical classes in EgoDex) with 99.99% temporal coverage versus 50.1% for OpenEgo, at 13.97 segments per minute with 9.64 s mean segment duration. Idefics2 sequence-macro consistency is 4.583/5 (95% CI [4.557, 4.608]) versus OpenEgo 3.029, EgoDex 2.916, and EgoXtreme 2.015; 85.4% of Open-AoE sequences score at least 4 and 2.2% score at most 2. It yields 1,760 candidate history-future windows per hour (97.8% of the ceiling, versus 98.9% OpenEgo, 68.1% EgoDex, 94.5% EgoXtreme), with 98.93% of entries having at least one valid hand signal and 98.02% both hands (OpenEgo: 66.2% and 55.6%), and it is the only release with all five supervision modalities (actions, bounding boxes, confidence, hand pose, camera pose) at 100% sequence-level availability; bounding boxes are 100% valid, 98.39% fully in-frame, mean confidence 0.947.

## Contributions
- An approximately 2,000-hour open egocentric manipulation corpus from 500+ contributors and 400+ consumer smartphone models, with MANO hand poses, camera trajectories, atomic action segments, and English text.
- An end-to-end open processing pipeline (on-device gating, privacy erasure, VLM scene labeling, SLAM-aligned hand reconstruction, three-gate QC) that turns raw smartphone video into structured training samples.
- A downstream toolchain (AoE-Visualization, AoE-Reconstruct-Retarget with EgoInfinity/Do-as-I-Do reconstruction, G1/Galbot/Sharpa/XHand retargeting and robotized-video synthesis, AoE-Training-Ready with 110D dense MANO, 48D wrist-fingertip, 62D Sharpa, 20D GR00T, and 20-26D ego action interfaces) serving VLA, WAM, and World Model training.
- A reproducible data-profile audit protocol comparing diversity, annotation consistency, and training-window yield across egocentric datasets.

## Limitations
All distribution statistics are computed from a single random 100-hour sample, and the paper explicitly states that the downstream benefit of camera-domain diversity remains a training hypothesis requiring controlled ablations. Cross-dataset comparisons are confounded by differing native annotation schemas, so vocabulary size and consistency scores audit supervision breadth rather than accuracy under a shared ontology. No physical-robot policy training or retargeting success rates are reported in the paper, so training utility is assessed only through yield and modality-availability proxies. Hand and camera annotations are algorithmically reconstructed (HaWoR, DROID-W) rather than ground-truth captured, and the release is a ~2,000-hour curated subset of a larger pool filtered by the three quality gates, so the released distribution differs from raw collection.

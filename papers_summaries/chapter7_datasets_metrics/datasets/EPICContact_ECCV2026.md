## Summary
Joint 3D hand-object pose estimation from egocentric RGB remains brittle in the wild because of severe mutual occlusions, ambiguous contact, and the scarcity of 3D supervision outside controlled lab settings. The paper makes two contributions: EPIC-Contact, an in-the-wild egocentric dataset of 2.3K video clips (62.3K frames) built on EPIC-Grasps clips, annotated with dense bijective 3D hand-object contact correspondences that are converted into posed hand-object meshes via a contact-based optimization pipeline (EC-fit); and HOPformer (Hand-Object Pose transformer), an end-to-end transformer that jointly predicts bi-manual hand and object pose in a single forward pass by conditioning object features on strong pre-trained hand priors through a cross-attention decoder. On the in-lab ARCTIC egocentric benchmark, HOPformer reaches 82.4% success rate (SR@0.05), 6.2 points above the current SOTA; on EPIC-Contact it nearly doubles the success rate while reducing contact deviation by 75% relative to prior learning baselines, demonstrating that hand-prior conditioning is the key to in-the-wild generalization.

## Background and Motivation
Training or evaluating hand-object pose estimation on in-the-wild interactions requires images paired with 3D ground truth for both hands and objects. To date this ground truth has come from expensive motion capture (e.g., ARCTIC), which needs careful calibration, marker hardware, and minimalist uncluttered backgrounds that do not reflect real egocentric scenes. MOW is the only prior in-the-wild dataset with paired hand-object meshes, but its object poses are only coarsely verified and fine-grained contact is ignored. Body-pose research has shown that in-the-wild contact annotation is feasible (DECO's vertex painting; PICO's contact transfer to objects), motivating a hand-object analogue. Meanwhile, hand-only pose estimation has advanced rapidly (HaMeR, WiLoR) while joint hand-object methods (ArcticNet-SF, JointTransformer) regress the two poses independently and fail to model their interaction, hurting performance under occlusion. The authors' intuition is to reuse robust data-driven 3D hand reconstruction as a prior to guide joint estimation.

## Dataset Construction
- **Source:** EPIC-Contact annotates 2,272 clips (62.3K frames) taken from the EPIC-Grasps dataset, which contains challenging egocentric hand-object interactions paired with 3D meshes for 9 object categories and manually labelled stable-grasp temporal segments. Clips cover 9 categories with per-category clip counts: Bottle (286), Bowl (396), Can (97), Cup (121), Glass (211), Mug (160), Pan (492), Plate (437), Saucepan (72). Scenes are diverse, cluttered, and in-the-wild, with objects that may be small, transparent, or heavily occluded.
- **Hand contact annotation:** Annotators "paint" contact labels on a uniformly subdivided MANO template mesh (3,106 vertices instead of the standard 778) while watching the clip, which provides multiple views of a consistent grasp; they label all contact regions, including those occluded in the egocentric view, annotating one hand at a time for bimanual clips. Inter-annotator agreement is Fleiss' Kappa 0.61 over 10 videos by 12 annotators (0.65 in DECO for bodies).
- **Bijective contact transfer:** Following ContactEdit, each hand contact region is parametrized by a 2-DoF contact axis; the hand is divided into three regions (thumb, four fingers, palm) with finger spacing estimated from WiLoR poses, so at most three axes — six clicks — yield full hand-object bijective correspondences on the object surface via an interactive web tool. Object template meshes are scaled to the image instance using a VLM (Gemini 2.5) prompted for multiple degrees-of-scale (e.g., pan diameter and handle length); on 30 ground-truth-measured objects this achieves 0.94 cm MAE (5.9% relative error), with 82.5% of samples within ±10% of true dimensions. Object-contact agreement is Kappa 0.62 over 10 videos by 4 annotators.
- **EC-fit and clip-level poses:** EC-fit optimizes posed MANO hands and object meshes from the contact constraints: object pose initialized from WiLoR on the central frame plus multiple random and category-aware pose priors; contact loss on bijective vertex pairs, an occlusion-aware mask loss that excludes hand-object and inter-object occlusions, penetration loss, hand mask loss, and a regularizer toward the initial pose; results are manually verified and corrected. The relative object-to-hand pose from the stable-grasp central frame is automatically broadcast across the clip using per-frame hand poses, with temporal jitter flagged by lower confidence. The posed hand-object mesh is shared across all frames of a clip.

## Evaluation Protocol
The task is: given an egocentric RGB image containing one object from a known category set, manipulated by one or both hands (or resting), estimate each visible hand's MANO pose/shape and the object's 6D pose (6D rotation representation, translation under a weak-perspective camera, plus 1D articulation where applicable). HOPformer extracts object tokens with DINOv2 ViT-G and hand tokens with WiLoR, passes object queries through an L=12 transformer decoder that self-attends and cross-attends to hand memory, aggregates via a learnable module, and routes disentangled tokens to MLP heads (right-hand pose/root/shape, left-hand pose/root/shape, object pose) plus an object-classification head used to retrieve the mesh from a pool at inference; main tables use oracle meshes to match prior work, with fully automatic results in the supplementary. Training uses frame-level hand losses (2D/3D joints, MANO pose/shape, camera), object losses (3D/2D keypoints, camera, classification, pose with geodesic rotation), and a CDev-based interaction loss on contacting frames. Splits: ARCTIC follows its original train/val splits (8 exocentric views for pre-training, 1 egocentric view for fine-tuning and evaluation); EPIC-Contact uses 2,035 videos for training and 237 for testing with identical hand-side and object-category distributions. Metrics follow ARCTIC: Contact Deviation (CDev, mm), Mean Relative-Root Position Error (MRRPE, hand-hand and hand-object, mm), Motion Deviation (MDev, mm), Acceleration Error (ACC, m/s^2), hand MPJPE (mm), Average Articulation Error (AAE, degrees), Success Rate SR@0.05/SR@0.1 (% of object vertices within 5%/10% of object diameter), and object classification accuracy; for EPIC-Contact these are made symmetry-aware. Baselines are ArcticNet-SF and JointTransformer (the SOTA), retrained with identical splits and protocol.

## Findings and Analysis
- ARCTIC (egocentric, in-lab): HOPformer achieves CDev 31.9 mm, MRRPErl/ro 31.1/29.4 mm, MDev 7.3 mm, ACC 4.8/6.2 m/s^2, MPJPE 16.1 mm, AAE 5.0 degrees, SR@0.05 82.4%, and classification 99.5%, versus JointTransformer's 35.0/34.0-29.9/10.4/6.9-10.1/20.0/4.9/76.2 and ArcticNet-SF's 44.1/33.9-36.8/11.8/6.3-11.3/22.9/8.0/59.0 — a +6.2-point SR gain over SOTA with comparable articulation error (5.0 versus 4.9).
- EPIC-Contact (egocentric, in-the-wild): HOPformer reaches CDev 20.7 mm, MRRPEro 65.8 mm, MDev 11.4 mm, ACC 2.5/4.1, MPJPE 19.9 mm, SR@0.05 29.8%, SR@0.1 69.7%, Cls 52.9%, versus JointTransformer (30.1/78.6/20.0/3.1-9.5/22.9/17.6/56.9) and ArcticNet-SF (94.2/166.7/70.1/3.9-5.3/44.8/16.5/55.9). Relative to ArcticNet-SF this nearly doubles SR@0.05 (16.5 to 29.8) while cutting CDev by about 75% (94.2 to 20.7 mm).
- The benchmark is substantially harder than in-lab data: JointTransformer drops from 76.2% SR@0.05 on ARCTIC (Ego) to 17.6% on EPIC-Contact, which the authors attribute to novel object instances, transparency, occlusion, and clutter (ARCTIC reuses the same object instances between train and validation).
- Ablations (ARCTIC egocentric): removing object losses collapses SR@0.05 from 82.4% to 66.8%; removing the CDev-based interaction loss raises CDev from 31.9 to 40.7 mm and MDev from 7.3 to 10.8 mm; replacing cross-attention with Concat+MLP collapses SR to 29.4% and CDev to 74.4 mm despite identical backbones, confirming the gains come from hand-conditioned feature refinement rather than the backbone; using generic DINOv2 tokens instead of WiLoR hand features degrades MPJPE to 20.6 mm and SR to 70.0%; disabling self-attention, the aggregation module, or decoder depth (L=1: SR 21.9%; L=6: 58.6%; L=9: 69.0%) each substantially hurt.

## Contributions
1. EPIC-Contact, the first in-the-wild egocentric dataset with dense bijective 3D hand-object contact correspondences and posed hand-object meshes (2,272 clips, 62.3K frames, 9 object categories), enabling training and evaluation on challenging real-world images.
2. A scalable contact-based annotation pipeline: subdivided-MANO vertex painting, 2-DoF contact-axis transfer with at most six clicks per clip, VLM-based multi-degree-of-scale object scaling, and the EC-fit optimization that converts contact into posed meshes with quality checks, plus clip-level pose propagation with released confidence scores.
3. HOPformer, a hand-prior-conditioned transformer achieving state-of-the-art on ARCTIC (82.4% SR@0.05 egocentric, +6.2 points) and EPIC-Contact, with ablations isolating cross-attention conditioning and interaction supervision as the sources of the gains.

## Limitations
The authors' supplementary limitations section states: the annotation pipeline, while robust, is still time consuming (around 3-4 minutes per annotated centre frame), and better contact initializations from trained methods could speed it up; ground truth is propagated from a single verified frame to the whole clip, and propagation can be noisy due to WiLoR hand-pose errors (hand-side errors or camera placement jumps) — mitigated by released per-frame confidence scores so users can filter low-confidence frames, and the paper notes its reported metrics are relative/root-aligned and hence unaffected. On the method side, HOPformer currently estimates poses for only a handful of object categories (9 for EPIC-Contact), and while articulated objects are explored on ARCTIC, object articulation in the wild is not explored and is left to future work.

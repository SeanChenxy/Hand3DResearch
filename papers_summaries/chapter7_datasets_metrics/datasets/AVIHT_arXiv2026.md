# AVI-HT: Adaptive Vision-IMU Fusion for 3D Hand Tracking

## Summary
Vision-only egocentric 3D hand tracking degrades severely under the heavy self-occlusion that arises during contact-rich hand-object interaction, while 6-DoF IMU sensing is occlusion-invariant but lacks a global spatial reference. This paper introduces AVI-HT, an adaptive visual-IMU fusion tracker, and, to evaluate it in realistic conditions, the DexGloveHOI dataset, which pairs egocentric video with time-synchronized on-glove IMU signals and marker-based 3D hand-pose ground truth across dexterous manipulation tasks. Evaluation covers two hand representations (UMETrack and MANO) with single- and multi-modal baselines. AVI-HT reduces mean keypoint error by 16.1% and its wrist-aligned variant by 24.2% over the baselines, with the largest gains on occluded fingers and fine-grained manipulation tasks.

## Background and Motivation
Grasping hides the hand from the egocentric camera precisely when manipulation is most dexterous, and the set of kinematically plausible poses that explain visible evidence becomes prohibitively large. Prior work splits into vision-only methods (which collapse under occlusion) and IMU-based trackers: dense 9-DoF systems suffer magnetometer interference indoors, while compact 6-DoF IMUs lack absolute orientation and drift. Visual-sensor fusion remains underexplored for fine-grained hand tracking mainly because of the absence of datasets providing synchronized egocentric vision, per-finger IMU signals, and accurate 3D ground truth during object interaction. The paper addresses both the method and the evaluation-data gaps.

## Dataset Construction
The dataset presented for evaluation is DexGloveHOI (the name used throughout the paper), built with a capture rig combining a Meta Quest headset (monochrome egocentric camera, 512x640 at 60 Hz), a data-sensing glove with 12 6-DoF IMU sensors (3-axis accelerometer plus 3-axis gyroscope, 200 Hz; one sensor on each proximal and distal phalanx of the four fingers, one on the thumb, one on the hand back), and a marker-based optical MoCap system with 12 infrared cameras providing ground-truth 3D hand pose. Marker positions are solved to UMETrack poses and converted to MANO via a Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer, so every frame carries ground truth in both hand representations. Sessions: 4 user sessions (4 participants), each about 45-55 minutes of usable capture, with 17 distinct dexterous manipulation tasks per session spanning complementary activity categories (e.g., cutting, scissors, screwdriver). Final scale: 3.5 hours of quality-controlled multi-modal data, 100K+ egocentric frames, 2.4M IMU samples, and 720 GB of synchronized recordings; each image is paired with per-image 6-DoF IMU signals and MANO and UMETrack ground-truth poses. Vision and IMU streams are synchronized by aligning each 60 Hz frame to the nearest IMU timestamp, with a 14-sample IMU temporal window per frame.

## Evaluation Protocol
The task is model-based 3D hand pose tracking from a monochrome egocentric image plus per-finger IMU windows, instantiated in two variants: AVI-HT-UME (real-time AR/VR tracking, ResNet encoder, 22 joint angles plus wrist SE(3)) and AVI-HT-MANO (offline reconstruction, ViT-Huge encoder, MANO parameters). Under the UMETrack representation, metrics are mean keypoint position error (MKPE), fingertip-only MKPE (F.MKPE), their hand-root-GT-oriented (Transformed) variants, and PUK AUC (P.A and P.A.T); baselines are UMETrack (vision-only), UMETrack + EKF (post-hoc Extended Kalman Filter fusion), and an IMU-only tracker. Under the MANO representation, metrics are PA-MPJPE, PA-MPVPE, and F-scores at 5 mm and 15 mm thresholds, against HaMeR as the vision-only baseline. Ablations report per-finger IMU contribution and per-activity-type breakdowns via the MKPE.T gap (AVI-HT minus UMETrack), plus sensitivity studies over additive IMU noise and vision-IMU temporal misalignment. Train/test splits for DexGloveHOI are not reported in the paper.

## Findings and Analysis
On DexGloveHOI with the UMETrack model, AVI-HT achieves MKPE 10.359 mm and F.MKPE 13.253 mm, versus 12.342 mm and 17.478 mm for vision-only UMETrack (16.1% and 24.2% improvement), and outperforms UMETrack + EKF (MKPE 15.406 mm), showing learned cross-sensor attention beats hand-crafted filter fusion. The IMU-only tracker is competitive on transformed metrics (MKPE.T 7.151 mm vs. AVI-HT's 7.021 mm) but cannot estimate global wrist position, underscoring modality complementarity. Under MANO, AVI-HT reaches PA-MPJPE 10.519 mm and PA-MPVPE 9.265 mm versus HaMeR's 13.754 mm and 12.736 mm (23.5% and 27.3% improvement), raising F@5 from 0.516 to 0.628 and F@15 from 0.882 to 0.936. Per-finger ablations show each IMU sensor contributes most to its own finger's tracking, with smaller cross-finger benefits attributed to kinematic coupling; the largest gains occur in occlusion-heavy, fine-grained activities such as cutting, scissors, and screwdriver use. Sensitivity studies show graceful degradation up to moderate IMU noise (MKPE from about 10.335 mm to 12.344 mm at 2x native noise floor) and a V-shaped error curve under temporal misalignment of -0.4 to +0.4 seconds, centered at zero shift.

## Contributions
- DexGloveHOI, a multi-modal HOI evaluation dataset pairing egocentric video, per-finger 6-DoF IMU streams, and dual-representation (UMETrack and MANO) marker-based 3D ground truth over 17 manipulation tasks.
- AVI-HT, an adaptive vision-IMU fusion framework with hierarchical cross-sensor attention and a kinematic prior mask, general across vision backbones and hand models.
- State-of-the-art tracking results under heavy occlusion on both hand representations, plus per-finger, per-activity, noise, and time-alignment analyses with practical deployment insights.

## Limitations
The data-sensing glove alters the visual appearance of the hand, creating a potential domain gap for downstream pipelines that expect bare-hand input (the authors suggest hand-avatar rendering as mitigation). All experiments use one specific glove, so generalization to other gloves with different sensor layouts, form factors, or IMU specifications remains unvalidated. Capture is seated with a constrained workspace, and train/test splits for the dataset are not reported in the paper.

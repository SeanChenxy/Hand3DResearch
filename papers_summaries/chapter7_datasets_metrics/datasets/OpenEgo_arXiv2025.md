# OpenEgo: A Large-Scale Multimodal Egocentric Dataset for Dexterous Manipulation

**Authors:** Ahad Jawaid, Yu Xiang
**Date:** 2025 (arXiv)

## Summary
Egocentric human video offers scalable demonstrations for imitation learning, but existing corpora lack either fine-grained, temporally localized action descriptions or dexterous hand annotations, and no single source combines scale, task diversity, and standardized hand poses. OpenEgo consolidates six public egocentric datasets (CaptainCook4D, HOI4D, HoloAssist, EgoDex, HOT3D, HO-Cap) into a unified resource of 1107 hours and 119.6M frames spanning 290 manipulation tasks in 600+ environments, with hand poses converted to a common MANO 21-joint camera-frame layout and new timestamped, intention-aligned action primitives. To validate utility, the authors train language-conditioned imitation-learning policies to predict future 3D hand trajectories, showing smooth error scaling across prediction horizons. The dataset targets training of world models, hierarchical VLAs, and vision-language-action foundation models.

## Background and Motivation
Internet video differs from robot deployment in viewpoint, hand-object proximity, and motion patterns, creating a distribution gap that weakens transfer for dexterous control, whereas egocentric video keeps hands and manipulated objects in frame from the actor's perspective. Recent trends push in two directions: imitation learning from egocentric demonstrations increasingly supervises dexterous skills with 3D hand trajectories (EgoDex, EgoMimic, Humanoid Policy, MotionTracks), and hierarchical VLA systems such as Hi Robot and pi0.5 rely on fine-grained action primitives predicted by high-level modules. Existing egocentric corpora fall short in one dimension or another — Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS lack dexterous labels, HoloAssist covers only 20 tasks, EgoDex has no aligned language primitives, HOI4D is small, and HOT3D and HO-Cap are lab-confined. OpenEgo is motivated by the absence of a single large-scale dataset unifying hand joints and intention-aligned language annotations.

## Dataset Construction
OpenEgo aggregates six public sources: CaptainCook4D (54 h, 5.6M frames, 24 tasks, 200 recordings), HOI4D (44 h, 2.4M frames, 16 tasks, 4k recordings), HoloAssist (166 h, 17.9M frames, 20 tasks, 2.2k recordings), EgoDex (829 h, 90M frames, 194 tasks, 338k recordings), HOT3D (13.3 h, 3.7M frames, 33 tasks, 19 recordings), and HO-Cap (0.67 h, 73k frames, 3 tasks, 64 recordings), totaling 1107 hours, 119.6M frames, 290 tasks, and 344.5k recordings. Coverage spans 10 kitchens and 610 indoor rooms, 24 cooking recipes, 1.4k distinct objects, and at least 258 unique participants, with both short- and long-form demonstrations. Hand poses are unified to the MANO 21-joint layout (four joints per finger plus wrist) expressed in the camera frame, with per-source pipelines: CaptainCook4D has no native hand pose, so 2D landmarks (MediaPipe) are back-projected through RGB-D depth; HOI4D uses released MANO parameters with landmark fallback for missing frames; HoloAssist, HOT3D, and HO-Cap poses are transformed from the world frame using per-frame extrinsics; EgoDex's 25-joint format is mapped to MANO-21 by dropping four non-MANO joints and reindexing. All sources carry a binary per-joint visibility mask that retains missing joints. Language annotations consist of descriptive action primitives with absolute timestamps (t_start, t_end) from intention onset to completion, actor labels (left_hand, right_hand, both_hands; person for navigation), object names or attribute-based descriptions, gaps between observed actions, and high-level task labels; primitives are added even to sources with existing fine-grained annotations where descriptions are incomplete. All resources are to be released at www.openegocentric.com under the source datasets' licenses (e.g., Apache 2.0, CC BY-NC 4.0, CDLA v2, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, CC BY-SA 4.0, CC BY 4.0).

## Evaluation Protocol
The benchmark task is language-conditioned 3D hand-trajectory prediction. State at time t is stacked left/right hand joints (42 joints, R^42x3) in the camera frame with a binary visibility mask; given an RGB observation, a language prompt describing the intended manipulation, and current joints, the policy predicts the next T actions. The policy is a ViLT-style model trained with a masked mean-squared error objective that ignores invisible joints. Due to computational limits, training uses a 0.1% subset of OpenEgo with 10% of demonstrations held out for evaluation (no leakage); training runs 15,000 AdamW steps with cosine annealing at batch size 896 (about 13.44M sampled instances) on 2x RTX 4090 GPUs. Metrics on the held-out split, computed only over visible joints, are AED (average Euclidean distance over the horizon), FED (final-step Euclidean distance), and DTW (dynamic time-warping distance), reported at horizons of 8, 16, 32, and 64 frames at 15 fps. No external baselines are compared; the paper establishes a reference protocol for the unified dataset.

## Findings and Analysis
On the held-out split, AED grows from 0.0491 at the 8-frame (0.5 s) horizon to 0.0714 at 16 frames, 0.0893 at 32 frames, and 0.1045 at 64 frames (4.0 s); FED follows the same trend (0.0633 to 0.1076). DTW rises more steeply, from 0.3918 at 8 frames to 1.1284, 2.7150, and 6.7975 at 64 frames, because timing offsets accumulate over longer horizons and increase alignment cost. The authors interpret this as evidence that the dataset provides a structured learning signal whose difficulty scales smoothly with the prediction horizon, enabling effective learning of short-horizon dexterous motion. They explicitly caution that the 0.1%-subset training means results should not be read as upper bounds.

## Contributions
- OpenEgo, the largest consolidated egocentric manipulation dataset with both standardized 21-joint dexterous hand annotations (unified MANO layout in the camera frame with visibility masks) and intention-aligned, timestamped language primitives, covering 1107 hours, 119.6M frames, 290 tasks, and 344.5k recordings across six public sources.
- Per-source conversion pipelines (2D-landmark depth back-projection, world-to-camera rigid transforms, joint-layout remapping) that make heterogeneous hand annotations directly comparable.
- An evaluation protocol and reference results for language-conditioned dexterous 3D hand-trajectory prediction on the unified data.

## Limitations
Hand joints are missing in some frames due to occlusion or absent source labels, which visibility masks only flag rather than fix. Language annotations are automatically generated and only partially verified, with possible temporal drift on long or ambiguous actions. For sources lacking native dexterous labels, 3D joint quality depends on the landmark estimator and depth sensing. Experiments train on only 0.1% of the data with a single architecture, so reported accuracies are not upper bounds; licensing constraints (e.g., EgoDex's CC BY-NC-ND) also require users to obtain underlying data from original sources.

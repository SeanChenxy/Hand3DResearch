# First-Person Hand Action Benchmark with RGB-D Videos and 3D Hand Pose Annotations

**Authors:** Guillermo Garcia-Hernando, Shanxin Yuan, Seungryul Baek, Tae-Kyun Kim  
**Date:** 2018 (CVPR 2018)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:1704.02463](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.02463); DOI `10.1109/CVPR.2018.00050`  
**Zotero item:** `8NWKU8IN` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/8NWKU8IN))  
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.  

## Summary

This paper introduces the first benchmark combining egocentric dynamic hand-action recognition with accurate 3D hand pose annotations: 1,175 RGB-D action videos (105,459 frames) covering 45 daily hand-action categories with 26 objects, performed by 6 actors in kitchen, office, and social scenarios. Hand poses (21 joints) are captured automatically via 6 magnetic sensors and inverse kinematics, and 6D object poses with 3D mesh models are provided for 4 objects spanning 10 actions. Evaluating 18 baselines shows hand pose is the most discriminative modality for first-person action recognition, and the benchmark exposes how severely occluded egocentric hands challenge pose estimators.

## Background and Motivation

Prior first-person action recognition modeled hands with silhouettes or discrete grasp types, while body-pose action recognition showed viewpoint-invariant pose features help; the paper asks whether fine-grained 3D hand pose similarly benefits hand actions. A key obstacle was the absence of reliable off-the-shelf hand pose estimators and of real annotated data with objects: existing pose datasets were synthetic, static, or object-free, and action datasets lacked pose labels. The dataset deliberately spans diverse hand configurations and temporal dynamics, and its occlusion statistics (on average only 10 of 21 joints visible per action) make it a stress test for egocentric pose estimation.

## Dataset Construction

An Intel RealSense SR300 is shoulder-mounted, recording 1920 x 1080 color and 640 x 480 depth at 30 fps. Six 2-mm magnetic sensors (five fingertips, one wrist) feed inverse kinematics over a 21-joint hand model; a seventh sensor on each tracked object yields 6D object pose. Six right-handed participants receive only safety instructions, keeping natural style and speed variability. Actions follow a taxonomy of 45 categories (kitchen 25, office 12, social 8) tied to 26 objects, with 34 distinct grasp types identified; there are on average 26.11 sequences per action class and 45.19 per object, including bimanual and hand-only actions (handshake, high five). Six-dimensional object poses and meshes cover milk bottle, salt, juice carton, and liquid soap across 10 action classes.

## Evaluation Protocol

Action recognition is evaluated on pre-segmented videos classified into 45 categories. Four training/testing protocols are compared: 1:3, 1:1 (600 train / 575 test sequences), 3:1, and 6-fold leave-one-person-out cross-validation. The 18 methods cover RGB (two-stream), depth descriptors (HOG2, HON4D, novel-view), pose-based (LSTM, Moving Pose, Lie Group, HBRNN, Gram Matrix, Transition Forests), and a hybrid multimodal approach (JOULE). Poses are normalized for hand size with the wrist as origin. A second evaluation swaps ground-truth test poses for estimates from a state-of-the-art ConvNet pose estimator trained under different protocols (with/without object interactions, cross-subject, cross-object) to measure the pose-accuracy to action-accuracy relationship. A supplementary experiment adds 6D object pose as a feature on the 261-sequence subset.

## Findings and Analysis

Gram Matrix reaches the best action accuracy at 85.39%, followed by Lie Group (82.69%) and the 2-layer LSTM (80.14%), while depth-only methods trail (HON4D 70.61%) and RGB two-stream achieves 75.30%; cross-person testing is hardest (LSTM 62.06% versus 84.82% at 3:1). Modality ablation with JOULE ranks hand pose (74.60%) above depth (60.17%) and color (66.78%), with all combined at 78.78%. Combining hand and object pose lifts accuracy to 91.97% versus 87.45% (hand only) and 74.45% (object only). The index finger is the most informative, and thumb plus index together boost accuracy. For pose estimation, training with hand-object images is crucial: error drops from 31.03 mm (object-free training) to 14.34 mm, raising downstream action accuracy from 29.63% to 72.06% versus 78.73% with ground truth; the model generalizes to unseen subjects (11.25 mm) but not unseen objects (19.84 mm). Per-finger errors are lowest for thumb (12.45 mm) and index (15.48 mm) due to less egocentric occlusion.

## Contributions

The first real egocentric benchmark pairing dynamic hand-action labels with accurate 3D hand pose, plus a 6D object-pose subset; an 18-method evaluation establishing hand pose as the most informative recognition cue and quantifying complementarity with RGB, depth, and object pose; and an analysis linking occlusion, pose-estimation error, and action-recognition degradation.

## Limitations

The dataset has only 6 right-handed actors, all interactions are pre-segmented clips rather than untrimmed streams, and object pose and mesh supervision covers just 4 objects and 10 action classes. Pose annotations come from magnetic sensors whose tape is visible in color images, and Gram Matrix-style methods degrade sharply (to 32.22%) when fed estimated rather than ground-truth poses, revealing sensitivity to pose noise.

# HandEdit: A Unified Benchmark for Egocentric Human-to-Robot Dexterous Hand Image Editing

**Authors:** Zhenjie Yang, Xingyu Jiao, Guopeng Zhong, Shuzhe Yang (equal contribution: Yang, Jiao; Fudan University, Inspire Robots, Shanghai Jiao Tong University, HKU, NTU, Shanghai AI Laboratory)  
**Date:** 2026 (arXiv)

## Summary
Dexterous robot learning is bottlenecked by costly embodiment-aware teleoperation data, while egocentric human videos are abundant but separated from robots by an embodiment gap that generic image editors cannot close without embodiment-specific priors. The paper frames human-to-robot dexterous hand conversion as an image-editing problem and introduces HandEdit, a large-scale embodiment-aware editing dataset of over 200M image-level editing instances derived from 300K+ clips of five public hand-object datasets, covering 26 target URDF embodiments (13 hand-only, 13 hand-arm), 600+ scenes, 1.1K+ objects, and 400+ tasks. A curation pipeline segments and inpaints away the human hand/arm, retargets MANO or 3D hand poses to each URDF, renders and composites the robot embodiment, and harmonizes the result into pseudo-ground-truth targets. The benchmark defines Hand-only and Hand-Arm tracks with a metric suite spanning generic similarity, GPT-4o-based VLM judgment, and embodiment-aware metrics, and evaluates 11 commercial and open-source editors, showing that hand removal is easy while target-embodiment fidelity and interaction preservation remain the unsolved core difficulty.

## Background and Motivation
Robot teleoperation data are expensive and platform-bound, UMI-style collection still needs dedicated equipment, and egocentric human videos cannot be used directly for robot policy learning because human hands and dexterous robot hands differ in appearance, geometry, articulation, kinematics, and interaction patterns; the gap widens for hand-arm systems where the wrist and arm must match the target morphology and camera viewpoint. Existing robotizing pipelines (H2R, Phantom, Masquerade, MimicDreamer, Mitty, and others) are tied to parallel grippers, fixed platforms, or embodiment-specific settings, and no prior editing benchmark jointly supports egocentric input, dexterous robot hands, URDF-conditioned editing, and multi-embodiment evaluation (per the comparison table spanning EditBench, InstructPix2Pix, MagicBrush, OmniEdit, Emu Edit, UltraEdit, SEED-Data-Edit, HQ-Edit, AnyEdit, VE-Bench, MotionEdit, and EgoEdit). HandEdit targets this intersection: edited pairs can train human-to-robot editing models that convert human video into robot-centric observations for policy pretraining.

## Dataset Construction
Sources are the egocentric portions of five public datasets: EgoDex (90M frames, 338K clips, 1080p tabletop, 500 household objects, 194 tasks), ARCTIC (2.1M frames, 339 clips, 2800x2000 mocap studio, 11 articulated objects), OakInk2 (4.01M frames, 627 clips, 75 objects, 150 tasks), HOI4D (2.4M frames, 4K clips, 1280x800 rooms, 800 object instances in 16 categories, 54 tasks), and HO-Cap (656K frames, 64 clips, 64 daily objects). The curation pipeline proceeds in stages, each with automatic checks and human screening: SAM3 segments the human hand or hand-arm region; ProPainter inpaints the occluded background; MANO or 3D hand poses are retargeted to robot joint states via hybrid coarse alignment followed by position-based refinement under embodiment-specific kinematic constraints; for hand-arm embodiments, a camera-relative virtual base is searched per sequence (initialized from shoulder keypoints or the wrist-trajectory center, varying horizontal translation and yaw, scored by IK feasibility, joint limits, collision checks, and trajectory quality, with a human operator selecting among the top three valid solutions and sequences excluded when none is plausible); the URDF is rendered under the matched egocentric camera and composited into the recovered scene; a lightweight Harmonizer module (about 20 MB, self-supervised on 10,000 natural egocentric hand images) fixes color, illumination, and boundary mismatches without altering joint configurations or interaction geometry. The target set spans 26 URDFs: 13 hand-only embodiments (Ability, Allegro, DexHand 021, Inspire RH56DFX and RH5DG2, Leap, OrcaHand, Revo2, Schunk SVH, Shadow Hand, Sharpa, Wuji, RoHand) and 13 hand-arm platforms (Jaka, KUKA, Panda, RM 65/75, UR5, xArm), with standardized materials and colors added to public URDFs for consistent rendering.

## Evaluation Protocol
Two tracks are defined: Hand-only (replace the human hand with a dexterous robot hand while preserving the interaction) and Hand-Arm (additionally replace the forearm or full arm, requiring larger-region background preservation and plausible arm kinematics). Each track contains 1K images paired with 13 corresponding target embodiments; harmonized pipeline outputs serve as pseudo-ground-truth references, and editing is URDF-conditioned (source image, text instruction, target URDF render, edited image). Eleven editors are benchmarked under their native conditioning interfaces with fixed prompt templates: GPT-Image-2, GPT-Image-1.5, Nano-Banana, Nano-Banana-2, Seedream-4.5, Flux-2-Pro, Flux-Kontext-Max, HunyuanImage-3.0, Qwen-Image-Edit-2511, OmniGen2, and FireRed-Image-Edit-1.1. Metrics have three families: generic similarity (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, FID computed on the full image, background, and edited ROI); GPT-4o VLM judgment decomposed into Semantic Consistency and Perceptual Quality, combined as the square root of their product after normalizing both to [0,1]; and embodiment-aware metrics covering human-hand removal (penalizing skin-like pixels in the edited ROI), target-robot fidelity (DINOv2 structural similarity to pseudo-GT plus an identity term that mixes maximum CLIP similarity against a rendered URDF reference-view bank with CIELAB color consistency, weighted 0.5/0.5), and interaction consistency (1 minus LPIPS on the dilated object-contact region). A blinded human study with five annotators, five-point scales, Krippendorff's alpha, and ICC(2,k) validates that the automatic metrics track human judgment.

## Findings and Analysis
On the Hand-only track, GPT-Image-2 is the strongest baseline overall: LPIPS(ROI) 0.482 and FID(ROI) 99.71 (both best), PSNR(Full) 19.85, removal 0.953, structural fidelity 0.780, ID fidelity 0.852, and interaction 0.703 (the latter two also best). GPT-Image-1.5 obtains the highest VLM score (0.765) yet scores only 0.730 structural fidelity and 0.435 interaction, and FireRed-Image-Edit-1.1 attains the top Perceptual Quality (0.754) with near-bottom Semantic Consistency (0.228), showing that visual plausibility alone does not imply embodiment-correct editing. Nearly all models score high on hand removal (0.904-0.955), whereas structural fidelity, ID fidelity, and interaction preservation separate models sharply, identifying embodiment-aware generation and contact preservation as the core challenge. The Hand-Arm track is harder: GPT-Image-2 reaches PSNR(Full) 15.07, removal 0.983, structural fidelity 0.778, interaction 0.595, and VLM 0.786 (GPT-Image-1.5 highest VLM at 0.856), while ID fidelity collapses across all models to 0.417-0.606 versus 0.753-0.915 on Hand-only, and interaction scores fall (best 0.634 for Nano-Banana-2). Against these, the pseudo-GT references themselves score removal 0.964/0.963, structural fidelity 1.000, ID fidelity 0.964/0.962, and interaction 0.821/0.788 on the two tracks, but their Perceptual Quality is only 0.492/0.482, quantifying the appearance gap of the pseudo-supervision.

## Contributions
- The first large-scale embodiment-aware image-editing dataset for egocentric human-to-robot dexterous manipulation transfer: 200M+ editing instances from 300K+ clips, five source datasets, 600+ scenes, 1.1K+ objects, 400+ tasks, and 26 URDF embodiments.
- A curation pipeline producing kinematically grounded pseudo-ground-truth targets (segmentation, inpainting, embodiment-specific retargeting, virtual-base search with IK and collision checks, rendering, compositing, harmonization) with stage-wise automatic and human screening.
- The first unified benchmark for URDF-conditioned dexterous hand editing, with Hand-only and Hand-Arm tracks and a three-family metric suite (generic similarity, VLM judgment, embodiment-aware metrics) validated by blinded human evaluation.
- A systematic evaluation of 11 commercial and open-source editors, exposing failure modes (residual human-hand artifacts, embodiment mismatch, interaction failure, viewpoint errors, background inconsistency) and providing paired data for training future embodiment-aware editing models.

## Limitations
The paper's appendix states that exact pixel-aligned human-robot image pairs cannot be physically captured, so HandEdit uses edited pseudo-references as low-cost supervision; these retain a gap from real-robot observations in fine-grained appearance and contact dynamics. HandEdit is positioned as scalable data for pretraining and mid-training that reduces, but does not replace, real-robot demonstrations, and the paper does not report downstream robot policy training results. Additional constraints evident from the protocol: the benchmark is image-level rather than video-level, the test sets are limited to 1K images per track against 13 embodiments each, the pseudo-GT targets are produced by the same inpainting/retargeting/compositing stack being superseded (with a pseudo-GT Perceptual Quality of only about 0.49), and evaluation of editors relies on harmonized composites as reference, so similarity metrics partially measure closeness to the pipeline's own artifacts.

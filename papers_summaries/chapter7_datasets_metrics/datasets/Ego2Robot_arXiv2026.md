# Ego2Robot: Scalable Robot Data Synthesis from Egocentric Human Data

## Summary
Robot policy pretraining is limited by the cost of collecting demonstrations, while egocentric human videos are abundant but separated from robots by an embodiment gap; prior retarget-and-render approaches had only shown per-task value at small scale. Ego2Robot is a scalable pipeline that converts egocentric manipulation videos into robot-format training data through action retargeting, robot-arm visual synthesis (arm segmentation, hand removal, robot base-pose search with inverse kinematics, depth-aware compositing), and three-level quality curation. Processing about 1,940 hours of egocentric video from four sources, it produces 18,561 hours of synthesized robot training data across 15 robot morphologies, the largest ego-to-robot dataset to date (about 9.6x amplification). To measure benefit, the paper extends RoboTwin2.0 with disentangled perturbation axes (visual appearance, scene layout, embodiment morphology, task semantics) and shows that pretraining on Ego2Robot-synthesized data mixed with robot data consistently improves out-of-distribution success, with gains validated by real-robot deployment.

## Background and Motivation
Large-scale robot corpora (Open X-Embodiment, DROID, AgiBot World) remain expensive and constrained in interaction diversity, whereas human hand interactions can be collected cheaply at scale across diverse objects and environments. Two research directions use human data: co-training on human video alongside robot data, and converting human demonstrations into robot format via retargeting and rendering (e.g., Phantom, EgoMimic). The latter had only been validated at limited scale, leaving open whether ego-to-robot synthesis can provide pretraining benefits for vision-language-action models. Existing generalization benchmarks (RoboTwin, LIBERO, CALVIN, RLBench, SIMPLER) bundle perturbations into single scores, so failures cannot be attributed to specific factors; decomposed benchmarks (Colosseum, LIBERO-Plus/PRO) are mostly single-arm. Ego2Robot contributes both the scaled data engine and a disentangled dual-arm, cross-embodiment evaluation protocol.

## Dataset Construction
Input sources are four egocentric corpora totaling roughly 1,940 hours: ANT (7 h, in-house pick-and-place with hand pose), EgoDex (732 h), ViTRA (249 h), and EgoVerse (954 h), plus a pure-video path (Path B) that estimates hand poses with WiLoR per-frame reconstruction and DynHaMR temporal optimization and segments long recordings into subtasks with Qwen3.5. Action alignment maps 21 hand keypoints to a parallel-gripper trajectory: a virtual fingertip blends index and middle tips (0.7/0.3), the tool center point is the thumb-virtual-fingertip midpoint, gripper width is their distance, and a handedness-corrected orthonormal grasp frame is built; trajectories are smoothed with Savitzky-Golay filtering and Gaussian-weighted SLERP. Because egocentric manipulation is faster than teleoperation, frames are subsampled per source (ANT and EgoDex to 60% of frame rate, EgoVerse to 45%, ViTRA to 25%). Visual alignment segments arms with SAM 3, removes them with ProPainter inpainting, then solves a keyframe IK-feasibility optimization in MuJoCo to place a virtual robot base, renders the robot from the original camera, and composites with depth ordering. The pipeline is applied independently to 15 morphologies (Panda, UR5e, ARX-L5, xArm7, Sawyer, Kinova Gen3, IIWA, Jaco, FR3, UR10e, ViperX, WidowX, Piper, YAM, Aloha-Agilex), generating parallel training streams. Three-level curation filters IK failures, self-collisions, action outliers, statistical discontinuities, and VLM-checked semantic inconsistency. Each sample contains a robot-composited frame, a camera-frame relative end-effector action, camera parameters, and a text instruction; camera-frame actions unify sources with unknown camera calibration.

## Evaluation Protocol
The evaluation extends RoboTwin2.0 with 11 independent perturbation settings plus camera-frame relative end-effector support, organized into four axes: visual appearance (background texture, lighting, robot hue), scene layout (table height plus/minus 4 cm, distractor objects, camera offset plus/minus 5 cm), embodiment (zero-shot transfer to UR5-WSG, ARX-X5, and Franka Panda with IK-aligned initial poses), and task semantics (50 tasks with unseen object instances and 505 paraphrased instructions); EBench (7 precision tabletop tasks in Isaac Sim with a higher-mounted camera) complements it. Models use a Qwen3.5-4B vision-language backbone with a diffusion transformer action head predicting 32-step camera-frame relative end-effector chunks. Pretraining compares robot-only data (DROID + AgiBot World + InternData, about 6,565 h) against mixes of Ego2Robot-synthesized and robot data at 1:3, 3:1, and 1:1, all trained for 200K steps on roughly 19.2M frames for fairness. Models are finetuned on RoboTwin's 50 clean tasks (50 demonstrations each) and evaluated with 50 episodes per task under each perturbation. Real-robot experiments use an ARX ACone platform on five long-horizon tasks with 20 teleoperated demonstrations each, additionally finetuning on pipeline-converted egocentric play video (about 7 minutes per scene).

## Findings and Analysis
Mixing Ego2Robot data improves out-of-distribution generalization: Ego2R+Robot (1:1) reaches 68.1% Clean (+5.9 over robot-only's 62.2) and 53.5% RoboTwin Randomized (+2.6 over 50.9), leading in five of seven aggregate columns; the 3:1 mix achieves the best EBench average (51.7%, +12.1 over 39.6). The per-perturbation breakdown at 1:1 shows visual gains (background 70.3%, lighting 65.8%, robot color 65.8%, i.e., +3.7 to +7.6), scene gains (camera offset 56.3%, +5.9), embodiment gains (ARX 51.2%, +7.1; UR5 25.0%, +4.8; Franka stays near 5.3% due to its large kinematic gap), and semantic gains (unseen objects 39.6%, +10.3; paraphrased language 68.5%, +5.4); UR5 peaks at 31.4% (+11.2) at the 3:1 ratio. Ablations with ego-only pretraining show the pipeline itself matters: raw ego co-training gives 28.1% on Randomized, single-morphology Ego2R gives 31.7% (+3.6), scaling to 15 morphologies reaches 33.5%, and adding raw ego data alongside 15-morphology Ego2R reaches 37.3%. On the real ARX ACone robot, Mix + Ego2R Play achieves the best success on all five tasks, with the largest gains over robot-only on Put Blocks (+14) and Insert Screw (+13).

## Contributions
- A complete ego-to-robot synthesis pipeline (action alignment, visual alignment, three-level quality curation) supporting 15 robot morphologies from both annotated and raw egocentric video.
- The largest ego-to-robot dataset to date: 18,561 hours of synthesized robot training data from about 1,940 hours of egocentric source video (about 9.6x amplification).
- A disentangled generalization benchmark extending RoboTwin2.0 with independent visual, scene, embodiment, and semantic perturbation axes plus EBench support.
- Evidence that joint pretraining on synthesized and real robot data improves out-of-distribution success across perturbation types and transfers to real-robot deployment, including with casually recorded egocentric play data.

## Limitations
The paper states that retargeting maps hand poses only to parallel-jaw grippers, discarding fine-grained finger articulation and limiting transferable skills; extending to dexterous hands is future work. Visual alignment relies on inpainting and depth-aware compositing, which can introduce artifacts under heavy occlusion or complex lighting. The evaluation is limited to the task scope of RoboTwin2.0. Additional implicit constraints follow from the design: synthetic data is limited to the 15 supported morphologies, Franka transfer remains weak (under 7% success), and the world-frame action space is avoided in favor of camera-frame relative actions, which ties supervision to the egocentric viewpoint.

# Keypoint Transformer: Solving Joint Identification in Challenging Hands and Object Interactions for Accurate 3D Pose Estimation (H2O-3D)

**Authors:** Shreyas Hampali, Sayan Deb Sarkar, Mahdi Rad, Vincent Lepetit
**Date:** 2022 (CVPR 2022)
**Identifier:** none printed on the PDF page 1; project website: [https://www.tugraz.at/index.php?id=57823](https://www.tugraz.at/index.php?id=57823)
**Zotero item:** `5ULFMXCB` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/5ULFMXCB))
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.

## Summary
This paper targets 3D pose estimation of two interacting hands, and of hands manipulating an object, from a single color image, where joint identification is the core difficulty because joints of two hands look alike and occlude each other. Instead of regressing one heatmap per joint (which conflates localization and recognition), the proposed Keypoint Transformer first detects unlabeled 2D keypoints, uses self-attention to associate each keypoint with a joint identity or background, and uses cross-attention with learned joint queries to predict 3D poses; it reaches state-of-the-art accuracy on InterHand2.6M with roughly half the parameters. The same paper introduces the H2O-3D dataset: over 75,000 multi-view RGB-D images (60,998 training and 15,342 test frames from 17 sequences) of two hands of different subjects manipulating YCB objects with functional intent, fully annotated in 3D by extending the HO-3D annotation method to two hands. On H2O-3D, their method achieves an MPJPE of 3.09 cm and object MSSD of 7.96 cm, serving as the first strong baseline for this benchmark.

## Background and Motivation
Two-hand pose estimation is significantly harder than single-hand estimation: appearance similarities between joints make identification extremely challenging, and close interaction produces self- and mutual occlusions. State-of-the-art bottom-up methods regress a heatmap per joint, so localization and recognition are solved simultaneously, and ambiguous heatmaps cause failures under similarity and occlusion; methods that detect left and right hands first and then predict poses independently perform poorly in close interaction. Recent fixes (joint segmentation, visibility, refinement layers) increase model complexity. The paper separates the two tasks and explicitly models keypoint relationships with attention. On the data side, existing datasets covered either two interacting hands (InterHand2.6M) or a single hand with an object (HO-3D); no dataset had two hands jointly manipulating an object with accurate 3D annotations, which motivates H2O-3D.

## Dataset Construction
H2O-3D consists of videos of two hands manipulating an object, automatically annotated with the 3D poses of both hands and the object.
- Source and sensor: captured with a 5 RGB-D camera multi-view setup; annotations are produced automatically by extending the optimization-based HO-3D annotation method of Hampali et al. to consider two hands, so no markers are used.
- Scale: 60,998 training images and 15,342 test images (more than 75,000 total) from 17 multi-view sequences.
- Subjects: the main paper states five different subjects; the supplementary material mentions six subjects.
- Objects: 10 objects from the YCB dataset; the test set contains 7 objects seen in training and 1 unseen object; per-object symmetry axes and angles are tabulated for evaluation (e.g., Mustard Bottle, Bleach Cleanser, Cracker Box: 180-degree Z symmetry; Bowl, Mug, Pitcher Base: continuous Z symmetry).
- Actions: each subject manipulates objects with both hands and a functional intent (e.g., using objects for their purpose rather than only holding them).
- Challenge and filtering: the dataset is described as significantly more challenging than previous hand interaction datasets due to many large occlusions between hands and objects; because the object is often severely occluded, images whose ground-truth object segmentation covers less than 2% of the cropped image are excluded from object pose training and evaluation (9,098 of the 15,342 test images remain for object pose).

## Evaluation Protocol
- Task: from a single RGB image (256x256 crop), predict the 3D poses of both hands and optionally the 3D pose of the manipulated object. The architecture detects up to 64 keypoints from a single-channel U-Net heatmap, encodes them with CNN features and positional encodings, associates keypoints with one of the 42 hand joints, the object, or background via self-attention, and predicts poses via cross-attention with learned joint queries; 20 object keypoints are sampled on the predicted object segmentation and 2 additional queries predict object rotation and translation relative to the right hand, trained with a symmetry-aware object corner loss.
- Pose representations: 3D joint locations, 2.5D joint locations, and MANO joint angles; the MANO representation additionally predicts hand shape and relative hand translation.
- Metrics: MPJPE (mm) for root-relative hand pose, MRRPE (mm) for translation between hands (InterHand2.6M, H2O-3D); on HO-3D, mean joint error after scale-translation alignment of the root joint and AUC metrics, and object pose error in the hand frame using the Maximum Symmetry-Aware Surface Distance (MSSD).
- Splits: official train/test splits of InterHand2.6M (1.36M train, 849K test), HO-3D (66K train, 11K test, objects seen in training only), and H2O-3D; comparisons with InterNet, Dong et al., and Fan et al. use each method's own reported split.
- Baselines: two Transformer baselines ('CNN+SA' with flattened low-resolution feature maps and 'CNN+SA+CA' close to DETR), InterNet, per-joint-heatmap methods with visibility/segmentation cues, and HO-3D methods including HandOccNet, METRO, I2L-MeshNet, Pose2Mesh, and ArtiBoost (supplementary).

## Findings and Analysis
- InterHand2.6M: the Keypoint Transformer achieves MPJPE of 12.78 mm overall (10.99 single hand, 14.34 two hands) and MRRPE of 29.63 mm, versus 14.22 mm and 32.57 mm for InterNet — about 10% higher accuracy — and it outperforms the two Transformer baselines by 16% and 12%; against per-joint-heatmap state-of-the-art methods it reports 11.30 mm MPJPE with the split of Dong et al. (vs 12.08) and 13.41 mm with the split of Fan et al. (vs 11.32 for Fan et al.'s model, which is more complex).
- Efficiency: with a ResNet-18 backbone (28M parameters, roughly half of InterNet's 48M), the method still outperforms InterNet (11.67 vs 12.63 mm single hand, 16.78 vs 17.36 mm two hands).
- HO-3D: mean joint error 2.57 cm and joint error AUC 0.54, versus 3.04 cm/0.49 for the HO-3D authors' method and 3.69/0.37 for Hasson et al.; object MSSD is 7.02 cm versus 11.99 cm. In the supplementary, on HO-3D V2 the method is close to HandOccNet on scale-translation aligned MPJPE (2.57 vs 2.40 cm) though HandOccNet is better under Procrustes alignment; on HO-3D V3 it reports 2.48 cm versus 2.34 cm for ArtiBoost, which uses additional training data.
- H2O-3D: MPJPE 3.09 cm and MRRPE 8.28 cm; the MRRPE is about 2.5 times worse than on InterHand2.6M, attributed to object occlusions between the hands; object pose reaches an MSSD of 7.96 cm (per-object values from 6.5 cm for Mug to 13.5 cm for Scissors).
- Ablations: the keypoint-joint association loss improves MPJPE by 10% (17.08 vs 18.91 mm) and MRRPE by 15% (33.14 vs 38.96 mm) on interacting-hand images; the 3D and 2.5D representations perform similarly (14.76 vs 14.73 mm MPJPE) while MANO joint angles are less accurate (18.01 mm); the method benefits from local keypoint features rather than whole-image features, is robust to noisy and missing keypoints (the attention assigns noise to background and selects nearby visible keypoints for occluded joints), and adding more cross-attention layers has little effect.

## Contributions
- The Keypoint Transformer architecture that decouples joint localization from joint identification, using keypoint detection, self-attention for keypoint-joint association, and cross-attention with learned joint queries for pose prediction, supporting three pose representations.
- State-of-the-art two-hand results on InterHand2.6M with roughly half the model parameters, and significantly improved hand and object pose results on HO-3D.
- An extension of the architecture to estimate the 3D pose of an object manipulated by one or two hands using keypoints sampled on a predicted object segmentation with a symmetry-aware loss.
- The H2O-3D dataset: more than 75,000 RGB-D frames from 17 multi-view sequences of two hands (5-6 subjects) manipulating 10 YCB objects with functional intent, automatically and fully annotated in 3D for both hands and the object, released publicly as a new challenging benchmark.

## Limitations
The authors state that the method does not model contacts and interpenetration between hands and objects, so some predicted poses are not physically plausible. During highly complex and severely occluded hand interactions the method fails to obtain reasonable hand poses. They suggest that incorporating temporal information and physical modeling could address these problems in the future. The H2O-3D annotations are produced automatically by an optimization pipeline (an extension of HO-3D's) rather than by direct measurement, and the benchmark's object pose evaluation must exclude the 6,244 test images where the object is barely visible under occlusion — both facts relevant when interpreting benchmark numbers. The main paper and supplementary also differ on subject count (five versus six), which the paper itself does not reconcile.

# HO-Cap: A Capture System and Dataset for 3D Reconstruction and Pose Tracking of Hand-Object Interaction

**Authors:** Jikai Wang, Qifan Zhang, Yu-Wei Chao, Bowen Wen (The University of Texas at Dallas; NVIDIA)  
**Date:** 2025 (NeurIPS 2025)  
**Identifier:** [arXiv:2406.06843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06843)  
**Zotero item:** `XQB2JAH4` ([Zotero](zotero://select/library/items/XQB2JAH4))  
**Evidence status:** Zotero metadata, abstract, and PDF extraction were verified.

## Summary
Obtaining ground-truth 3D shape and pose annotations for hand-object interaction videos currently requires either expensive motion capture with markers, costly 3D scanners, or labor-intensive manual labeling, and existing automated pipelines (e.g., HO-3D, H2O) do not scale to unseen objects or many interaction types. The paper introduces a capture system combining eight calibrated Intel RealSense D455 RGB-D cameras, one Azure Kinect, and a HoloLens 2 headset, together with a semi-automatic annotation pipeline built on pre-trained vision models (SAM2 for segmentation, MediaPipe for hand keypoints, BundleSDF for object reconstruction, FoundationPose for object tracking) with multi-view consistency checks and SDF-based pose refinement — no mocap markers, no 3D scanners, and no domain-specific training. Using this system the authors collected HO-Cap, a dataset of 64 videos with roughly 656K RGB-D frames from 9 subjects manipulating 64 purchasable objects across pick-and-place, bimanual handover, and affordance-driven usage tasks. Benchmarks cover hand pose estimation, novel and seen object detection, and unseen object pose estimation, with annotation error validated below 5 pixels for both hands and objects.

## Background and Motivation
Datasets for hands and objects have relied on three annotation regimes, each with drawbacks: mocap systems (FPHA, ContactPose, GRAB, ARCTIC, OakInk2) are costly, need calibration between mocap and image cameras, and marker hardware introduces artifacts; manual labeling (DexYCB, OakInk, HOI4D) does not scale to large frame counts; and fully automatic systems have limited scope — HO-3D is restricted to objects with known 3D models and cannot handle unseen objects, while H2O requires training an object tracker per captured object, hindering scaling. The semi-automatic HANDAL pipeline lacks dynamic human-object interactions and hand annotations. The authors target a markerless system that handles arbitrary rigid objects without pre-existing models, provides both third-person and egocentric views, supports uni- and bimanual interaction with 3D reconstruction of novel objects, and doubles as a source of human demonstrations (hand and object trajectories) for embodied AI and robot imitation learning — including multi-object rearrangement tasks they describe as novel for this purpose.

## Dataset Construction
- **Capture hardware:** Eight Intel RealSense D455 cameras plus one Microsoft Azure Kinect positioned above a table, calibrated with Vicalib into a common world frame near the table center; the higher-resolution Kinect is used primarily for 3D object reconstruction. Subjects wear a Microsoft HoloLens 2 for synchronized egocentric RGB-D video and head-pose tracking; HoloLens point clouds are fused with those from the RealSense cameras, and a refined HoloLens camera pose is estimated using the annotated object poses. Streams are recorded as synchronized ROS bags.
- **Scale and composition:** 64 videos, approximately 656K RGB-D frames, 9 subjects, 64 unique objects (all commercially available, each with a reconstructed textured 3D mesh). By handedness: 35 right-hand sequences (41,327 frames), 8 left-hand (8,373), 21 bimanual (23,244). By task: T1 pick-and-place 28 sequences (29,706 frames), T2 handover between hands 21 sequences (23,244), T3 affordance-driven object use 15 sequences (19,994).
- **Annotation pipeline:** Objects are reconstructed by manually rotating each object before the Kinect while SAM2 tracks masks from two hand-picked initial points, and BundleSDF (with LoFTR-based initialization and online pose-graph optimization) extracts a textured mesh. In interaction videos, object 6D poses are initialized by FoundationPose run per view, harmonized across the eight cameras with RANSAC-based multi-view consistency, and refined by SDF optimization on the fused multi-view segmented point cloud with a temporal smoothness loss. Hand poses use MANO with per-subject shape calibration: MediaPipe 2D landmarks are triangulated across view pairs, filtered via a projection-loss-based RANSAC scheme per joint, gap-filled by linear and cubic-spline interpolation, and MANO pose is fitted to the resulting 3D keypoints. A final joint hand-object SDF optimization over both MANO and object poses plus smoothness terms reduces mesh intersections. Annotations comprise per-frame MANO hand poses, 21-joint 2D/3D keypoints, object 6D poses in the world frame (transformable to camera frames via included intrinsics/extrinsics), object and hand segmentation masks, textured object meshes, and first-person HoloLens video.
- **Annotation quality:** On 800 randomly sampled images across the 8 RealSense views, manually labeled hand joints and object keypoints show mean 2D projection errors after joint refinement of 3.42 (±1.73) pixels for objects, 4.58 (±2.72) for the left hand, and 3.82 (±2.29) for the right hand, all under 5 pixels, with errors decreasing monotonically across refinement stages.

## Evaluation Protocol
Three benchmark tasks are defined. Hand pose estimation: 2D/3D positions of 21 joints, evaluated with PCK at thresholds 0.05-0.2 and MPJPE (mm), using ground-truth hand bounding boxes as input for externally trained models (A2J-Transformer, HaMeR). Object detection: MSCOCO-style AP metrics over all 64 objects, in a novel-object setting (CNOS with rendered CAD templates, GroundingDINO with object-name text prompts, zero-shot) and a seen-object setting (YOLO11 and RT-DETR trained on a train/val split covering all subjects, views, and objects, tested on sequences not shared with training). Novel object pose estimation: MegaPose and FoundationPose, given ground-truth 2D bounding boxes and the dataset's 3D models without per-instance training, evaluated by the AUC of ADD and ADD-S over all 64 objects. The paper does not report a fixed train/test split ratio beyond this train/val versus held-out test sequence organization.

## Findings and Analysis
- Hand pose: HaMeR clearly outperforms A2J-Transformer (MPJPE 28.9 mm versus 78.7 mm; PCK@0.1 79.2 versus 26.8), which the authors attribute to its ViT backbone and 2.7M-image training set. HaMeR's error is nonetheless much larger than on the datasets it was originally benchmarked on, indicating that HO-Cap's natural grasps and hand-object occlusions pose unique challenges.
- Object detection: zero-shot novel object detection is weak (CNOS AP 25.3, GroundingDINO AP 17.0, both suffering false positives), whereas models trained on HO-Cap perform strongly (RT-DETR AP 75.9, AP50 90.0; YOLO11 AP 71.4, AP50 85.9), with both excelling on medium and large objects; small objects remain hard (APS around 21).
- Novel object pose estimation: FoundationPose achieves 89.3% ADD and 95.7% ADD-S AUC versus MegaPose's 67.1%/83.0%, attributed to its transformer architecture and extensive synthetic training data.

## Contributions
1. A scalable, markerless capture system (eight RGB-D cameras plus HoloLens 2) and semi-automatic annotation method that produces 3D shapes and world-frame poses of hands and objects from multi-view RGB-D video without 3D scanners, mocap, or domain-specific training.
2. The HO-Cap dataset: 64 videos, ~656K RGB-D frames, 9 subjects, 64 objects, covering pick-and-place, bimanual handover, and affordance-driven use, with multi-view calibration, segmentation masks, and egocentric streams suited to imitation learning and embodied AI.
3. Baselines for hand pose estimation, object detection (novel and seen), and unseen object pose estimation that establish the dataset's difficulty and utility, plus validation that the annotation error is under 5 pixels.

## Limitations
The authors state three limitations of the annotation framework. First, BundleSDF fails to reconstruct certain object types well, notably textureless and metallic objects, so such objects could not be included in the dataset. Second, because hand pose estimation depends on MediaPipe detections, videos with frequent MediaPipe detection failures were excluded. Third, when small or cylindrical objects (e.g., spatulas or hammers) are held inside the hand, minimal color, depth, or geometric information is visible, causing cross-view inconsistencies and unreliable world-frame pose tracking; such videos were also excluded. The dataset additionally assumes rigid objects.
